<br>Preparing took quite a bit of time.

Or rather, it took that long for me to pull myself together.

“…Whew.”

How much time had passed?

Hoping to shake off my tension, I started talking to the mascot costume sitting with me.

“So, you see, right after I got off work…”

“….”

“And then I suddenly got on the elevator and ended up here. Isn't that absurd?”

“….”

“…This is seriously cruel, just cruel.”

“….”

Hearing me out, the mascot suit nodded repeatedly.

A gesture that seemed to say, *Yeah, I know, I know.*

Whether it really understood or not, I couldn't say, but anyway.

“…Are you all alone here?”

At my question, the mascot suit sat still for a moment before slowly nodding.

“…You must have it pretty rough, too.”

“….”

The mascot suit turned its gaze elsewhere.

“…People didn't usually talk to you like this to begin with, did they?”

“…!”

The mascot suit nodded vigorously.

Well, fair enough.

Even if I were the one dropped in a place like this with no idea what was going on, I’d probably just find it annoying if some inexplicable mascot costume tried acting friendly.

Unless, of course, you'd died and come back to life like this.

“…Phew.”

Thinking that, I turned my gaze away.

Alright.

I slowly opened the notebook.

[135. By the way, what kind of people usually get dragged here? I'm a college student. I go to K University.]<br>
[136. I was just working at a construction site until a minute ago. I need to go take care of my mother.]<br>
[137. I found No. 136 near the elevator. I moved them to the corner.]<br>
[138. I cleaned things up a bit, though I'm not sure how much of a difference it made.]<br>
[139. No. 138 must've cleaned up a little. It's pretty clean, huh?]

There were the records of countless people.

Just how many had died here?

And, if it all ended for me right now…

“….”

How many more would die coming after me?

I looked back.

The path I'd just crossed over.

I’d already died once right here.

As for me, once I died that was it, but…

“The other people must have died a lot before getting here, right?”

“….”

“And going forward, if I die, the people coming after me will die too, won't they?”

“….”

The mascot suit tilted its head.

Probably because the mascot suit had never been past this point.

“…I'll be going.”

Just like before, the mascot suit gave me a thumbs-up.

As if telling me to have a safe trip.

“…Treat the next people who come along well.”

Though whether it actually would or not was anyone's guess.

Seeing the mascot suit slowly nod, I turned my gaze away.

Time to go.

I looked at the door I'd come through and took a step toward it.

The moment I opened the door, a dark hallway and a floor soaked to the-bone with blood welcomed me.

Fighting down a sigh that tried to escape on its own, I looked ahead.

Well, whatever. Let's do my best.

Right as I was about to set my foot down,

the mascot suit tapped me on the back.

“…?”

When I turned my head, the mascot suit was holding out the sandwich and juice again.

Now that I thought about it, I hadn't eaten the sandwich or the juice after regressing.

“…Thanks for the food.”

Accepting the juice and sandwich, the mascot suit gave me another thumbs-up.

* * *

After finishing the job and drenched in sweat, I was back inside the NPC room.

I'm so exhausted I could die.

“….”

I lowered my gaze back to the notebook.

■■■■■■.

Now I knew what this meant.

And the same went for why everyone had no choice but to jot things down in a rush.

Stepping back outside the door, the familiar scenery welcomed me.

A scenery made familiar by dying once.

This time, it wouldn't be all that difficult.

Because I knew every single strategy.

I took long, confident strides.

I repeated the rhythm: move forward when the light was on, stop when it went dark.

I heard a rustling sound from behind.

I didn't pay attention.

I only stopped walking when things felt strange or when I had a bad feeling instinctively.

Because there was nothing else to fear.

The elevator came into view much faster than before.

After all, I hadn't trembled with fear or kept my guard up against my surroundings.

“Please save me.”

A voice came from behind.

Yet I didn't even turn my head.

“Please, save me.”

“….”

“Save me, please.”

Ignored.

How long did I ignore the sounds coming from before long, the elevator entrance welcomed me.

Still ignoring the sounds coming from behind, I walked slowly into the elevator.

Without looking back, I opened the notebook.

[149. Just exercise caution on the path before the shelter. If you're barefoot, I recommend putting on shoes.

On the path after the shelter, you must only walk when the light is on.

Neverrunneversurleyeverwalknever■■■■■■.]

I glanced slightly to the side.

A mistake everyone made at least once.

No. You couldn't even call this a mistake.

Something all of us naturally, unconsciously did the moment we stepped into the elevator.

A single trap hidden while desperately erasing even the contents of the notebook.

And yet, a trap designed so that others would have no choice but to fall for it helplessly.

Just as I'd thought earlier, the presence of the mascot suit was actually poison.

It made you mistakenly think there was someone you could talk to.

And in doing so, it gave people hope.

The hope that they might not be alone.

■■■■■■.

*‘Don't look back.’*

It targeted loneliness.

And so, we looked back.

Whether out of fear, in search of hope, or simply because we were startled, there were bound to be plenty of reasons…

Many people must have looked back, and many must have died.

That must be why there were so many clothes scattered across the floor of the corridor.

I was no different.

I'd looked back at someone's call, and that's why I died.

The others were the same.

In the first place, once you get into the elevator, you usually have to turn around to press the button.

Meaning that unless you died and came back like me, you were bound to look back at least once.

Then what was the difference between those who survived and those who didn't?

Could you survive if you turned around inside the elevator?

No, that couldn't be it.

If it were, everyone wouldn't have left behind warnings telling you not to look back.

“….”

I recalled what No. 24 had said.

*The advice I'll give you is, keep the elevator's speed in mind.*

Keep the elevator's speed in mind.

And keep the chime in mind, too.

The elevator doors didn't close instantly.

It took a certain amount of time for them to close.

Without turning my head, I reached out my hand and felt along the wall.

Soon, I felt the cold texture that felt just like an elevator button.

And from behind, the voice calling out to me was still audible.

“Ding…”

“….”

I am definitely not looking back.

Not after everything I'd been through.

I shifted my gaze to the notebook in my hand.

First things first, let's get to work.

I sat down and started writing in the notebook.

Since I’d done everything I could do.

First.

<s>[150. Watch out for the floor]</s>

The floor didn't need watching out for anymore. Because…

Scribbling out the text with heavy strokes of my pen, I began to write.

[150. Cleared out all the obstacles in the front section. No problems as long as you move only when the lights are out.]

I’d cleared away all the obstacles myself, after all.

I came close to dying a few times, but I hadn't died, so it was fine.

If anyone asked why I did something so crazy...

Why *did* I?

Once I escaped this place, other people would probably get dragged in here again.

Besides, ever since I'd gained the ability of Regression, I had a way out even if I died, and as a result, I hadn't died even once.

Lastly...

“….”

Just writing *'Don't look back'* wouldn't make sense to anyone.

Something indirect... yet something they couldn't possibly misunderstand.

First of all, I had to assume someone would read this notebook.

Which meant it would be censored.

Then...

Something only humans possessed, something only people could understand...

“….”

A hint...

Right. I'll write that.

I picked up my pen and started writing in the notebook.

[150. And think of the story of Orpheus. Don't repeat the mistake Orpheus made.]

This was what I had in mind.

Orpheus and Eurydice.

The one who went all the way to the underworld for his beloved, succeeded in bringing her back, but in the end couldn't help himself and looked back, losing his loved one.

To be precise, Orpheus had gotten his body out.

It was just that Eurydice couldn't make it outside.

Exercise caution until the very end.

Doubt everything.

Wouldn't this be enough?

At least by my common sense, I figured they'd understand it well enough.

Other than this...

[150. There is only one button.]

Just in case, let's put their worries to rest.

Let's see. The button...

If there were multiple buttons, wouldn't you inevitably have to turn your body around?

Then what should you do?

“….”

Why agonize over it?

You could just die and memorize it next time.

Thinking that, I fiddled with the button, and—

*“...Whew.”*

Fortunately, just like I'd written beforehand, there was only one button.

I'd been putting on a brave front inside, but I really didn't want to go through that pain again. Thank goodness.

Letting out a sigh of relief, I pressed the button.

*CLANK!*

With a loud noise, the door began to close.

A screeching sound, followed by the sound of machinery.

I'll just wait until the door closes.

Thinking that, I closed my eyes and waited.

Treating me like a fool.

I was never, ever going to look back.

How long had I waited with my eyes closed, thinking that?

After a dull thud signaled the door closing, the elevator shook slightly.

The usual sound the door made when shutting.

*“...Hah.”*

I slowly turned my head—

and locked eyes with the thing still staring at me, its head jammed in the elevator door.

With a *creeeak*, it looked right at me.

Its face was still floating there.

Where its eyes should have been, pitch-black holes sat empty, and with its body out of sight, nothing trickled from the cleanly severed surface of its neck.

Mechanically, it opened its mouth.

As if that was the only thing it could do.

“Please... save... me....”

*—SLAM!*

Before it could say anything else, the elevator door slammed shut.

“….”

My heart felt like it was going to burst.

I thought I was going to die.

Even if I hadn't actually died, just now...

Barely suppressing my pounding chest, I leaned against the elevator wall and slid down into a seated position.

*“...Whew, hoo-ooo....”*

I thought I was dead.

Seriously, why was the elevator closing so slowly?

I genuinely thought I was a goner.

Thinking that, I fumbled with the notebook, and—

*“...Huh?”*

Before I knew it, I realized the notebook had vanished.

So once you get out of here, the notebook disappears.

Which was actually a relief.

If the notebook didn't disappear, wouldn't that mean this place wasn't the end?

Thinking that, I closed my eyes.

Well, anyway, I survived.

What was I supposed to do now that I’d survived?

Should I call the cops?

Or else, what... post on the internet?

How much time had even passed?

If too much time went by and I missed my shift, what on earth was I supposed to tell my team leader?

While I was leaning against the wall thinking those things—

*—BEEP-BEEP-DING!*

A sound chimed from the elevator.

“…?”

That's the sound our elevator makes when it gives out an announcement or something.

Thinking that, I perked up my ears.

&lt;Congratulations.>

What reached my ears was an unexpected word of congratulations.

Congratulations?

For what?

Just as I was thinking that,

&lt;Congratulations.>

Once again, the voice continued.

It was a mechanical voice.

A voice so heavily laced with static that it was impossible to tell whether it belonged to a man or a woman.

&lt;No. 150 has cleared the first floor at the fastest speed among all past users.>

&lt;Furthermore, you have laid the groundwork for others.>

&lt;Due to your consideration, those who come after will be able to clear the floor much more easily.>

&lt;We present you with a reward accordingly.>

*“…A reward?”*

A reward?

Why would it say that?

It sounded almost like...

there was a next floor.

Cold sweat trickled down.

&lt;This is the reward given to No. 150.>

With a ding, something dropped from the air.

A half-torn ticket.

...A ticket?

That thought lasted only a moment.

&lt;We give you something that will help you advance further.>

Its voice echoed out.

Something to help me advance further?

&lt;We hope you do your best on the next floor as well.>

And before I could even reply, a ding echoed through the air.

“….”

I slowly turned my head.

The elevator door was open.

And right beyond it, the mascot costume I'd seen earlier was waving at me.

“Yikes!”

*“...!!!”*

When I screamed, the mascot costume scurried backward against the wall as if startled itself.

You scared the living daylights out of me.

A pristine bear-faced mascot suit with a filthy, grime-stained body.

That was...

*“…The mascot?”*

“!”

Hearing my words, the mascot costume nodded repeatedly and spread both arms wide.

As if it had been startled too, or as if it were welcoming me.

“How on earth are you here—”

Once again cutting me off, a voice poured out of the elevator.

The familiar voice announcing the floors that we always heard when riding elevators.

&lt;You have arrived at the second floor.>

“…!”

In an instant, cold sweat ran down my spine.

There *was* a second floor.

The thing I wanted to imagine least in the world had become reality.
