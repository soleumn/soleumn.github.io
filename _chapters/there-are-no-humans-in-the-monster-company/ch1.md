<div style="color: var(--texto-secundario); font-style: italic;">

<br>It's rare for everything in life to go according to plan.

After all, isn't it precisely the variables that make life fun?

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

Every morning, we step out the door heading somewhere.

We check the subway schedule, set our alarms, sort through our to-do lists in our heads, and step outside.

But the place we actually end up is always different.

A packed street, a sudden downpour, an unexpected text from someone we weren't planning to hear from—every one of these little deviations reshapes the day.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

Variables always come with two faces.

One is the face of chaos. The other is the face of opportunity.

Thanks to that, there's a moment where you freeze on a crowded sidewalk, and then a moment where your heart races as you hit "Accept" on an unexpected offer.

Perhaps our lives are made more brilliant not by the plan itself, but by that split-second of ingenuity that throws it off course.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

And so the plan falls apart, but in its place, something new takes root.

Sometimes, that something turns out to be a better direction.

We call it "chance." The more poetic among us call it "fate."

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

That's what life is.

The more you try to control it, the more it slips through your fingers. The more you let go, the more an unexpected place stands ready to welcome you.

What matters in the end is keeping forward, even while you're being shaken.

So let's quietly welcome today's unexpected variables, too.

They might just make our lives more beautiful.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

</div>

*'Fuck this. Fucking hell. It's all goddamn bullshit.'*

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

Variables are what make life interesting?

Unexpected things are what enrich your life?

Only some brainless piece of shit would talk like that.

Yeah, I get it. That kind of stuff probably sounds pretty damn poetic to someone like I was last night—standing on the verge of their first day of work.

But right now, I want to kick those words with my bare feet and shove them into the trash can.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

*'...What the hell is this.'*

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

I'd tidied my collar, forced down whatever was bubbling up in my chest, and opened the front door.

What greeted me should have been the ordinary hallway of my apartment building.

Under the familiar fluorescent lighting, a few delivery boxes rolling around—the usual scene.

But what spread out before me was the interior of a company.

And not just any company, mind you. This one was considerably further from "normal" than most.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

The first thing I noticed was an enormous, hairy arm sticking out from somewhere.

Ten thin columns of flesh resembling fingers extended from the arm, tapping against the wall in alternating patterns as though playing a piano.

Another creature, whose head had been replaced by a mass of purple tentacles, was holding a cup beneath a vending machine.

On one side of the hallway, someone in a business suit was passing by in a hurry—but everything below the waist was missing. Every part that wasn't its lower body had been folded flat like paper.

As if the very image of a person had been printed thin and stuck on a wall.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

"...."

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

I was still frozen dead in front of my front door.

The monsters in their corporate cosplay were commuting as if nothing was wrong.

No stench, no gore, no blood. It looked like the most normal company on earth.

Except for the fact that not a single human being was in sight—other than me.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

*"...Am I dreaming?"*

The low moan that leaked from my lips was swallowed by the eyes of a coworker passing by, all of them blinking in unison.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

***

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

How did I end up in a company crawling with these monsters?

Honestly, I'm not entirely sure myself.

To explain, I'll have to rewind a bit.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

My life plan was simple, originally.

Study just enough to get into university, graduate without any issues, land a decent job and make some money.

In hindsight, I thought I was tracking pretty well along that ordinary trajectory—right up until the moment I got into college.

Even when I ended up becoming an non-commissioned officer, I figured it was just one of countless coincidences.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

And then everything fell apart. Literally, in an instant.

I saw things that weren't supposed to be there, heard things I didn't want to hear, and couldn't get a single word out.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

Yeah, it was just something like that.

I have no intention of spreading the real reason around.

What matters is that I eventually graduated, got discharged, came back to civilian life, and slapped the label of job-seeker on myself.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

The problem was that job-hunting turned out to be more combative than I'd expected.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

Rejected on paper. Rejected in interviews. Endless revisions to my self-introduction document with no end in sight….

My bank balance draining toward zero in direct proportion to the passage of time.

A life where falling behind on rent meant getting kicked out, and running out of living expenses meant going hungry—all while still job-hunting.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

One day, after walking out of a job interview I couldn't even count which number it was,

a square piece of paper on the road caught my eye out of nowhere.

A black business card. No gloss. The edges warped, like mold had been eating at it.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

The fact that I couldn't just ignore it right then was the biggest mistake of my life.

I picked up the business card out of curiosity. The company website address printed on it.

I was drawn in almost against my will, and the moment I opened the page, there it was—written in letters big as a doorway:

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

**<center>Sapilgwijeong LLC<br>Now Hiring<br>Starting salary: 68 million won / 100% interview pass rate</center>**

<br>

Over five million a month.

The instant I saw that sentence, I was already hooked.

Was it a scam? A kidnapping? I didn't even have the luxury of telling the difference at that point.

If anything, I would've been fine with it being a scam as long as a decent amount hit my account.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

I sent my self-introduction document—the one that had been sitting in a permanent state of readiness via email—and called the number on the business card.

After I asked whether I could do an interview today, the place I was directed to as the interview venue was the fourth floor of a rundown building.

No intercom. No sign. Just a half-open door giving off the smell of mold.

The kind of atmosphere that screamed something was seriously wrong at a glance.

But humans are genuinely pathetic creatures.

Once you've sunk even a little cost, you just can't easily back out.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

*'I'll just take a quick look and leave.'*

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

With that mindset, I walked all the way in.

Looking back now, either I was truly crazy at the time, or maybe it wasn't even my judgment anymore from that moment on.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

A small interview room.

The fluorescent light flickered, and the air was thick with a sticky smell that was either oil or mold—impossible to tell which.

A person in a suit sitting in the center of that interview room.

No. Something resembling a person.

At first it was clearly just one person, but for some reason, every time I turned my head, another face appeared.

A head beside a head, a head, and then another head.

Below the smiling face was a dozing one, and beside that, a crying one.

Pale faces, each with different expressions and breathing sounds.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

***"Mr. Jeong Haeil? You're here for the interview?"***

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

One of those many faces said that.

Someone did speak, but I couldn't explain exactly where the voice came from.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

***"I'm Manager Myeon, team leader of Extraction Team 1."***

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

One of the countless faces clicked its tongue in disgust as it spoke.

After that, my memory is hazy.

I think I heard the grotesque voices of those grotesque faces while looking at documents, and I think I signed something in an agreement column.

Why didn't I refuse?

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

***"Alright. We'll get in touch within a few days, so just show up for your first day. It's a pleasure to have you, Employee Jeong Haeil."***

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

The last thing I saw was the monster displaying seven faces with a kind smile, and in that moment, consciousness went out like a light.

When I opened my eyes again, I was back in my apartment.

And the moment I looked to the side, I was holding both the business card I'd picked up on the street and, for the first time, an employee ID card.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

**<center>Employee Jeong Haeil<br>Sapilgwijeong LLC | Extraction Team 1</center>**

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

The employee ID card, still left in my hand with that sticky, uncomfortable feeling, and the musty-smelling business card too—

I wanted to believe it was nothing more than a grotesquely vivid nightmare—an illusion conjured by an exhausted brain.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

But then, a few days later, my phone buzzed with a message notification.

The moment I saw the words on the screen, I had to face reality.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

**<center>[Caller ID: Blocked]<br>You can start coming in from tomorrow. I'll pick you up in the morning.</center>**

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

Picking me up in the morning?

I couldn't remember where the company was.

Even when I searched the website on the business card again, there was no record of it at all.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

So in the end, I had no choice but to believe it.

That unknown promise to come pick me up.

Hoping that this accidental incident wouldn't ruin my life.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

***

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

And right now, this is the scene I'm facing.

I took a deep breath first.

An unfamiliar hallway. The indifferent morning procession of things in suits that were decidedly not human.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

*'Ah, so it's a dream.'*

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

I gripped the doorknob.

Hoping to see the narrow hallway of my studio apartment again, the familiar stairs of the building, I carefully closed the door and then opened it again.

But when I opened it, all I could see was the back of a coworker walking ahead, their countless eyeballs blinking.

The scene before me hadn't changed.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

***"Employee Jeong Haeil."***

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

In that instant, a chilling voice from beside me.

I tried as hard as I could to appear composed, slowly turning my head.

I checked my expression in the glass wall, fixed a blank mask onto my face, and only then was I able to fully shift my gaze.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

Bloodless, pale skin—like a plaster cast soaked in water.

Seven faces, stuck gloopily onto wobbly flesh—no eyes, no tongues, just bottomless black holes gaping open.

A face screaming, a face gasping, a face crying, a face snickering.

Among them, one face with a calm expression smiled like an ordinary boss and spoke.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

***"It's your first day, right? Is something the matter?"***

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

That monster from my memory.

The seven-faced monster that interviewed me in that grotesque building and made me sign.

The scream rising to the top of my throat—I forced it down by biting my tongue.

The moan that felt like it would burst out was swallowed back down my throat along with the taste of blood.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

*'If I lose it now, it's over.'*

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

I could feel it instinctively.

That the moment I stood out here, or ruffled those monsters' feathers, I would never face a good outcome.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

I swallowed the bitter, metallic taste of my own saliva.

As naturally as possible. As expressionless as possible.

At the absurd sight of the seven-faced monster, what remained of my sense of normalcy ground a little further.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

"Yes. It's my first day, so I'm a little… nervous."

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

One of the seven faces snickered, and another nodded with a tearful expression.

But the most human-looking face, positioned at the front, maintained its tolerant smile as it spoke.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

***"We all are. Everyone's nervous on their first day."***

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

He pulled something like a keycard from his pocket, flicked it between his fingers, brushed past me, and gestured forward.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

***"Come, Employee Jeong. Do you remember my name?"***

"Ah, yes. You said Manager Myeon back then⋯⋯."

***"Good. I'll give you a brief introduction to the company today. You can learn the details one by one starting tomorrow. Oh, could I have your phone? It's for the security app."***

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

I nodded, handed Manager Myeon my phone, and followed behind him.

My heart was still pounding as if it would burst, but thankfully my legs were only shaking enough to go unnoticed.

Past the hallway crawling with monsters, into the elevator, and after descending for what felt like forever, the doors opened⋯⋯.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

The first thing that hit me was the stench.

Fishy, moldy, burning metal and rubber, perfume, raw eggs…

Things that could never possibly belong together were mixed together and tore their way into my nostrils.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

Manager Myeon casually handed me my phone back and started walking.

The inner hallway writhed with a wet, fleshy texture, and even the lights blinked like eyeballs, glaring at me.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

***"The core of our Extraction Team 1 is Dimensional Entity management."***

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

Manager Myeon's voice echoed.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

***"Safely storing Dimensional Entities as company assets, and taking out only what we need when we need it. That's our desk work."***

"…Dimensional Entities?"

***"You'll meet them soon."***

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

Where he stopped was in front of a thick, sealed metal door.

The words "Extraction Room Zone D" were clearly engraved on the surface.

When Manager Myeon held his keycard to the excessively wide panel, the lock disengaged and the massive door slowly opened.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

What seeped out from the gap was not a human presence.

Damp breathing, unfamiliar crying, whisper-like static—all of it poured out at once and swirled inside my ears.

The air entering my lungs felt like rotting water, and the moment I inhaled, my throat tightened.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

I instinctively tried to step back, but froze.

I couldn't move my legs. Something from beyond felt like it had stretched my ankles taut and nailed them to the ground.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

When the door fully opened, rows of transparent containment chambers stretched endlessly before me, and inside them, monsters too unrecognizable to make out were packed tightly.

Something with only arms. A floating face. A shadow-like thing that moved as if flowing along the fluorescent light.

All those monsters simultaneously raised their heads and looked at me, the unfamiliar outsider.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

I pressed down the goosebumps spreading across my entire body and bit my lip.

I couldn't even breathe.

The countless eyeballs behind the containment chambers blinked simultaneously, and it felt like they were calling my name.

In that moment, Manager Myeon's voice whispered lightly beside me.

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

***"Welcome to Sapilgwijeong LLC. I truly congratulate you on your hiring, Employee Jeong."***

<div style="height:2rem"></div>

Seven faces broke into laughter at once.

And as if the monsters in the containment chambers were laughing along too, eardrum-splitting screams erupted like a wave.

Not crying. Not screaming. Not laughing. Something else entirely.

As if every sound that humans couldn't imitate were tangled together and pouring out all at once.

In the middle of that chaotic vortex, only one thought surfaced clearly.

My life is…

pretty fucking screwed, it seems.
