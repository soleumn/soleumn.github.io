<br>The space shattered into fragments and collapsed.

As if it no longer needed to maintain its form now that the observers had left.

It cast off the mask it had been forced to wear and began scattering into its original shape.

I fell.

Plummeting beneath the shattered space, I watched.

The train pulling away, leaving the crumbling space behind.

Yeah, this isn't a bad choice.

I repeated that to myself and closed my eyes.

<br>

<center>*&emsp;*&emsp;*</center>

<br>

...I think I need to correct what I said earlier.

This is really bad.

Incredibly bad. Possibly worse than I ever imagined.

I was still falling.

"Ugh...!"

Falling endlessly was like being continuously pummeled by a typhoon.

The friction of the air made it hard to breathe.

My hair and the hems of my clothes kept fluttering wildly.

On top of that, the fragments of space scattered around me rushed past, leaving only afterimages.

How much time had passed since I got off the train?

It felt like about 30 minutes.

And I was still falling.

Surely this state wouldn't last forever.

...Damn, just imagining it sent chills down my spine.

How much longer do I have to endure this?

"Stop...!"

*Halt-!*

The moment I shouted, unable to bear it any longer.

My body, which had been falling endlessly, jolted to a stop in midair.

The scenery that had been rushing past me like tunnel lights also froze in place.

"...?"

Why did it actually stop?

"...Fall?"

*Whoosh-!*

As if it had heard my words, my body plunged downward again.

"Stop! Stop!"

*Halt-!*

The fall halted again at my shout.

So, this is something I can control?

If so, they should have told me sooner.

I'd been falling for 30 minutes feeling like I was about to have a heart attack...

I slowly steadied my breath and looked around.

"Ha."

I'd managed to stop the fall, but I couldn't help but sigh again.

Belying the distance I'd fallen, I was still on the platform of Yongsan Station.

But the space wasn't a single one.

Around me, all of time was spread out like a catalogue.

Night, day.

Sunlight, snow, rain and wind.

Crowded hours, hours when no one was there.

People boarding trains, people who missed their trains.

The modern era, an old railway station, an accident, a severed station attendant, a sea of fire, an even older railway station.

And a field even older than that.

All those moments were right before my eyes.

The pasts that, ordinarily, would lie buried beneath the surface called the present, in strata where they can't be seen.

What on earth is this space...

Suddenly, Author Na Bit's words came to mind.

<br>

-If there were a being higher-dimensional than humans that could see time spread out like space, what would this street look like? Next to a person peacefully drinking tea, flames would surge up; beside a person burned black and dead, a child's bicycle would pass by; shopping mall music, the sound of shells exploding, and sirens would all mix together—it would be incredibly chaotic and colorful, wouldn't it?

<br>

Was this the kind of sight the author had been talking about?

She saw the same thing as me with her eyes, but in her mind, she was painting this kind of hidden side.

If Author Na Bit had put what was in her head into words.

And if I had edited that writing, would I have been glad to see this sight for the first time?

Ah.

I was blankly admiring, then realized belatedly.

Come to think of it, there was an ending I'd decided on.

The ending of this bizarre story that had suddenly invaded my life.

That was to look over Author Na Bit's new work, but it seems that's gone out the window.

My mood became a bit complicated.

But well, this is a common occurrence.

If anything, it's rare for things to go as planned.

Besides, I don't think the current ending is the worst.

It's a bit ambiguous whether it's complete or incomplete, but at least it's not a dog's death.

Anyway, someone got out alive.

Moreover, I bought twelve for the price of one—it wasn't a losing deal.

With this, wouldn't it count as an ending of "I'll leave the rest to you"?

So there's no need to regret.

Even if it is a bit unfortunate.

I composed myself and looked around again.

More importantly, how long do I have to stay here?

Surely this isn't what death is.

Just as I was blankly gazing at the time spread out around me.

*Crrrrack-!*

A sound of something breaking rang out, and above my head brightened white.

*Crrack- Craaack-!*

A sound as if the world was being torn apart.

I raised my head at the sound and light, and held my breath on the spot.

A pure white billow filled my entire vision.

Wings made of light, their ends beyond measure, were descending toward me.

It felt as if the sky was approaching me.

Overwhelmed by the sight, I froze in place.

Then, a small, soft hand touched my cheek.

Only then did I notice.

The woman who had descended, leading those wings.

In the pure white light, the woman gently cupped my cheek.

"Now, let's go."

At her whisper, my heart sank with a thud.

At that moment, I realized it instinctively.

The fact that I stood before death.

And that I was about to die soon.

The survival instinct belatedly stirred up fear.

But I couldn't resist.

The being before me was too immense, and like someone already dead, I could only wait for this gentle touch to cut off my breath.

*Press-*

But...

*Press-*

For some reason, the woman only pressed my cheek with her hand.

"...?"

*Press- Press-*

At the touch that kept pressing my cheeks as if teasing a child, I felt my awe and fear gradually subside.

As I stared blankly at her, the woman looked back at me.

Then, as if she found it strange, she murmured.

"How odd. Why is this happening?"

"...Um, is there a problem?"

"What did you do?"

"Pardon?"

"You're fixed to the space right now."

"Fixed?"

"To put it in terms you can understand, you've become a lingering spirit?"

"...!"

"Ah, I don't mean you've become a ghost. You're not dead yet, after all."

"...?"

"But you've been bound to this space. Something is still tying you down."

The woman said this while turning my cheek this way and that.

Her attitude was as light as if she were examining a broken watch.

"...So what happens to me now?"

"I'll take you with me."

"But you can't take me right now, can you?"

"You're bound by causality, so a time will come when it's undone. Though I don't know if that'll be a few years or a few hundred years."

"A few hundred years?"

"It's fine. I'm good at waiting."

No, I'm not fine!

You expect me to spend a few hundred years in a place like this?

As I was horrified, the woman withdrew her hand from me and spoke.

"If waiting gets boring, you can fall asleep. When the bind is undone, I'll wake you."

"...What exactly is binding me?"

"Your ties to the world. Among them, there's one that hasn't been undone yet. I can't see exactly what it is yet, but it will loosen soon. Your world will regard you as dead from now on."

"..."

It was something I'd already braced myself for, but hearing it in words made it feel real anew.

"When all of it is undone, will I die?"

"Yeah."

"What is death like?"

"I can't say. But I will guide you."

A bird that guides souls.

Perhaps that referred to this woman.

Apparently deciding she couldn't take me right away, the woman settled herself onto the empty air beside me.

What is this?

Am I really supposed to wait hundreds of years like this?

...Then how about letting me go back out and live about 50 more years instead?

I couldn't keep up with this situation and its scale at all.

Then I touched the cheek where the woman's hand had been.

Her touch was gentle, and warm.

There was no malice in her attitude toward me.

Come to think of it. This woman was the one who had kept me alive until now.

If so, wouldn't it be fair to say her feelings toward me were close to goodwill?

I tried to examine the woman, but noticed the white wings behind her shimmering.

Something already grand, and now it was glittering too—I couldn't look at her comfortably.

"Can't you get rid of the wings behind you?"

"Wings?"

At my request, the woman looked back at her own back.

Then she chuckled softly and said.

"These aren't wings. They're my domain. The place where you woke up earlier."

"...?"

Come to think of it, the white billow behind the woman had no definite form.

It was just stretched out white, and I had arbitrarily assumed they were wings.

But that's the space I was in earlier?

"This is my younger sibling's domain, so I can't move around freely. That's why I brought my own domain along."

So that's the kind of worldview this is...

"But earlier, it didn't seem to shimmer like that."

If it was the space I was in, there was one coffin and it was all white.

It didn't shine so flashily.

At my question, the woman answered in a monotonous voice.

"It's because they're making a fuss right now."

"Making a fuss?"

"Mostly complaints. They must not like the situation I just created."

Complaints?

Come to think of it, this woman sometimes spoke in plural.

Saying "we," she had revealed several times that she wasn't singular.

With a sense of disbelief, I asked the woman again.

"What is it they don't like?"

"Opinions vary. Why did you save the losers, save Seo Doun too, the choice you just forced was the worst, and so on..."

"By any chance, are there any complaints hoping for my survival?"

"Yeah."

The woman nodded lightly.

"Originally, it was fifty-fifty on whether to kill you or let you live. But the recent incident changed the mood a bit. The opinion that you should live because you did well is now the majority."

"Then just let me live."

I seized on it without a second thought.

No, even a death row inmate gets spared if the execution fails.

You just failed to kill me, so just let me off.

If my death isn't certain, and can be overturned at your whim...

Besides, it's not like you particularly dislike me or want me dead.

I placed my hopes on this woman's subtle goodwill.

Indeed, the woman kept smiling at me.

And even now, with a smile at the corner of her lips, she said to me.

"No."

"..."

"I don't want to keep watching you."

"...Why not?"

"Because I like you."

It was an answer I couldn't understand.

"I'll kill you and give you rest."

It was a way of thinking I couldn't understand.

...But thinking about it differently, it wasn't so incomprehensible.

Yeah, now that I say it, I understand.

This was the older sister of the monster called 「Big Cat」.
