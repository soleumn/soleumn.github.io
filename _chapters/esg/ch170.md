<br>BANG-! THUMP-!

SWOOSH-!

Two doors opened in succession.

Then water gushed out violently, just like an opened dam.

The water quickly rose to our knees.

SPLASH-!

We helplessly lost our balance against the overwhelming current.

“Outside!”

Cha Sieon shouted.

Using our hands to push off the floor as if swimming, we escaped back into the waiting room.

Most of the floor in the large waiting room was still dry.

However, the water flowing from the other side of the corridor was rushing out at a rapid pace.

Moreover...

BANG-! THUMP-! THUMP-!

SWOOSH-!

Doors started opening everywhere, and water began to pour out.

Damn it, are they really opening a floodgate or something?

“To the tunnel for now...!”

“Wait a minute!”

Cha Sieon suggested taking refuge in the tunnel, but leaving him behind, I looked around the waiting room.

Meanwhile, a few more doors opened and water poured out.

Fearing I would lose all the candidates this way, I ran toward a door that was still closed.

Then I pulled that door open.

“Seo Doun!”

Cha Sieon’s mixed-warning shout.

And what unfolded beyond the door I opened was the towering blue sky.

“Team Leader, we have to open the doors! We need to open the doors ourselves before the water pours out from them!”

Only then did Cha Sieon grasp my intent.

From then on, we ran around the waiting room and opened doors indiscriminately.

WHOOSH-

As I opened the door to reveal the sky, the water pooling on the floor naturally flowed outward.

Seeing that, I shouted again.

“Match the spaces to the sky!”

So far, the proportion of doors revealing the sky when opened was roughly 40%.

So matching the spaces to the sky was not all that difficult.

The only problem was the unknown force that, competing with us, accelerated the opening of doors and the pouring of water.

BANG-! THUMP-! THUMP-!

SPLAAAAASH—!

Before long, every door in the waiting room had opened.

However, the score was dismal.

The doors from which water poured out were about three times more numerous than the doors connected to the sky.

Now it was only a matter of time before the water rose.

<br>

Twenty-six hours had passed since we were isolated in the waiting room of the 「Railway Station」.

The first floor of the waiting room was soaked with water, so we moved our location to the second floor.

And opening the door to one of them, we explored the 'wide field'.

“How is it?”

I asked Cha Sieon, who came back after looking around the field.

However, there seemed little need to hear the answer.

Team Leader Cha's grim expression already said it all.

“There is no end. It does not seem like a proper space.”

“...”

It had now been two hours since the water started pouring.

The momentum of the falling water had not diminished even a bit.

And that water was steadily filling up the platform on the lower floor of the waiting room.

How long would it take for the water level to rise up to here?

I could not even gauge it.

At shortest, a few hours; at longest, a few days would pass.

Among the options available to us, the most suitable place to avoid submersion was the 'wide field'.

Enclosed spaces with ceilings, like tunnels or stalactite caves, meant guaranteed death once water filled up.

Not that one could just dangle high up in the blue sky, either.

Yet the seemingly plausible “wide field” was just as hopeless.

“Still, just in case, let’s leave this door open.”

If forced to choose between drowning right here once water reached the second floor or being stranded once more in an endless field, the latter was clearly preferable.

“You mentioned earlier that you had a dream.”

Then Cha Sieon suddenly asked me out of nowhere.

“That Senior Lee Seop told you to wait.”

“…It’s not certain. It just felt like I had a dream like that, and even if my memory serves me right, it might just be a mere dream.”

Cha Sieon nodded at my explanation.

His face was full of thought.

After propping open the door leading to “wide field,” we went and sat down in a spot with a clear view of the first-floor waiting room.

As we ate a meal that couldn't quite be classified as breakfast, lunch, or dinner, I spoke up.

“What did Agent Lee Seop do before this?”

We no longer hid Lee Seop’s name.

Since we were already at Railway Station, what was the point of playing it safe?

Perhaps Cha Sieon shared the same sentiment, as he answered without hesitation.

“He was a veterinarian.”

An unexpected identity was thus revealed.

“So when someone was injured, he would perform simple surgeries on-site.”

“…?”

Well, okay, sure.

Humans were animals, after all.

And it must have been an emergency.

So this wasn’t a violation of medical law; it was first aid. Yeah.

“What was his character like?”

“He was a man of rare virtue. Especially in Bureau.”

Since this was the answer I had expected, I nodded.

Then, reading between the lines of what Team Leader Cha had said, I paused.

“Then what about Section Chief?”

“…….”

Team Leader Cha would rather keep his mouth shut than lie.

“Then what does that say about Section Chief Kwon Museop’s character?”

“There is much to learn from Section Chief.”

“Does his character happen to be included in that?”

“His current character, somewhat.”

“What about his past character?”

“I will decline to comment.”

Classic Team Leader Cha.

He refused to speak hollow flattery even if it killed him.

To be fair, even in my eyes, Section Chief Kwon Museop was exceptionally strict with Team Leader Cha.

Yet if this was an improvement compared to the past, just how harshly had Section Chief Kwon Museop worked Team Leader Cha back then?

No, even without hearing about it, I could pretty much picture it.

If a man nearly two meters tall was given the pitiful nickname Kongji, that really said it all.

Chuckling dryly, I mulled over Cha Sieon’s words.

Then, I muttered absentmindedly.

“…He must have been a good father to Lee Sol, too.”

“Had he been by his family’s side, he would have been a better father than anyone.”

Was that an affirmation or a denial?

In the end, it leaned closer to a denial, so I subtly took Lee Seop’s side.

“It’s not like he left on his own, right?”

“…….”

“That alone should make him a good father.”

In the dreams where I saw Lee Seop, he was always weeping and begging.

To save her.

Not himself, but Lee Sol.

If that wasn't merely an illusory dream, Lee Seop was definitely a good father.

And if what I heard in the dream really was Lee Seop's voice, we would have to wait here.

In this space where the water was steadily rising, that is.

48 hours into isolation.

WHOOOOSH-!

The first floor of the waiting room was finally submerged as well.

And as the door where water poured in and the door where water drained out were both submerged, an unexpected phenomenon occurred.

A cool whirlpool formed in the water filling the waiting room.

The water rising alone was maddening enough, but now it was even generating waves like a water park.

In about three to four hours, that water would reach the second floor.

But that was something that would happen three to four hours from now.

Right now, completing the bingo board in front of me came first.

“Yoon Woojeong.”

“Kim Wooin.”

“Ha Yuju.”

“Kwak Jaegyeom. Three bingos, I'm done.”

Declaring victory, I showed my notebook to Cha Sieon.

You might wonder why on earth we were playing bingo at a time like this, but this was far better than doing nothing.

Just watching the water level rise felt like preparing mentally right before being subjected to water torture.

To endure that stress, we drew lines on our notebooks again.

“Team Leader.”

“Speak.”

“Don't you regret coming back to catch me?”

“Seven to three.”

“Seven for regret, three for acceptance?”

“Seven for ‘I have to drag that thing out,’ three for ‘that thing is talking nonsense again.’”

Was it because we had been stranded together for two full days?

He had no qualms about referring to me as “that thing.”

Equally dumbfounded by Cha Sieon's vocabulary and statement, I asked back.

“Is there anything *you’re* actually afraid of, Team Leader?”

At my question, Cha Sieon’s hand moving the pen came to a halt.

Cha Sieon pondered for a moment, then spoke in a noticeably lowered voice.

“I hate watching helplessly while being unable to do anything. To the point where I'd rather just die.”

I glanced at Cha Sieon for a moment before looking back down at the bingo board.

As I filled in words on the bingo board, Cha Sieon spoke up this time.

“Seo Doun.”

“Yes.”

“What did you write in your will?”

“Sigh……”

I had barely managed to forget about it.

“Just... whatever words came to mind.”

“Is it something that would get you socially ruined?”

“No, it isn't……”

Just what do you take me for, Team Leader Cha?

“Then what are you worried about?”

The entirety of what I wrote.

Even explaining that in detail was embarrassing, so I just answered briefly.

“Because it's so long-winded and sentimental.”

“You’re long-winded on a normal day anyway, Seo Doun.”

“……”

Piqued, I glared at Cha Sieon, and for some reason, Cha Sieon meekly avoided my gaze.

Come to think of it, this bastard was the one who told me to write an embarrassing will in the first place.

Did he feel at least a shred of responsibility?

Right, if things go wrong here, Team Leader Cha's will will be made public too.

……Should I take comfort in not being alone?

BOOM-!

It was right when I was trying to comfort myself.

THUD-! CRASH-!

The roar rang out again.

We stopped what we were doing and went on high alert.

And yet……

“It feels like the sound has gotten closer than before, doesn't it?”

“It feels that way to me too.”

Is something approaching?

At that moment, a sound that felt like it was tearing eardrums echoed out.

GIGIGIGIGIGIK-!

“……!”

I reflexively covered my ears, then forcibly pulled my hands away.

Isn't this the crying sound of 「Big Cat」?

Having similar thoughts to me, Team Leader Cha took out a tablet.

Then he started recording.

BANG-! THUD-!

Loud noises continuing. And vibrations.

「Big Cat」 seemed to be making some kind of sound too, but the sound of the water was too chaotic, so I could not hear it well.

How long did we concentrate on that sound?

SCREE— SCREE-SCREE-SCREE—!

BANG-!

Along with a bizarre sound, the glass ceiling collapsed with a crash.

SWISSH-!

As if waiting for it, a madly pouring water bomb gushed down from the holed ceiling.

“……!”

It was an amount of water incomparable to what came out of the door.

When the water fell like a waterfall, the water rising below it swirled while creating an even fiercer torrent.

At this rate, instead of three or four hours, we looked like we would drown within an hour.

“If we are going to head out to the hill, we must go now.”

Cha Sieon spoke in an urgent tone.

The door connecting to the "wide field" we secured.

That door would also soon be submerged in water.

So if we want to go out, we need to go out now, but…….

Looking back and forth between the ceiling where water was pouring and the door where the field was visible, I asked Cha sieon.

“What about telling us to wait?”

“I will follow Seo Doun's decision.”

Damn it, that means he already made up his mind himself.

Suddenly saddled with the choice, I thought urgently.

Something is coming.

Water is rising.

Lee Seop told us to wait.

That might just be a dream.

I hate doing nothing and just watching.

I'd rather die.

Lee Seop is a good father.

“……I'll try waiting here.”

I ended up saying words I would regret.

And one hour later.

49th hour of isolation.

The water rose up to the ceiling.

We were hanging onto the steel frame beneath the glass ceiling, enduring the fierce torrent with our whole bodies.

And perhaps because we reached the point of gasping for breath.

I suddenly wanted to confess.

That what I mentioned earlier about an editor was actually a made-up excuse.
