<br>I vaguely woke up to the light filtering through the curtains.

A familiar ceiling. A safe and quiet room. A soft bed.

And plenty of leisure time with no need to force sleep away.

It was the best.

Satisfied with my situation, I buried my face in the pillow.

57 hours.

That was the time it took from when Cha Sieon and I were deployed to the suppression operation at 「Railway Station」 until we returned.

It felt like I'd been trapped there for over a week.

To think it was merely two days and nine hours.

My sense of time was a mess, but anyway, that disaster, hardship, and suffering were over.

A refreshed body after washing, clean and comfortable pajamas, pleasant air.

Even just the right amount of fatigue to help with deep sleep.

I was glad to be alive.

*Bzzzt—*

My smartwatch vibrated lightly.

It seemed like a message had arrived, but sleep came first right now.

*Bzzzt—*

Yeah, later.

*Bzzz— Bzzz— Bzzz—*

...What now.

Forcing my eyes open, I checked the name displayed on my smartphone.

Sure enough.

The sender was Cha Sieon.

Aren't you... tired...?

Intending to just check the content and go back to sleep, I tapped my smartwatch.

However, after checking the content, I had no choice but to get out of bed.

The message Cha Sieon sent was short.

<br>

—I'm going to visit Lee Sol.

<br>

<center>*&emsp;*&emsp;*</center>

<br>

Lee Sol's body recovered right after we secured the core of 「Railway Station」.

Just like the rescued person from 「Ant Hill」, her broken body was quickly restored, and her consciousness, which had remained hazy the entire time, returned to normal.

However, because the physical mutation had lasted for too long, her condition required monitoring.

Therefore, Lee Sol was still staying in the contaminated isolation room.

Still, since they said her recovery was distinct, I thought I should meet her and greet her warmly.

...That was what I thought when I stepped out.

Team Leader Cha's expression was strangely stiff.

What was with this atmosphere again?

Feeling an uneasy premonition for some reason, I asked Cha Sieon.

“By the way, didn't you say we had to wait a bit to meet Lee Sol?”

“Analysis Team 5 requested that we come.”

“A request?”

“They say Lee Sol has been refusing meals since regaining consciousness.”

“...?”

We barely managed to save her, and now she's on a hunger strike?

Is there any reason for that?

Or did some other problem arise?

I belatedly understood Team Leader Cha's mood.

And that this visitation wasn't just a warm reunion to share feelings.

Growing serious as well, I strode across the hallways of the Bureau.

A moment later.

We arrived at the isolation room where Lee Sol was staying.

However, what greeted us was an unexpected cold shoulder at the door.

“Sol says she can't meet you, Team Leader...”

Keeping us standing in the hallway, the researcher in charge of Lee Sol spoke.

“What is the reason?”

“It seems she remembers everything from when she was contaminated. She said she feels too sorry even to look at her uncle...”

“...”

“I contacted you hoping she might listen if she saw you, Team Leader, but oh dear...”

The researcher muttered, looking troubled.

Then he spoke to us again.

“I’m sorry, but please wait a moment. I’ll try to persuade her a bit more.”

The researcher stepped back into the isolation room, leaving us waiting in the hallway.

Too sorry to face us, huh.

I never imagined she would react like this.

Yet when I thought about it, it would have been far more unnatural if the kid had suddenly greeted us as if nothing were wrong.

For the past six months, Lee Sol had been trapped here, unable to go home.

Her body had gradually twisted, and unable to control herself, she had gone on rampages.

She had hurt people around her, and in return, she had been treated harshly.

At such a young age, the shock must have been immense.

Processing all those experiences would surely take quite some time.

*Click—*

A moment later, the researcher emerged back into the hallway.

Then, with an even darker expression, he shook his head.

“It really won’t work today. I’m so sorry, especially after you came all the way here.”

It seemed Lee Sol was adamantly refusing the visit.

“Did she happen to say what—”

Just as I was asking the researcher for the exact situation,

Cha Sieon suddenly headed for the door of the isolation room.

“Uh…”

The researcher was flustered, but he didn't try to stop Cha Sieon.

Neither did I.

Meanwhile, Cha Sieon strode right into the isolation room, and the researcher and I followed in after him.

In the middle of the spacious isolation room, I saw Lee Sol dressed in a gray sweatsuit.

Sitting on the bed, Lee Sol was sniffling and crying.

Another researcher was standing in front of her, trying to comfort her.

“…!”

Spotting Cha Sieon, Lee Sol jumped in surprise.

Lee Sol immediately hid behind the researcher’s back.

As if she had committed some grave crime.

Even from a distance, I could tell the child was shivering.

Cha Sieon, however, paid no mind and strode right up to her.

“Lee Sol.”

“…”

“It’s not your fault, so come out.”

Cha Sieon spoke bluntly.

Just because he was dealing with a fifteen-year-old didn't mean his voice became particularly gentle or affectionate.

Rather, he was more indifferent than usual, which actually made it sound more personal.

Like how one would speak to family.

It was a natural commanding tone one might use with a much younger sibling or a misbehaving niece.

Yet Lee Sol did not obey.

She wasn't explicitly refusing either, so the researcher caught between them glanced back and forth before subtly stepping aside.

With that, Lee Sol was exposed.

Lee Sol was sitting on the bed, her head hung low.

Like a child who had already been scolded, even before any scolding began.

“Why aren't you eating?”

At Cha Sieon’s pressing tone, tears dripped steadily from Lee Sol’s eyes.

Cha Sieon merely watched her, and after Lee Sol sobbed silently for a long time, she finally squeezed out a question.

“…Did my dad die?”

“…”

“Because of me?”

At Lee Sol’s question, Cha Sieon paused for the first time.

Cha Sieon turned to look at the researchers, who hurriedly shook their heads in surprise at his gaze.

The researchers stroked their chins, looking rather bewildered.

It was a natural reaction.

They weren't foolish enough to let slip any news about Lee Seop to Lee Sol.

Seeing the researchers' denial, Cha Sieon looked back at Lee Sol and asked,

“No. Who told you that?”

“Dad, I met him…….”

……She met her dad?

“He said he’s fine now, and told me to live well…….”

No way.

Did he come to Lee Sol just like he appeared in my dream and spoke to me?

“Did Dad die because of me? Dad, was he alive……?”

Lee Sol spat out the words while trembling.

Sobs mixed in every time she pronounced the word "Dad."

Before a truth she couldn't possibly handle, as if simply waiting to be punished.

We were left speechless at Lee Sol's question.

We couldn't lie.

But we couldn't tell the truth, either.

Her dad had been left neglected for two years, neither truly alive nor dead.

And then, because of her, they made the decision to kill him.

That was the best option.

No matter how you sugarcoated it, the facts were the facts.

We couldn't say this to a child.

Silence followed.

As if finding the answer in that silence, Lee Sol began to cry softly again.

“……Why are you only telling us that now?”

The researchers sighed at the sight.

They must have finally realized why Lee Sol had been refusing her meals and crying.

Lee Sol continued to cry, and Cha Sieon said no more.

The researchers tried to comfort her, but it was no use.

“I think we should leave her be until she calms down.”

Thinking nothing else could be done, the researchers tried to send us away.

Cha Sieon and I had no choice but to turn around.

“Just a moment.”

“……?”

“May I talk to her for a moment?”

I spoke to the researcher before stepping past the isolation room door.

The researcher looked puzzled at my request.

However, since it wasn't an unreasonable request, they soon nodded.

Thinking it would be better to talk quietly, Cha Sieon and the two researchers decided to wait outside for a bit.

And twenty minutes later.

I called back the people waiting in the hallway.

“Come in. Sol says she has something to say.”

At my words, Cha Sieon and the researchers entered the room looking quite surprised.

Lee Sol was still sniffling.

No, since she had just finished wailing, her face was even more tear-soaked than before.

Lee Sol saw Cha Sieon and let out another flurry of tears.

Even so, Sol regulated her breathing and spoke to Cha Sieon, word by clear word.

“Thank you, Uncle. And I’m sorry for hitting you……. When I grow up, I’ll be a dutiful filial to you…….”

“……?”

Cha Sieon was flustered.

Leaving him behind, Sol also greeted the researchers.

“Teacher, thank you for taking care of me all this time.”

“Huh? ……Huh?”

“I’ll eat my meals…….”

Lee Sol vowed to eat with a determined look on her face.

While the researchers welcomed the sight, they looked at me in bewilderment.

Cha Sieon also looked at me, asking with his eyes.

What on earth did you do?

To that, I replied with a relaxed smile.

Middle schoolers, honestly.

<br>

<center>*&emsp;*&emsp;*</center>

<br>

Lee Sol ate a whole bowl of rice, cried a bit more, and then fell asleep.

The adults who had struggled to feed and put her to sleep, especially the researchers who had been on edge all day, looked exhausted and drenched in sweat.

“We were really worried since she wouldn't talk and only cried since yesterday…….”

Muttered a researcher, exhaling a sigh of relief.

Then he let out a dry laugh and asked me,

"Just what on earth did you say to her?"

"I just said some conventional things."

"Conventional things?"

Lee Sol-ah, you're very sad, aren't you?

What did your dad say at the very end?

He told you to live well, right?

But is it okay for you to skip meals or not?

Do you really think your dad would want you to cry like this?

And if you keep crying, how do you think your uncle, who worked so hard, would feel?

...I had simply repeated those words for twenty minutes while observing Lee Sol's reactions.

At my explanation, Cha Sieon stared at me with cloudy eyes.

As if doubting whether what I had done was comfort, persuasion, or gaslighting.

On the other hand, the two researchers nodded seriously.

"As expected..."

"This is precisely why the humanities are important..."

"I guess this is why people need to read books."

"Sol is just good-natured, so she listened well."

In truth, Lee Sol's dad and uncle did all of that.

Lee Sol's experience was undoubtedly a painful one.

Yet, at least she wasn't lonely.

She wasn't isolated either, and there was someone by her side protecting her until the end.

So she would be able to get back up, no matter what.

Because someone who has received good things finds it easy to become a good person.

However, the researchers shook their heads vigorously.

Then they brought up an unexpected remark.

"No, Agent. We truly realize the greatness of the humanities right now."

"We all cried after reading your will too, Agent."

Will.

Will!?
