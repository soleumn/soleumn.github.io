<br>Honestly, I was taken aback.

When my nonchalance was pointed out.

When the woman's words—that I was terrified of death—came to mind.

When the thought that my reaction was strange crossed my mind for the first time.

I felt for myself that something was wrong.

So I explained.

I tried to explain somehow, to fit everything within the bounds of my understanding.

Because I felt that only then would I be at ease.

<br>

*BOOM-!*

With a deafening roar, the water surged violently.

“*Ugh*……!”

Half-swept away by the raging current, I grabbed the wire and barely managed to fight against the surge.

I felt like a fish trapped in a fishbowl.

And the fishbowl we were inside was now shaking madly.

<br>

Every person has their own narrative.

No, I desperately hope they do.

Because only then can I understand.

Once a narrative is complete, one can make sense of it later through each person's ending.

Why the world is so suffocating, and so cruel.

But what if we aren't stories that must be completed?

What if all of this is just a play we staged because we couldn't endure our own worthlessness?

<br>

The water level rose higher and higher.

When the glass ceiling was about to touch our heads, Cha Sieon shouted.

“Mr. Seo Doun! We're heading for the field!”

“Yes……!”

In the end, we changed our course.

Because just waiting here meant we really would drown.

It seemed my dream was nothing more than a simple daydream.

Still, in preparation for a situation like this, we had tied a wire to the door leading to the field.

If we dive and make it out to the field, at least there won't be a ceiling…….

I raised my head to take a deep breath.

Then, an unexpected shadow caught my eyes.

A colossal shadow casting over the glass ceiling above our heads.

A shape visible beyond the translucent glass.

Something vast, simply enormous, was drawing closer and closer to the ceiling.

As if peering into a fishbowl.

Or as if about to shatter the glass wall and devour us.

<br>

Honestly, there's no reason humans shouldn't be crushed to death like bugs.

Humans, after all, are the kind of beings who crush bugs to death the moment they see them.

Even if a god or a species superior to humans appeared and treated humans that way, with what right could we complain?

No.

Even this is assigning too much meaning.

Would a god really bother tormenting humans?

Humans might just be usurping the name of god because they want to dress up their desperation as a grand tragedy.

<br>

*SPLASH-!*

We plunged into the water to escape that shadow.

Over our heads poured the debris of the shattered ceiling.

*RUMBLE-!*

Steel frames plunged into the water, dragging down clusters of air bubbles.

As a result, the water clouded over, obscuring our vision.

Above that, shards of glass rained down.

*SPLASH-! RUMBLE-! SPLASH-!*

Fragments of the ceiling falling in succession.

And following right behind them was something enormous, something unidentified.

In its shadow, even the underwater world turned dark.

Like a literal wall, it bore down on us as if to crush us.

And once more, a deafening crash resonated.

<br>

Whatever.

I don't know anymore.

I had completely, utterly, and absolutely no idea what was going on.

I was also tired of asking why this was happening to me and searching for meaning.

So, I would admit that reality and stories are different.

And that reality isn't a game, either.

Stories are at least kind.

Every device is used reasonably before making its exit.

The characters meet an ending befitting their place.

Games are even more human-friendly.

They are mountains that can definitely be climbed, and every problem has a walkthrough and a solution.

But reality is not like that, and unlucky humans vanish fleetingly within it.

Without ever knowing the meaning of their own existence.

No, maybe such meaning never existed in the first place.

Maybe all of this is just the pitiful struggles of utterly wretched humans.

...Even so, if there is a God, please look upon us at least once.

Upon the plight of a human who has been isolated for far too long.

And the desperation of those who hate watching it more than dying.

<br>

“*Puhah!*”

I had been floundering in the pitch-black water, but suddenly I could breathe again.

“*Gasp, huff*...!”

Catching my breath, I looked around.

For some reason, I was back above the surface of the water.

What supported my body was an unrecognizable, thickly twisted lump of iron.

Looking to the side, Cha Sieon was also catching his breath, dripping wet.

What happened?

I was being crushed underwater by something like a giant wall.

Did that wall actually fish us out?

Unlike me, who was frantically looking around, Cha Sieon was staring intently at one spot.

After a moment, Team Leader Cha spoke.

“The water is draining.”

The water is draining?

Skeptical of his words, I raised my head.

The water that had been pouring from the ceiling had stopped.

No, the ceiling was gone.

The ceiling had collapsed entirely, opening up completely so that only the open sky was visible.

That sky was blue and clear, without a single raincloud.

Perhaps because the water pouring from above had stopped, the pool of water below was now calm as well.

However, the water level was dropping fast enough for us to noticeably feel it.

We watched the water drain.

As the water level lowered, the steel structure that had lifted us up was also revealed.

And when the water was completely gone.

Stepping onto the ground, we were struck utterly speechless.

The thing that had broken through the ceiling and descended.

The thing that had crashed over us underwater and scooped us up from the water.

It was none other than 「Giant Siren」.

<br>

<center>*&emsp;*&emsp;*</center>

<br>

Its entire body was destroyed.

Only a single arm was barely attached to its giant siren head.

What we had been riding on was the back of its hand.

Nothing of its body below the chest was visible.

Seeing that, I suddenly recalled.

The image of Lee Seop, who had struggled to deliver a message to us in 「Hotel」, only to be gruesomely punished for it.

“*Hah*...”

A sigh barely escaped my lips.

Did you crawl all the way here, ending up in this state?

Because you hated watching helplessly, too?

「Giant Siren」 stood frozen like that, with less than half its body remaining.

It didn't sound its siren, nor did it attack us.

Like a machine that had already ceased operating, it merely rusted under the dazzling sunlight pouring down.

We stared at the sight for a while.

*Step—*

After a long silence, Cha Sieon took a step forward.

He approached beneath the head of 「Giant Siren」 and drew his knife.

Then, just as Baek Sara had done before, he dug beneath its collarbone.

*Scrape— scrape, scrape—*

「Giant Siren」's arm swayed.

As if reacting to pain.

Yet that was all; it did not attack us.

Cha Sieon looked back at me.

Knowing what that meant, I walked over to where he stood.

I saw the core.

The very thing we had missed right before our eyes.

I quietly swallowed my breath, and Cha Sieon handed me the ring I had dropped.

Taking it, I lowered my voice and asked.

“Do you have any last words?”

Cha Sieon looked at me.

He seemed to ponder for a moment, then finally parted his lips after a long pause.

“I'm sorry I was late.”

Did he hear it?

He probably did.

Because it was quiet here now.

<br>

<center>*&emsp;*&emsp;*</center>

<br>

「Giant Siren」 began to collapse.

Just like an ancient castle crumbling away.

It dispersed without leaving even dust behind.

With that, the ruined 「Railway Station」 rapidly restored itself.

Soon, when the waiting room regained its former appearance, Cha Sieon's comms rang.

—Team Leader Cha Sieon, do you copy?

A tense voice.

It was the Bureau's Situation Control Room.

Cha Sieon, who had been sitting beside Lee Seop's body, took a deep breath and spoke.

“I copy. I am with Agent Seo Doun.”

A loud commotion echoed through the speaker.

It was the sound of the control team agents cheering. 

Cha Sieon closed his eyes for a moment as if enduring the noise.

Then he continued.

“「Giant Siren」 silenced. Core of 「Railway Station」 secured. The phenomenon of 「Railway Station」 overlapping with other Special Zones appears to have been resolved. In addition, the body of Agent Lee Seop...”

Cha Sieon's words cut off for a moment.

However, he soon continued.

“...has been recovered. The situation requires retrieval, so if there are personnel on standby at the entrance of 「Railway Station」, please send support.”

—Relaying to command.

As if understanding the situation, the control team agent's voice also grew much lower.

To that, Cha Sieon added in a dry voice.

“Please also check on the status of Contaminated Person Lee Sol.”

—Understood, checking right away!

After that, just from the sounds filtering through, I could tell the situation room had become frantic.

The situation room began making calls all over the place, and we waited silently for the result.

Prepared to accept whatever outcome came, we quietly held our breath.

The reason I like stories is that this world is incomprehensible.

The world is chaotic, ruthless, and cold.

Modeling such a world and refining it into a single book brings a sense of relief.

Watching a story race toward an utterly rational conclusion lets me breathe.

On top of that, authors—unlike God—are kind, bestowing mercy upon the pitiful cries of people.

That is why I love human-made books and the stories contained within them, yet I still haven't resolved to live inside a book forever.

That was because this world, on top of everything else, is fickle.

—We have confirmed Lee Sol’s situation with Analysis Team 5.

After a wait that felt like an eternity, the answer finally came from the situation room.

—They report that the mutated parts of Lee Sol’s body are collapsing.

“……”

Collapsing?

Amid our bewilderment, we struggled to grasp the meaning of those words.

Yet the more we thought about it, the more confused we became.

Even so, while we couldn't bring ourselves to speak, someone in the situation room shouted again.

—The parts mutated by contamination are falling away on their own! They say she’s recovering her original form!

“*Haa*…”

Only then did I exhale the breath I had been holding.

Cha Sieon was the same.

Unable to bring ourselves to laugh or cry, we silently closed our eyes.

<br>

The world is chaotic, ruthless, and cold.

Yet it was not hopeless forever.
