<br>The quest that I most dislike in Cell-X has begun.

However, it was impossible to cancel a quest that had already started.

I first checked the pudding slime.

When I reached out to my head, a soft body came up onto my palm.

I quickly put my hand down and checked.

“…….”

I saw Dominic who had turned into neon green slime.

As soon as I saw the sight soaked in fluorescent liquid, I almost burst out laughing, but I held it back.

It was because Dominic seemed to be in a bad mood.

I looked worried and wiped the pudding slime off with my hand.

“Are you okay? I’m sorry, it’s because of me.”

I fulfilled my selfish desires by pretending to clean him.

I massaged the soft body, and briefly took off and touched the prison guard hat that I had been curious about for a while.

I was curious about what material the prison guard’s hat was made of, but it wasn’t as soft as slime and felt like a regular hat.

I restored Dominic to the sparkly pudding slime as best I could and then put the hat back on.

Dominic, who had been holding still as I kneaded, finished by shaking out his ears to expel the last remaining liquid.

Then he looked up at me and said.

“Watching over Roasted Chestnut is not as easy as I thought.”

It seemed like he couldn’t understand the existence of God being harassed by things that were infinitely insignificant by his standards.

“I’m sorry, Dominic. I will get stronger quickly.”

When Dominic heard my answer, he burst into laughter for some reason.

I don’t know what’s funny, but Dominic then silently jumped up and climbed onto my shoulder.

Actually, it wasn’t the time to talk to Dominic.

This is because time is important here.

I took off my doctor’s gown and twisted it like I was doing laundry.

I finished reading the quest window with the sound of neon green liquid dripping as background music.

The place where the Chief of Security pushed me was the new prison guard training center.

I wanted to recruit him as a colleague because his favorability score was high, but if his suspicion score was also high, I would be pushed into this place.

Each time you complete training here, your progress as a new prison guard increases.

However, there was one catch.

Regardless of training completion, the progress of becoming a prison guard was guaranteed to reach 100% after a certain period of time.

It was because of the neon green liquid that I fell into when I first fell into the training room.

The neon green liquid, a key component of prison guards, was infectious.

The more you drink, the higher your starting percentage will be, and the less you drink, the lower your starting percentage will be.

*‘Once you touch it, you will definitely be infected, but… .’*

Still, thanks to being as careful as possible, I started with the lowest percentage, 1%.

I shook off as much of the neon green liquid that was left on my body.

*‘If you don’t have a solution, keep quiet. It’s a nuisance.’*

The system window no longer appeared.

In any case, there was nothing the system could do besides block my view.

And I really had no intention of becoming a prison guard anyway.

I walked down the dark passage. After I was walking along a corridor-like area for a while, suddenly, my vision became brighter.

Before I knew it, I was in a small, square room.

I knew that in rare cases the name given by the trial would change, so I just assumed it was something like that.

Why a lamb?

Since it was changing anyway, they should have given me a cool name; why did they give me an animal name?

In the game, the player starts playing without any information about the creatures.

Just like the human prisoners in the prison, the Creatures are considered to be violent criminals and choose harsh options for them.

There is no creature here who has committed a heinous crime.

The remote island prison that was thought to hold only the most vicious criminals was actually a detention facility for extracting biofuel from Creatures.

They used human criminals to block outside views and exploit creatures.

A total of twelve Creatures were imprisoned in this prison.

Except for creature No. 6, free communication with humans was impossible.

Creatures who could not properly protest were imprisoned, their freedom was taken away, and they were oppressed with violence.

And the Chief of Security was a scumbag who knew the whole truth.

I raised my baton.

And I brought my hand down.

“…Ugh!”

Even though I was prepared, it hurt quite a bit. I swallowed my groan and brought the baton down again.

The ‘10 hit’ rule had a slight loophole.

It was that as long as I was in contact with the creature, hitting my own body would be counted as a hit to the creature.

It was a way to pass the training without losing the creature’s favor, even though it was a bit painful.

*‘I only have to endure it ten times.’*

To be counted as a hit, a certain level of force had to be applied. I swung the baton violently.

It was when I had hit about six times.

Rustling…
