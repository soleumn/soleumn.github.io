<br>I looked at ‘me’.

‘I’ was sitting quietly.

In front of ‘me’, several people were raising their voices.

The atmosphere was so hostile that it wouldn’t have been strange if someone grabbed someone by the collar right then and there.

Strangely, everyone's faces were blacked out with ink.

Yet I recognized every single one of them.

Barely daring to breathe loudly, ‘I’ dropped my head.

The left hand resting on my knees came into view.

On the outside, there was nothing visibly wrong.

The fingers, clean without a single scratch, trembled faintly against 'my' will.

<br>

*“What on earth did you do to get nerve damage?! You said you were fine until yesterday!”*

<br>

The paper cup resting on the table knocked over, splashing lukewarm water heavily across the medical certificate.

The black print reading ‘Left Ulnar Nerve Damage and Sensory Paralysis’ blurred from the moisture.

Complex medical terminology followed below, but the gist was simple.

It meant that without immediate surgery, there was a high likelihood of permanent damage to the left ring and pinky fingers.

‘I’ recalled the doctor’s words.

*“Sixth man? Ha, the sixth man? Are you saying we should put a 2nd-team call-up in the mid-lane who hasn’t even practiced with the team once in the finals? A 100% win rate, a grand slam is right in front of our eyes!”*

The coaching staff couldn't bring themselves to say anything more and just read the room.

The front office leader raised his voice once again.

<br>

*“Are you not thinking about the sponsor penalties and the club’s value? Especially when even a Cheongang VIP is coming to watch live, our star player is absent on the day of the match? Do you want to see the club shut its doors or something?! Tens of billions are on the line. If we ruin today’s finals, the club goes down with it!”*

<br>

Everyone was talking about ‘me’. But no one was looking at ‘me’ as they spoke.

‘I’ barely managed to part my lips.

<br>

*“...Excuse me.”*

<br>

All gazes instantly turned toward ‘me’. They were eyes filled with a certain expectation.

A suffocating silence hung in the air for a moment.

‘I’ said the words they did not want to hear.

<br>

*“Um… I’m going to use the restroom.”*

<br>

The front team leader waved a hand irritably, and the director sighed and said,

<br>

*“Ha… Fine. There’s not much time left until the match, so make sure you’re back on time.”*

*“I’m sorry. I’ll be right back.”*

<br>

‘I’ opened the waiting room door and slipped outside.

*Click*. As soon as the door closed, the sound of people’s voices was muffled by a layer.

However, it wasn’t to the extent that ‘I’ couldn’t still hear them talking about me.

<br>

*“Isn’t the player’s own will the most important thing…?”*

<br>

‘I’ fled the waiting room as if running away.

Instead of heading to the restroom, ‘I’ headed to the emergency stairs.

I closed the heavy iron door and crouched down on the cold stairs.

After sitting there for a while, I took out the cell phone in my pocket with my right hand.

When I turned on the screen, a flood of messages had arrived. They were all messages from my teammates.

While the coaching staff and the club were discussing ‘my’ participation, the other teammates had been instructed to stay away.

It seemed everyone had sent messages out of frustration.

I immediately stepped backward.

The moment my foot reached back, I tumbled down. It was only natural, considering these were stairs and not flat ground.

All the effort of sprinting up was almost wasted as I nearly plummeted to the first floor, but after flailing frantically, I was lucky enough to stop halfway down.

Practically sprawled flat across the stairs, I caught my breath.

I couldn't see well.

It wasn't that my eyes were injured; my vision just seemed to have gone dark for a moment.

Breathing was difficult, so I opened my mouth to gasp for air, only to dry heave.

After staying curled up for a while, only after considerable time had passed did I slowly raise my head.

*'I have to go up.'*

I couldn't bring my body to stand.

Trying to steady my trembling frame, I thought.

Did I regret the choice I made back then?

If someone were to ask me, I couldn't say no.

If I went back to that time, would I make a different choice?

However, to this question, I could answer clearly.

That I would make the exact same choice.

I would agonize over it again and again, but in that moment, there was simply no helping it.

Because it was the only way to protect my fans from ridicule and blame, and the only way to repay the affection they had poured into me.

I had always wanted to be a player they could be proud of.

I wanted my fans to be able to speak proudly when they talked about me. I didn't want to create moments where they would be saddened by my defeat.

So even if I were to turn back time, I would still choose the match.

I suddenly turned my head to the side.

Dozens of cameras that had been in the cooking studio were still following me.

I could feel the gazes of the lenses recording my every reaction.

Surrounded by cameras, I looked up at the stairs continuing endlessly upward.

I also saw the shimmering holographic screens, anchored like gateways that refused to disappear.

To go up, I would have to face that thing from earlier once again.

I could only ascend if I didn't run away.

*'Do I really have to go up?'*

The thought crossed my mind that it might be fine to just stay here.

As I lay flat and still, the voice was heard once more.

This time, it was a little clearer.

After hesitating for a long time, I forced my body up. And slowly, I climbed the stairs.

Taking a deep breath, I faced the holographic screen, but.

"Ugh...!"

I tumbled down the stairs just the same.

Similar events repeated a few times.

However, I clenched my teeth and climbed the stairs. 

I wondered who was calling out to me. I wanted to know if someone was genuinely waiting.

For reasons as trivial as that, I headed upward.

Only after memorizing every single breath of that day's memories without missing a single one was I able to go up.

My injured leg made climbing the stairs a little more difficult.

Because of his limp, his speed was noticeably slow.

As he climbed the stairs, tiny screen-like insects repeatedly appeared and vanished beside him.

He tried not to look, but the text inevitably caught his eye.
