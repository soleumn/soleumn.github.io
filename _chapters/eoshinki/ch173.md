<br>Plenty of money was what made a player’s heart feel rich.

The same went for Trials.

The more money you had, the more ways you could approach a challenge.

The Young god had caused a major disaster, but it had already happened. All I could do now was make the most of it.

The Young god was just as excited as I was at the sight of the gold moving before our eyes.

As I shoved him back in—he was making a fuss, trying to burst outside—I pointed my camera at the monsters.

Then, belatedly, I noticed someone staring at me.

“……”

Cecilia was looking right at me, Pulse Gun in hand.

She’d been off in the distance, tinkering with the circuitry to operate the AI kiosk.

But I’d gotten so excited that I’d shouted for the captains.

Loud enough for Cecilia to hear.

She hopped from display case to display case like they were stepping-stones, making her way over to me.

Holding a light in one hand and a Pulse Gun in the other, she asked, “You’re filming, right?”

Then, without another word, she set up the lighting.

For now, I decided to focus on the gold right in front of me.

The captains were already moving with practiced ease, having been through Trials with me several times.

Mo Haein used the Black Moon Sword to fish a slate out from among the monsters, while Kwak Hanmuk had already picked one he liked and was pointing a boom mic at it.

“Hyung, let’s film this one first.”

The way ‘hyung’ slipped out of Kwak Hanmuk’s mouth as he gestured at the writhing tentacles with the boom mic sounded perfectly natural.

He often called me that jokingly, so it didn’t feel all that strange to me, either.

I was just about to start filming the tentacle monster Kwak Hanmuk had chosen when—

“…!”

The display case I was standing on shook violently.

I realized we had a slight problem.

The monsters the Young god had summoned were intensely interested in me.

Just not in a good way.

A greedy hunger emanated from the monsters creeping toward us.

They wanted to tear me apart and eat me.

Every monster swarming through the enormous convenience store sent its gloomy presence toward me.

Just as a tentacle came surging up the display case, about to wrap around my ankle—

*CRASH!*

A violent collision rocked the store.

I stared down in shock.

Beneath me, suspended in midair with prayer beads wrapped around my waist, the Black Moon Sword had split the display case in two.

Beside it, a tentacle writhed in pain after being struck by the beam of a Pulse Gun.

“Whoa, shit. I almost got a black eye.”

Kwak Hanmuk checked that I was safe with some colorful language.

Mo Haein, gripping the Black Moon Sword, glanced up at Cecilia.

The two women had attacked the tentacle at the same time. For a moment, they stared at each other.

Cecilia raised her Pulse Gun, ready to fire at the tentacle again.

Clutching my camera, I hurriedly shouted, “Don’t kill them!”

In my rush, I’d started speaking informally. I was ordering around the captains in a Young god’s voice.

“I’ll film while we restrain the monsters. Can you control the boom mic and the light with the prayer beads?”

“Hell yeah.”

“Good. Sis, keep the monsters away from me. Don’t kill them.”

“Don’t worry. Keep filming, Roasted Chestnut. I’ll make sure no one touches even a single strand of your hair.”

“And Hae—Haein, after you clap the slate, help her keep the monsters back.”

I looked at Mo Haein with some trepidation.

Seeing my expression, Mo Haein gave a faint smile and replied, “Got it.”

Thankfully, she didn’t use ‘that title.’ I felt relieved and steadied my camera.

The filming began in earnest.

With incredible control over the prayer beads, Kwak Hanmuk moved the boom mic, the light, and me all at once.

He deftly avoided the monsters charging at me while finding the best angles for the shoot.

As if I were dangling from a wire, I filmed overhead shots and captured the monsters’ movements with vivid clarity.

Meanwhile, Cecilia and Mo Haein moved busily to keep the monsters from disrupting the shoot.

But mishaps kept happening.

*Zzzzzt!*

Cecilia fired her beam gun at the same time Mo Haein swung the Black Moon Sword.

“……”

The two women’s eyes met. Mo Haein calmly stared at her. Cecilia said, completely unfazed, “My mistake.”

The beam from the Pulse Gun had narrowly missed Mo Haein.

After staring at Cecilia for a moment, Mo Haein swung the Black Moon Sword without looking away from her.

“Watch yourself, too.”

She knocked away a monster trying to wrap around us like thread, her tone indifferent.

“I feel like I’m about to make a mistake myself.”

The Young god inside me saw what was happening and burst out, shouting, “How dare you go after my subordinate…!”

As he acted like he was about to punish Cecilia on the spot, Kwak Hanmuk whipped the prayer beads around me and pulled me over to his side.

He gave me a halfhearted attempt at calming me down.

“Come on, hyung. It was an accident. Let’s let it slide this once.”

But despite his words, he seemed to be subtly egging the Young god on.

After finally calming the Young god down, I called out to Cecilia.

“Cecilia…”

“My Castañita. I’m sorry I startled you.”

She answered with a tender voice and warm eyes.

But I knew how jealous she could get.

I could see exactly how things would go: she’d never seriously hurt Kwak Hanmuk or Mo Haein, but she’d keep taking little swipes at them.

If Cecilia really did hurt my subordinates—

I wasn’t sure I could hold back.

In the end, I spoke to her as if I were admonishing her.

“I told you they’re my younger siblings.”

Cecilia’s expression hardened the moment she heard me. She looked at me and slowly moved her lips.

“…I’ll be careful.”

Then she turned her gaze away.

I wanted to comfort her, but there wasn’t time.

First, we had to finish filming as quickly as possible.

I kept my camera moving without a moment’s rest.

I knew what kind of edit would bring in the most money, so I captured only the shots I needed.

And at last, I filmed the final scene.

“That’s a wrap, so now—!”

Before I could tell everyone to pack up, it happened.

The moment I announced the end of filming, Cecilia pulled out another Pulse Gun and gripped one in each hand.

She swiftly changed the settings on the guns, then pulled the triggers.

*Brrr-r-r-r-r!*

A stream of beam rounds flew in every direction like machine-gun fire.

When the frenzied barrage finally ended, not a single monster was left alive in the convenience store.

Cecilia slowly lowered her guns and turned to me with a sweet smile.

“Roasted Chestnut.”

In a voice that was both tender and ominous, she whispered, “How about you and I have a little talk, just the two of us?”

I’d been planning to comfort her anyway.

But before I could say anything, Mo Haein called out to me.

Using ‘that title’…

“Oppa.”

The words that followed were spoken calmly, right in front of me as I stood there dazed, as if someone had plucked out my soul.

“Come talk with us. I can’t let you go alone.”

She swung the Black Moon Sword down, flicking the sticky fluid off it, and said without batting an eye, “That woman seems strange.”

The video caused an enormous sensation.

I’d sold it exclusively on the condition that the newspaper publish stills while its sister TV channel aired the footage. It earned figures I’d never even managed to hit in a game.

The circulation numbers and viewership ratings were astonishing, and the incentives came pouring in—enough to call it a windfall.

Even after paying for repairs to the convenience store we’d reduced to a ruin, I was still rolling in money.

And the captains and I had risen dramatically in status.

We’d gone straight from Labor Grade E to A.

“Roasted Chestnut, you did great.”

I looked at Cecilia, who was happier for me than if it had been her own achievement.

We’d gone into a separate room to talk after she came to the shelter.

We’d filmed incredible footage in the middle of the night at the convenience store, but Cecilia and the captains had reached a point of no return.

That day, I’d barely managed to stop Cecilia from quietly firing her Pulse Gun before we hurriedly returned to the shelter with the captains.

Since then, Cecilia and the captains had been ignoring each other by unspoken agreement.

Kwak Hanmuk would try to greet her or strike up a conversation with his usual easygoing charm, but Cecilia never really responded.

I wanted to somehow mediate between them, but it wasn’t easy.

Cecilia was so jealous that she couldn’t stand even the slightest sign of me putting my subordinates first.

And for some reason, whenever she acted like that, the captains would subtly fire back, making it even harder to intervene.

While I wondered what I could do, I sincerely thanked Cecilia.

“Thanks, Sis.”

It was entirely thanks to her work as a broker that we’d made it into the TV news as well as the papers.

Cecilia shrugged. “You’re the one who did well. From now on, you’ll have to be even more careful.”

Once people learned that a rookie stringer had covered this story, I’d attracted an incredible amount of attention overnight.

Everyone was eager to know who had brought in such a sensational story.

If they found out the rookie stringer was the Young god, covering stories in the future would become difficult.

I’d have to avoid attention as much as possible and move carefully.

I was hiding in the shelter Cecilia had arranged for me, but I had a feeling someone was starting to follow our trail.

Cecilia suggested we move again soon, then held out a newspaper.

“And read this.”

It was the Day News.

I’d expected an article about the Young god or the convenience store explosion, but the front page was dominated by a completely different story.

Before I even read the article, the enormous photograph caught my eye.

My lips parted on their own.

It was Hyper Eden’s Tower.

But, as always, it didn’t stand pristine and spotless.

Jagged black lines had been drawn all over the Tower. They were unmistakable cut marks.

I hurriedly read the article.

I skimmed over the parts about Hyper Eden’s citizens being horrified and worrying about Mad Messiah.

At the very end of the article, it mentioned the source. Another stringer who covered mysteries had tipped them off.

I looked at the photograph again.

*‘He must be lonely.’*

I couldn’t even imagine how lonely it must be, shut away by himself like that.

He was probably hurt that I was spending all my time with my other subordinates.

I wanted to rush straight to the Tower.

To stroke his head, tell him he’d done well waiting…

*‘Ah, seriously, stop.’*

I pushed the Young god’s thoughts away.

Of course, I desperately wanted to save Je Hyun-oh, too.

But as things stood, I had no way into the Tower unless Black Block opened it for me.

The Young god might have the power to open the Tower’s doors, but if I tried that, I could disappear.

Or my body might give out before I even got that far.

Still, it didn’t seem like Black Block was treating Je Hyun-oh badly.

He knew I’d never forgive him if he hurt Je Hyun-oh even a little.

What worried me most was that Je Hyun-oh couldn’t fly freely through the sky.

I couldn’t imagine how suffocating it must be for my subordinate to be trapped inside the Tower.

With my heart full of worry, I sent him a message.

I’d been sending Je Hyun-oh messages three times a day, morning, noon, and night.

They might not reach him inside the Tower, but sending them was the only thing that gave both me and the Young god even a little peace of mind.

As I sent the message and stared at the newspaper again, my expression clouded, and Cecilia called my name.

“Roasted Chestnut.”

“…Yeah?”

“You’re a god. Why do you love and care for your subordinates so much?”

She took the newspaper from me as she spoke.

“Honestly, that sort of thing… isn’t very godlike. Considering what lies ahead, you’d be better off not getting so attached to your subordinates.”

She wasn’t speaking out of simple jealousy toward the captains.

Cecilia was giving me that advice because she was genuinely worried about me.

I thought I understood how she felt.

Even now, I was taking reckless steps because I wanted to save my subordinate.

But no matter what I said, I wouldn’t be able to convince Cecilia.

So I just smiled and replied, “I don’t really know, either.”

“……”

Cecilia looked at me with an expression that was somehow pleading.

She seemed to want to convince me, but after staring at me for a long while, she let out a long breath.

“Have you decided what you’ll cover next?”

“Not yet.”

That convenience store video had let me skip several stages at once.

I’d made it onto TV right away, so the next quest would be much more difficult.

I was thinking back over the reporting quests I’d been given under the mystery theme when Cecilia handed me an envelope made of fine paper.

It was a decidedly analog object, out of place in a cyberpunk world.

“Open it.”

I took the envelope and opened it, a little dazed. There was an invitation inside.

The moment I read the first line, I looked up at Cecilia.

She watched me with an expression mixed with tension, fear, and a little anticipation.

“Do you think you can cover it?”

At the same moment, a quest window appeared.
