<br>When I played games, I often cleared them without a single death—or even a single hit.

Yet here I was, losing a heart right at the start for no reason.

This had never happened to me in any game.

Of course, this was a Trial, but it still felt terrible.

After respawning, the first thing I did was check on the Pudding Slime.

"Roasted Chestnut. Are you hurt?"

He knew I was fine, but he asked anyway.

I think I understood what he meant.* 'Punish this arrogant little creature.'*

After confirming Dominic was safe, I looked up.

Everyone was staring at the useless loser who had fallen out of the stage the moment he entered.

Won Taebeom asked, his face grim.

"Trainee Han Goyo. What's the meaning of this?"

The Trial usually had people within a certain range enter in a random order.

Someone who had entered before me had enough time to target me and push me off.

There were thirteen trainees in total, including myself.

At this point, I couldn't pinpoint the culprit.

Demanding to know who pushed me here wouldn't end well.

The perpetrator had likely acted with that very outcome in mind.

For now, it was best to just take the blame and observe the situation.

There was still a very slim chance it was an actual mistake, so I planned to let it slide once.

"I apologize. I was careless."

But even after my answer, Won Taebeom didn't back down easily.

As an instructor, he must have known that losing hearts in Hopdpop didn't cause any damage, yet he still reacted that way.

*'It must be the bond we forged in The Cell X,'* I thought. Recalling the peanut butter and jelly sandwich Won Taebeom had shared with me, I wore a reassuring smile.

He finally managed to tear his gaze away from me.

"...Be careful, Trainee Han."

After offering his warning, Won Taebeom briefly scanned the recruits who had entered, counted their numbers, and then commanded, "Everyone, check your quest."

The upbeat, chaotic background music mingled with his words. It was a beat blending bouncy synth-pop and chiptune sounds with a catchy brass section and a funky bassline.

The playful, beeping dissonances scattered throughout the track added a whimsical touch.

As I listened to the music, which naturally lifted my spirits, I finally read the Main Quest.

...I had loathed entering PTB, but Hopdpop was on a whole other level of contempt.

The System Window didn't even demand a True Ending this time. Instead, it spat out a curse, telling me to do whatever I wanted.

Staring at the screen, I knew I had to enter the next Trial with at least three stars.

Even worse, the penalty for failure wasn't just a massive drop in recognition—it threatened to send me all the way back to 0%. I'd only just started earning Star Fragments; losing my access to the Galactic Trading Post was out of the question.

As I carefully read through the quest window, I recalled the theory lesson Mo Haein had taught me.

I'd learned something new yesterday: the quest windows for other Adaptees were completely different from mine. Theirs never included detailed explanations, and even the single-line descriptions were far more concise.

For example, if another Adaptee were to receive this Main Quest window in Hopdpop, it would probably look something like this:

<div class="system-window" style="text-align: center;">
  <div class="system-content">
    <strong>◆ &lt;Hop Drop Pop> Main Quest: Finish the course.</strong>
  </div>
</div>

The ambiguity of the quest window was one of the main reasons why Adaptees found it so difficult to clear the Trials.

The system was providing me with relatively detailed additional explanations.

I wondered if this was perhaps the difference between a god and a human.

"All recruits, change your outfit colors and equip at least one accessory item. This is to ensure you can be distinguished from one another."

Hopdpop basically provided a variety of accessory items.

New items were unlocked as the number of plays accumulated or whenever someone finished in first place. In addition to accessories, there were also several outfit skins.

Won Taebeom, who had already entered Hopdpop multiple times, was wearing an outfit skin to distinguish himself from the recruits.

He wore a gray tracksuit set that suited him perfectly.

At the instructor's command, the recruits accessed their wardrobes.

But the reaction was lukewarm. It seemed to be because the Hopdpop accessories all had a cute, frilly vibe.

The recruits' faces soured, as if they had expected something like tracksuits.

*'But they're so cute...'*

As I was thinking to myself, Won Taebeom spoke with an impassive expression.

"Hopdpop items are simple cosmetic accessories with no additional abilities, but in other Trials, a single item can mean the difference between life and death. Keep in mind that this is a practical training exercise."

His stern voice, stating the facts so bluntly, seemed to quell the recruits' dissatisfaction.

When I opened my wardrobe, a full-length mirror appeared in the air along with a list of items.

The Pudding Slime reflected in the mirror was the first thing that caught my eye.

He looked... so empty.

I glanced around to make sure no one was watching, then leaned in and whispered.

"Dominic."

"Yes, Roasted Chestnut?"

"Can you... wear accessory items?"

"Hmm..."

It didn't seem like Dominic had officially joined the party, but judging by his reaction, he could still equip items.

I knew the terrible thing that lurked beneath that squishy exterior.

*'Still, it's better to be cute at first glance.'*

It would be less scary that way.

So, I recommended the Towel Sheep Horns accessory to Dominic. It was a basic Hopdpop item, and one of my personal favorites.

After some earnest persuasion, I finally convinced the reluctant Pudding Slime to equip it.

The moment the Pudding Slime, now sporting sheep horns, looked at me...

All my past memories vanished, replaced by an overwhelming surge of affection.

"Dominic... you look... so cool...!"

As I spoke with awe, Dominic wiggled his squishy body left and right a couple of times for me.

It felt a bit like he was playing with a child, but I was satisfied since I'd achieved my goal.

After finishing Dominic's makeover, I sank into deep thought about which accessories to wear.

Hopdpop's default outfit consisted of a short-sleeved anorak, shorts, long socks, and round sneakers.

Just as I was about to pick some plain accessories that would go well with my current outfit...

These weren't items I had unlocked in the Archive Game.

All the completely new items I'd never seen before bore names related to the Trials I had cleared.

I examined the items one by one, glancing around the room. The recruits were only wearing basic accessories. It seemed I was the only one who had received new items in my wardrobe.

The previous Trials were affecting Hopdpop.

I felt a jolt of shock at discovering yet another connection between the Trials, but at the same time, I felt a strange sense of nostalgia.

Standing before the full-length mirror, I tried on the items one by one.

I chuckled to myself as memories flooded back, trying on different items one by one.

Even after quickly equipping and removing them several times, I still couldn't decide. Everything looked so good that I was torn.

To avoid standing out, I knew wearing basic items would be best. But I couldn't resist the temptation and quietly pulled out one of the newly added items.

<div class="system-window" style="text-align: center;">
  <div class="system-content">
    <strong>Orca Plushie Headband: With an Orca, anywhere is as blue as the ocean!</strong>
  </div>
</div>

Since everything else seemed too flashy, I decided to stick with the basic outfit and only chose the most understated accessory—the Orca headband.

I set the outfit's colors to white and black, just like an Orca.

The basic outfit allowed for color changes and even adding a few stripes. I added a thick white stripe down the center of the black top, and it gave off a decent Orca vibe.

I thought Orca would love to see me like this.

After finishing my outfit, I closed the wardrobe.

And then I felt a little flustered.

"...?"

Because all the recruits and Won Taebeom were staring at me again.

Their faces were all filled with shock.

I wondered why they were acting like that, but then I realized.

*'Could they have seen me equipping all those items?'*

It seemed like the others had also seen me trying on all the items one by one.

Since I had put on all sorts of flashy items by myself, it was no wonder they were surprised...

I glanced at Won Taebeom.

His eyes were shaking, as if he were also greatly surprised.

It seemed to be because he knew, from his extensive experience in Hopdpop, that the items I possessed were simply unbelievable.

*'Still, he seems way too surprised.'*

He'd already entered both The Cell X and the Trial with me, so he should know I'm prone to causing all sorts of trouble inside.

Just as I was starting to feel a little awkward under his piercing gaze, Won Taebeom finally looked away and brought the situation under control.

"All recruits, focus."

The recruits, who had been so distracted by staring at me that they'd forgotten to equip their items, scrambled to get ready.

Once Won Taebeom confirmed everyone was prepared, he announced, "As stated in the previous briefing, today's practical exercise is designed to train your response when entering a Trial without a specific strategy. From this moment until the completion of Hopdpop, the Instructor will not intervene under any circumstances."

He pressed the macaron-shaped start button beside him, signaling the beginning.

"Starting now."

At his words, a cheer erupted, jellybean confetti exploded, and the castle gates swung open.

Beyond the sturdy chocolate-bar gates, the first obstacle appeared: a suspension bridge made of wafer cookies.

To enter the castle, they had to cross this long, wafer bridge.

As the recruits rushed across, the wafers crunched and shattered under their feet.

I brought up the rear, pretending to run with the rest.

Thirteen recruits were now on the bridge. Just as the leader had crossed about two-thirds of the way...

*Riiip.*

Something tore.

I quickly grabbed the left rope and began counting the seconds in my head.

Then, I jumped upward at the exact right moment.

Simultaneously, the right rope holding the suspension bridge snapped.

The bridge tilted sharply to one side, leaving the wafer board dangling from the remaining rope. The recruits who had been crossing it screamed as they slipped off.

I stood on the narrow edge of the now-vertical wafer board, watching them fall.

The recruits' eyes widened in shock the moment they saw me.

"What?! How are you—!"

*Splash!*

But before they could finish their sentence, they plunged into the blue carbonated ade river and vanished.

It takes a little while to respawn, and the more you die, the longer the respawn time becomes.

Moreover, without any checkpoints, you had to start all over again from the very beginning. This meant that losing hearts naturally pushed your rank further back.

The key to a fast finish was simply to avoid dying as much as possible.

But completing Hopdpop was no easy feat.

Even if you played well, others would still troll.

Right nearby, a recruit was desperately clinging to a wafer board, trying not to fall. But another recruit grabbed him, and they both plummeted together.

"Trainee Han Goyo!"

I looked down diagonally.

There, a recruit wearing a banana headband was holding on by jamming his hands into the fragile wafer.

At this rate, he was bound to fall soon.

As soon as our eyes met, Banana shouted.

"Help me!"

I turned my gaze back to the path ahead.

Including myself, there were six recruits still on the planks. Four of them, excluding Banana and me, were struggling to move forward, clinging to a single rope.

Everyone was supposed to fall here the first time they played Hopdpop. I had almost fallen myself during my first run.

*'How did they manage to grab the rope with only their reflexes, not even knowing the floor would collapse?'*

I needed to observe them a bit longer.

Just as I was about to keep walking, a furious shout rang out.

"Hey! Why aren't you helping? Fuck, grab me already!!"

Banana's language turned foul the moment things didn't go his way.

He was the recruit who had been glaring at me since I entered the classroom—one of the candidates who might have pushed me.

I stared calmly at Banana, who was now spewing curses, and said, "I'll help you."

A look of momentary confusion crossed Banana's face.

I walked leisurely toward him, reaching the wafer board he was clutching.

*CRUNCH!*

I stomped down on the wafer board. A web of cracks spread across its surface.

"You crazy bastard! What the hell are you—!"

Ignoring his curses, I kicked the board again. The brittle wafer shattered into pieces.

I offered a kind smile to Banana, who was screaming in fury.

"The fastest way to recover from a total failure is to reset."
