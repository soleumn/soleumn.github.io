The fast food restaurant stage featured a Hidden Monster that absolutely had to be photographed. This was the kind of monster that would land on the front page of the newspaper and earn me a fortune.

I headed out into the city at night with the Special Team.

Instead of goggles, I wore a white half-mask—a last-minute purchase I'd made while sending the recorder to Cecilia. Though it only covered half my face, pulling my hood low helped me avoid too much attention.

The citizens of Hyper Eden dressed so eccentrically that my half-mask didn't even draw a second glance.

I walked the night streets, bathed in the glow of neon signs and billboards.

The Tower at the city's center was visible between the buildings.

The Tower, a landmark that could be seen from anywhere in the city, was the residence of its master, "Mad Messiah."

Soaring high, the Tower stood clean and aloof, unlike the other buildings plastered with holographic advertisements.

Its exterior was so smooth that not a single rivet or seam between metal plates could be found, whatever material it was made of.

The Tower, which looked as if it had been carved from a single, massive piece of metal, had no entrance.

Nor did anyone enter or leave.

Even the most scoop-hungry Stringers didn't dare go near the Tower, and I had never set foot inside it myself.

*'Maybe a god lives in there.'*

The citizens revered the Mad Messiah as a god. When I played the game, I had assumed it was a metaphorical jab at the Mega Corporation, but now I was realizing it was a literal statement of fact.

"Roasted Chestnut."

Dominic called my name as I kept my gaze fixed on the Tower.

"Do you want to know what's inside?"

I did. I wanted to uncover the truth I'd never learned in the game.

But I didn't want to solve this simply by relying on Dominic's power. I had a promise to keep with Cecilia regarding the Tower...

"I can investigate this for you, so just say the word if you wish," Dominic offered gently.

I merely nodded in response.

His offer brought to mind the Administrator of the Galactic Trading Post's warning and the price Sa Gongyeon had mentioned for the information.

I had entered the First Stage only to register my presence on the map before immediately leaving. Now, I had arrived safely at the Second Stage: a fast-food joint in the slums.

Even from the outside, the closed restaurant exuded a sense of immense history and tradition. It seemed Hyper Eden's cutting-edge technology had completely missed the mark here. The place stood in stark contrast to the dazzling skyscrapers visible just a turn of the head away.

On the wall, a billboard flickered incessantly despite the store being closed, displaying an ad for hamburgers.

"There is an event where part of the building collapses."

"...Oh."

Kwak Hanmuk's expression also soured.

I glanced at Je Hyun-oh, but he seemed completely uninterested in the fast-food restaurant.

In fact, he looked like he'd been observing the Tower for a while now.

Come to think of it, couldn't Je Hyun-oh just fly to the top of the Tower?

I guided the Special Team to the back of the fast-food restaurant.

The employee entrance was locked with a crude analog device and opened easily.

We had finally entered the Second Stage, but...

"...!"

What appeared before my eyes was no decrepit fast-food joint.

A completely unfamiliar space filled my vision.

Amidst a chaotic jumble of 8-bit sound effects, dozens of classic arcade game machines stood lined up in rows.

It was an arcade.

Setting aside the fact that it wasn't a fast-food restaurant, the sight was completely out of place in a cyberpunk world.

As I stared at the pixelated graphics on the screens, I suddenly realized how quiet it was.

There was no one around.

Not only was the Special Team gone, but even Dominic had vanished.

I was all alone in the arcade.

Just as panic set in, a System Window appeared.

It wasn't a notification for the Second Stage, but an Event Quest window.

The pixelated Mo Haein was a sight I knew all too well.

I had seen her countless times in the Archive game.
