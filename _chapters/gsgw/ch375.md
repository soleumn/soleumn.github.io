<br>And so, a listless, miserable morning dawned.

<br>

*— Morning has broken. All workers, please prepare for today's assignment~*

<br>

I woke up inside the cramped worker break room, my body still feeling like absolute trash, sharing the space with a corpse. I had held onto a sliver of hope that I might wake up back in my own bed at home, but it was a completely futile expectation.

"Ha..."

Of course it was impossible. 

*‘There's no way this ghost story world would be that soft on me.’*

Dry-washing my face, I got up from the worn-out, torn mattress.

My body, which had run on nothing but chocolate, began to ache with joint pain, and my head felt dizzy. Even in the midst of this, I deliberately tried my best not to look toward the end of the wall where the corpse lay staring blankly at the air.

"Ha."

I stayed as far away from it as possible, lying down while constantly brainwashing myself that it was just a mannequin. Even though my physical condition was totally shot and I had essentially slept like I was dead...

*‘I guess having a trash physical condition actually helps in a way like this.’*

<br>

*— Morning roll call will be conducted in 10 minutes. Please get rich today too~*

<br>

The morning broadcast sounded exactly like a facility for illegal labor run by loan sharks. In a very literal sense, it felt like I was going to lose my mind.

They expect me to enter a ghost story in this state? The dread I felt was entirely different in nature from back when I was part of the Security Team.

*‘The days when I was a full-time employee, receiving items dropped from that mysterious black box, feel like a literal dream...’*

Yet, even within this garbage physical state, my mind was still sharp and functional. 

And my plan was perfectly clear.

"...Alright."

Taking a deep breath, I staggered over and stood near the doorway.

<br>

*BEEEEEEP!*

<br>

A short moment later, the door slid open. 

I immediately surveyed the hallway outside. 

From every single door lining the corridor of the worker break rooms, people dressed in shabby uniforms were streaming out.

...Just as written in the diary, their eyes looked strange, and their faces were hollow.

<br>

**<center>​[Workers in the Z800s and 900s, begin moving. Do not delay your pace.]</center>**

<br>

The unbothered voice of an employee instructed over the announcement system, and the workers began moving as if they were entirely used to it.

"Yikes."

Following them, I gritted my teeth and forced my feet forward. 

However, as I closed the gap and practically joined the crowd, several workers openly furrowed their brows, actively steering clear of me, or throwing curses my way the moment our eyes met.

"Fuck, what are you glaring at?"

There was even someone who tried to push me away with a kick. 

Though it was intercepted and stopped by someone else in a different sense.

"Hey, why are you touching something so filthy?" 

"Fuck, if that creeps onto me, I’m leading with my foot next time." 

"......"

No... this is insane. 

*‘I guess it's because my number is at the very end of the line.’*

Let's just let it slide for now. I was at a point where I didn't even have the spare calories required just to walk…

<br>

*‘Man.’*

Even in this abysmal environment and among a group of mere 'workers,' a stark hierarchical structure could be felt. Or perhaps, it was precisely because the labor environment was such a absolute gutter that it manifested this way.

*‘For now, let’s just lay low and not draw attention.’*

I had to keep in mind that today was meant to be an exploratory phase. Silently, I followed at the very rear of the procession.

After walking a bit further, the end of the hallway came into view. 

The pathway leading toward the 'Off-Duty Zone' remained firmly shut—the very place where the completely dried-up ritual altar stood when I first opened my eyes. 

Instead, the side door next to it was wide open, revealing a completely new space.

"...!"

It was a spacious indoor area partitioned into three sections, looking exactly like a livestock pen.

<br>

<div style="border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave, rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.1)); border-radius: 12px; padding: 16px; text-align: center; margin: 20px 0;">
  <strong>Worker Waiting Area</strong>
</div>

<br>

While the atmosphere of the shabby, rusted facility remained unchanged, a clean, standardized signpost of the exact type used by Daydream Co., Ltd. was affixed above. 

Standing in each partitioned stall were 'employees' dressed in outfits that resembled full-body hazmat suits.

*‘Personnel.’*

It was my first time seeing people here who weren't classified as workers. The logo and nameplates printed on their uniforms... 

Were incredibly familiar to me.

*‘They're Security Team employees.’*

The Daydream Security guard nameplates. The moment they entered my vision, it felt as though my brain's processing speed reflexively kicked into overdrive.

*‘This really is a facility operating directly under Daydream.’*

The moment reality truly sank in, a brief shudder that felt strangely close to a twisted sense of relief passed through my chest.

<br>

**<center>[Yes. Please receive your work assignments from the person in charge.]</center>**

<br>

The security guards holding clipboards approached the crowds packed into the stalls, looking like supervisors at a casual day-labor market, and began checking off personnel starting from the earliest numbers.

I stood quietly in the corner where the Z-groups were gathering, catching glimpses sideways to assess the crowd of workers.

Predictably... the ones who looked to be in relatively better condition belonged to the X-group.

"Number 491? Oh, that guy lost his mind, I dunno. He won't come out of his room." 

"Did you hear that? Fuck, he went crazy, that's hilarious." 

"Losing your mind just because you're scared of a ghost is a skill issue~"

As I listened to two guys from Group X snickering while discussing someone from Group Y who failed to show up for roll call, their faces struck a chord of familiarity.

*‘They're the ones from when I first opened my eyes.’*

The very bastards who had beaten me and poured water over my head in front of the ritual altar. 

Let's see... checking the numbers printed on the red stickers stuck to their bodies...

*‘X145, X141.’*

Their numbers were definitely way ahead of my Z999. 

Considering what was written in the diary... it meant they held numbers that placed them on the relatively luckier side of this workspace.

<br>

*<span style="color: var(--texto-secundario);">—Seems X, Y, Z are ranks for the workers... I went to X. Looking roughly at the rooms, it seems better to go in alphabetical order—the smaller the letter rank, the better.</span>*

<br>

*‘So they're flexing because they got assigned a decent tier here...’*

If they cross paths with the wrong anomaly just once, they'll end up completely ruined in an instant, just like the guy left in my room.

For a brief split second, I almost felt a wave of pity for these people who had absolutely no idea they were currently running barefoot through a literal minefield of supernatural horrors—but I quickly snapped out of it. My own situation as Z999 was obviously the one that was vastly worse...

And besides, anyone who beats a person up and pours water over their face is clearly not normal. Let's move on.

At any rate, I had successfully verified the overall state of the workers. 

*‘...It seems the further back you go in the alphabet, and the higher the digit of your number, the fewer people are assigned per room.’*

And if I looked closely, individuals whose states were visibly 'corrupted' or off began to catch my eye one by one...

For instance, there was a person holding Rummikub tiles tightly in both hands, frantically rearranging them like a maniac; someone muttering entirely to themselves; a person whose hair was half-charred to a crisp; and right next to me, a person standing completely barefoot with a vacant, hollow expression.

*‘...Contamination symptoms.’*

Were they even receiving any proper treatment? There was no way to know. 

But my chance to test the waters arrived sooner than expected.

"Yes... Everyone from our room, all three of us are here."

The roll call for the front groups wrapped up, and a security guard finally stepped up right in front of me.

"......"

I quietly observed, hoping to spot any identifiable physical features, but these branch security employees were eerily featureless. 

Because their entire bodies were covered under identical hazmat suits, every single one of them looked exactly the same.

*‘They feel... mass-produced.’*

Instead of the bizarre, highly volatile, and dangerous aura that emanated from the headquarters' Security Team, this felt more like the unsettling dissonance of looking at a completely sterile, standardized, and unfeeling collective.

Looking toward the head portion of the hazmat suit, I forced my mouth open.

"...Hello."

No response came back. 

The security guard holding the clipboard simply glanced at my number and indifferently marked a circle (O) on the slot for Worker Z999.

Then, moving to the slot right above mine, which still had the entry for 'Worker Z993' remaining, he callously marked a cross (X) over it.

Good. Right now.

"That person... I think he's dead..."

The guard's pen stopped.

"He keeps... acting like that, shouldn't he be sent to somewhere like the infirmary...?"

I deliberately let my voice sound clumsy and broken, trembling slightly as if I were just a terrified regular civilian babbling out of sheer fear.

A brief, stark silence hung in the air.

Then, the security employee raised his hand... 

and silently pointed toward the back of the waiting area.

"……"

As I turned my head, I saw a worker notice posted on the wall.

<br>

<div style="border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave, rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.1)); border-radius: 12px; padding: 16px; text-align: center; margin: 20px 0;">
  Additional purchases of daily necessities and services can be made <u>voluntarily</u>.<br><br>
  (CASH ONLY, NO CARDS)
</div>

<br>

What kind of insane nonsense is this? 

*‘Don't tell me... even using the infirmary is a service the worker has to purchase out of their own pocket?’*

Save me, seriously... 

What a capitalistic hellscape. The working environment itself was nothing short of a ghost story in its own right.

However, asking a question like, "Do I happen to have any cash on me too?" would undoubtedly look incredibly suspicious coming from someone who had supposedly spent over two weeks here, so I stopped myself right there. 

Surely, an opportunity to figure it out would present itself naturally later.

"Ah, I understand……"

I muttered, and with the security employee passing right by me with his clipboard, the morning roll call for the workers came to an end.

And then, the highly anticipated 'work' began.

<br>

**<center>[Groups X, Y, and Z in the 990s will move together.]</center>**

<br>

The workers, numbering around a dozen including myself, walked somewhere with our eyes covered by blindfolds, and after being loaded into a vehicle of transportation, we were moved once again.

<br>

*Whirrrrrr—*

<br>

The creaking noises and the engine exhaust made it difficult to discern exactly where or how we were moving. 

What was certain was that after about 5 to 10 minutes had elapsed, we arrived somewhere.

<br>

We had arrived.

<br>

**<center>[Yes. Arrival has been confirmed.]</center>**

<br>

**<center>[Workers, remove your blindfolds and prepare to disembark.]</center>**

<br>

Soon, as I removed my blindfold, a rusted space revealed itself.

Only then could I see that I was riding on something resembling a minecart placed on old rails. 

*‘Hmm.’*

The interior brought to mind a closed mine shaft or a confinement facility, much like the break area, but the humidity was abnormally high, causing water droplets to fall onto the floor.

<br>

*Drip.*

<br>

A dismal and worn-out feeling. 

*‘If you isolate people in a place like this, wouldn’t even a safe ghost story end up twisting into something malicious...?’*

The concept for this branch was certainly thorough. I wondered what kind of bastard had set it up this way.

<br>

**<center>[Workers, everyone disembark!]</center>**

<br>

At any rate, I stepped down in accordance with the same robotic announcement broadcast here.

<br>

**<center>[Security employees, please verify that guards are equipped.]</center>**

<br>

Before long, something resembling a thick armband, looking like a blood pressure cuff or a guard, was fastened onto my left forearm.

And it locked in place so that I couldn't remove it on my own.

*‘...Sigh.’*

Around me, I could hear the sound of the higher-numbered workers muttering unverified warding spells, and the sounds of the lower-numbered workers snicker-snacking among themselves.

Under the watchful eye of a security employee, I was made to stand in front of a door located in the middle of a hallway that was completely indistinguishable from any other outward appearance.

<br>

**<center>[Worker Z999, deploying.]</center>**

<br>

The iron door swung open with a harsh, screeching metallic creak.

And then, I was roughly shoved inside the door by a security employee. My powerless body was pushed inward without offering any resistance. 

*‘Wait.’* 

Just like this, without even a manual?

<br>

*Screeech, thud.*

<br>

But giving me no time to protest, the door slammed shut behind me. 

*‘Wow, please.’*

This is insane, seriously... 

I was locked in.

An instinctive surge of fear welled up from within. 

*‘From this moment on, I am experiencing a ghost story...’*

Tension reflexively gripped the back of my neck. No, perhaps it was because it had been far too long since I was last confined in a dark, enclosed space with something objectively terrifying that I found myself closing my eyes tightly.

Perhaps the only reason I didn't let out a scream was because I lacked even the energy to do so... 

*‘...Sigh.’*

Regardless, if I wanted to make it out of here alive, I had to execute the task properly.

After taking a deep breath, I opened my eyes and looked straight ahead. 

"……"

A pitch-black interior. 

In the unpaved isolation room, a concrete wall was visible next to the dirt floor.

Amidst a damp, suffocating smell of moisture, a single red lightbulb hung alone, casting a dim glow downward. That light eerily illuminated a worn-out chair. And sitting squarely on top of that chair was...

...A wooden doll.

<br>

*<span style="color: var(--texto-secundario);">—So far, the work I've been given is burning wooden dolls with a lighter every day.</span>*

<br>

Cold sweat trickled down my skin. 

*‘Oh, damn it.’*

This was the very ghost story that the deceased Worker Z993 had been assigned to handle.

<br>

*<span style="color: var(--texto-secundario);">— SorryPleaseSaveMeSorryPleaseSaveMeSorryPleaseSaveMeSorryPleaseSaveMeSorryPleaseSaveMeSorryPleaseSaveMeSorryPleaseSaveMeSorryPleaseSaveMeSorryPleaseSaveMeSorryPleaseSaveMe</span>*

<br>

As the final page of the diary flashed through my mind, the inside of my mouth began to completely dry up. 

Why did it have to be this one, of all things?

This was a supernatural anomaly where it was already proven that a single mistake meant death. The oil-stained wooden doll bore vivid burn marks across its entire body, and looking at it brought the contents of the diary back to mind, making my blood run cold.

*‘But what I thought while reading that diary was...’*

<br>

**<center>[Worker Z999.]</center>**

<br>

"...!"

I snapped my head up.

<br>

**<center>[Yes, you can hear me. Worker Z999. Now, try striking that wooden doll.]</center>**

<br>

"……"

An announcement voice came through, as if a speaker had been placed somewhere inside the room. 

It was the detached, unbothered voice of a researcher.

But... *‘Strike it?’*

<br>

*<span style="color: var(--texto-secundario);">—So far, the work I've been given is burning wooden dolls with a lighter every day.</span>*

<br>

It was a completely different instruction from what the previous person had done, an action that anyone could see was deeply unsettling. 

*‘Ha.’*

This wasn't a situation where I was voluntarily clearing a ghost story; rather, I was forced to carry out whatever tasks the researcher ordered me to do. 

And on top of that... an action that possessed an incredibly high probability of triggering an anomaly!

<br>

**<center>[Do not look back, and follow the instructions as directed.]</center>**

<br>

"...Excuse me."

No response came back. I simply stood frozen in place.

<br>

**<center>[You know full well that if you try to hold out and refuse to do it, we will leave you in there until you do. If you're late, you won't receive your daily pay, so why act like this?]</center>**

<br>

"……Researcher. Are you there……"

<br>

**<center>[Failing to implement the next instruction will result in penalty points.]</center>**

<br>

"……"

I turned my head. Toward the unsettling wooden doll sitting on top of the chair.

<br>

**<center>[Strike it.]</center>**

<br>

I took a step closer.

<br>

**<center>[Hurry up.]</center>**

<br>

And then, raising one hand, I struck down at the doll.
