<br>Surprisingly, the act of Daydream Corporation throwing dozens or hundreds of people into a single ghost story at the same time can be frequently found on the wiki.

Because it's entertaining.

In fact, I had even experienced it firsthand not long after I joined Daydream. It was a place where dozens of employees had died.

*‘The Mansion of the Blind…’*

My mouth still went dry just thinking about that place—a mansion hosting a bizarre exhibition where people had their facial features and limbs confiscated as an admission fee.

<br>

**<center>[Re-announcing. We will be entering a special night shift today.]</center>**

<br>

So, from the moment they mobilized and moved over a hundred workers in the evening, I had a rough idea. A PTSD alarm was flashing furiously inside my head.

*‘This is one hundred percent it.’*

They intend to drive all these workers into a single ghost story. And usually, exploration records like this have a survival rate cut in half. Because that makes it entertaining to read.

*‘Damn it.’*

I forced myself to relax my expression instead of gritting my teeth. Instead, I looked at Jin Nasol. 

She also seemed to have guessed the situation, packing her painkillers with sunken, calm eyes. As expected of an elite team member...

"The effect of this medicine is disgustingly weak. Are you sure this is all there is?"

"……Yes."

I don't know.

*‘Let's not trust her that much…’*

In this world of ghost stories, completely devoid of blood, tears, or common sense, it is safest to trust only myself. No, you can even be betrayed by yourself...

"Ah, fuck, we need to rest our bodies to work again tomorrow. What kind of bullshit is calling people out like this at night?"

"No, they said they'll pay double by adding a night shift allowance...!"

"R-Really?"

"Damn, as expected of a major corporation's generosity!"

Two million won in cash. At that magical phrase, the workers quickly stop resisting, though they continue to grumble.

<br>

**<center>[Yes, yes. Please put on your blindfolds.]</center>**

<br>

As I was loaded and carried away like cargo, I desperately tried to deduce the situation and narrow down the possibilities. 

What kind of ghost story could it be?

<br>

**<center>[Please step off.]</center>**

<br>

Soon, the mine cart stops, and we are guided toward a single door.

<br>

**<center>[Now, line up. Yes. You will stand in groups of five.]</center>**

<br>

A five-man cell, then.

*‘An awkward number.’*

Seeing them divide into random groups based on the order of the line ahead, I quickly calculated the timing and lined up around the middle. 

I hesitated on whether or not to separate from Deputy Jin Nasol, but figured that even in the worst-case scenario where only a few survive in a single group, Jin Nasol would likely cooperate up to a limit of two people, so we stood together.

*‘Usually, it's more advantageous to move together.’*

<br>

**<center>[Hmm... yes. Good.]</center>**

<br>

After the security guards checked the workers and signaled each other, the containment room eventually swung open.

*Creeeeak……*

"……."

"Isn't it... way too dark?"

The inside of the door was completely invisible. 

The place, heavily shrouded in darkness, almost felt like a total void.

"Hey, you should at least give us a flashlight if you want us to go in or not."

"And can this many people even fit in there?"

<br>

**<center>[Yes. It is plenty spacious inside. Once you enter, you'll be able to see everything you need to see.]</center>**

<br>

"But still……."

<br>

**<center>[Sigh... seriously.]</center>**

<br>

Irritation began to bleed into the researcher's previously indifferent voice.

<br>

**<center>[Then drop out of the operation. Drop out and take the penalty points.]</center>**

<br>

"……!"

<br>

**<center>[So either take the disadvantage or get in quickly. Can't you see you're wasting everyone's time right now? Do you want to keep everyone from sleeping tonight?]</center>**

<br>

"That……."

"Yeah, seriously, what's a grown man doing throwing a fit over the dark?"

"We just have to go in, go in."

As a few workers with earlier numbers chimed in and gave him dirty looks, the protesting worker fell silent. 

Even though it was a necessary request for their own sake, they instinctively walked on eggshells, wanting to avoid displeasing the researcher, who held all the power.

*‘Most of the workers... seem to think that the people who didn't return after a shift were either laid off or sent to the hospital.’*

They probably thought the rumors about workers dying were just hearsay, or if they were superstitious, they believed those people were possessed by ghosts because their spiritual energy was too weak. 

Combined with the delusion that since they themselves had been perfectly fine despite doing the exact same work, they would continue to be safe in the future.

*‘Complacent, yet realistic.’*

Conversely, on the faces of the Z900-series workers, I could read a stiff rigidity born of anxiety and tension, or signs that they were already half out of their minds…

<br>

**<center>[Receive your manuals and enter. Simultaneous entry for the same row.]</center>**

<br>

However, the entry had already begun. 

The workers at the very front stepped into the darkness one after another. 

And then, it was my line's turn.

"……."

The security guard handed a piece of paper to me, as I was closest. 

Was it the same person from the first day?

"Th-Thank you……."

I took it. 

The moment I took a step into the containment room door while holding the slightly coarse paper—

<br>

*Thud.*

<br>

The darkness swallowed us.

"……."

Strangely, we didn't bump into the team ahead of us. 

We slowly walked forward.

One step. Another step.

"No, just how far do we have to go in……?"

Just before the worker next to me could finish his sentence—

*Whoosh.*

The brief sound of wind brushed past, and then our vision cleared.

"……!"

What tickled my forehead was a mountain breeze. 

Before our eyes, the slope of a low mountain covered in dark green branches revealed itself.

"A-A mountain?"

"It was a door leading outside? No, but how is this even possible?"

"No, what kind of mountain is this……"

Deputy Jin Nasol jerked her chin toward the paper in my hand.

*‘Check it.’*

Even without being told, I was already unfolding the paper I had been given. 

If merely stepping into the containment room acted as the entry trigger, then this paper had the absolute highest probability of containing the escape hints.

The contents of the folded paper finally revealed themselves.

<br>

<mark>Collect <strong><s>4</s>3</strong> stamps and claim your prize.</mark>

<center><small>Let's visit the famous spots of Sagyeong Mountain in the sunless darkness to build our courage and fortitude, and discipline our minds.</small></center>

**<center>Fairy Rock – Sagyeong Villa – Osaek Mineral Spring</center>**

Information Desk in front of the 1st-floor entrance of the Training Center. 

<br>

"……A courage test?"

Seriously. 

Would it kill them to at least pretend it wasn’t a ghost story at first?

*‘Why does this have to be so hardcore…?’*

A ghost story about a courage test.

"Hey, let’s look at that together."

I readily held out the paper so that my fellow team members could see it clearly.

A line at the very bottom of the flyer—which looked old-fashioned, tacky, and a bit crudely made, as if slapped together in Photoshop—newly caught my eye.

<div style="text-align: right !important; margin: 20px; padding: 16px;">
   This is an ongoing experience program operated for organizations leasing our Shinmaru Training Center.
</div>

<div style="text-align: right !important; margin: 20px; padding: 16px;"">
   Available to participate at any time without registration.
</div>

At that moment.

"Shinmaru Training Center?"

"…!"

"Do you know this place?"

"Uh… isn't this the training center where all those kids died after that accident?"

What?

A sudden chill washed down my back, as if cold water had been poured over it. 

The worker, wearing an uncomfortable expression, rubbed his chin and spoke.

"No, I’m sure I heard about it. Right before I started working at this site… those students died in droves."

"……."

"They said some cult built that training center. Didn't they say they couldn't even find the bodies of the kids?"

A bleak silence fell over us.

"…But are we standing right there right now? Wow, seriously."

"No, first of all, let's go back and ask the PA system folks properly before moving on…"

With that, we turned around.

<br>

All we could see was a path winding through a dim, wild mountain. 

The door to the containment room was nowhere to be seen. 

We were stranded squarely in the middle of a wild mountain.

<br>

"……."

"Uh, what happened…?"

"No, the door we just came through… no way."

This won't do. 

It was obvious these people had never directly experienced such blatant, supernatural darkness before.

"Is it just too dark to see?"

"Probably, right? If not, then…"

"Ah, you must be right. Since we can't see properly…"

I quickly chimed in and agreed with them. 

If I didn't, everyone might descend into total panic right here and now.

*‘Need to find the escape route fast and get out.’*

My mind raced so fast it felt electric. 

First, I had to quickly deduce what kind of ghost story this was. Let’s see… a training center where a fatal accident occurred, a courage test…

*‘It's too cliché.’*

Even this time, the fragmentary conditions were common tropes that were perfect for a ghost story. It was hard to narrow it down because several representative ghost stories came to mind. Like the Wooden Doll incident, it could just be one of many various cases.

*‘I think I’d know instantly if I just experienced one definitive gimmick…’*

*Tap, tap.*

Deputy Jin Nasol tapped my back.

"Ditch those guys and let's move."

"Pardon?"

"The flyer doesn't mention anything about headcount. Let's go. Why are we dragging dead weight around for no reason?"

"Now, wait a moment."

In this terrifying mountain, moving as a group of five feels overwhelmingly safer than moving as a pair…!

*‘The more, the merrier—more people means it’s less scary!’*

Just as I was sweating and trying to manufacture a rational argument to say this out loud—

"Ah, damn it, I can't see a thing. They said we'd be able to see everything once we got inside, but it's pitch black…"

"Everyone."

One of the workers chuckled and pulled something out. It was…

"A flashlight!"

"…!"

"It's all about using your head."

The worker proudly held up a small flashlight, despite his tense face.

"Since it's a night shift, I snuck it in."

"Wow, this is why people need to be smart."

"Whew, thanks to you, we can walk comfortably. Keep it a secret from the company, okay? Yeah, of course!"

The faces of the workers brightened slightly at the return of peace of mind brought by light and an open field of vision.

True. If a mountain at midnight lights up, the fear drops by more than half.

However, this…

*‘…Did they really think Daydream wouldn't notice them smuggling that in?’*

No, they definitely knew. There are CCTV cameras everywhere; there's no way they wouldn't catch it.

If they knew, why didn't they stop it? 

Especially when they explicitly refused to hand out flashlights themselves.

<br>

*<span style="color: var(--texto-secundario);">—Once you enter, you'll be able to see everything you need to see.</span>*

<br>

That refusal clearly had an underlying intention. There must be a reason why the researcher didn't provide one…

"……."

Wait a minute.

"Now, let's turn it on quickly and…"

"Do not turn it on."

"Huh?"

"I said, do not turn on the flashlight."

"Geez, look at you lowering your voice all of a sudden."

"I guess he's the type to act tough when he's scared?"

Unfortunately, the workers who knew me from back when I was Z999 ignored my words and moved casually, snickering.

"Whatever. You're just scared you'll see a real ghost."

Damn it.

"Alright, there are no ghosts~"

As the worker pressed the switch on the flashlight…

<br>

*Smack!*

<br>

I swept my leg to knock him down and pinned him to the ground.

"Whoa, whoa!"

Overpowering his body to keep him down, I first struck the flashlight away.

"Is this bastard crazy?!"

"Fuck!"

The other workers rushed in to pull me off him.

*‘Damn it!’*

The flashlight rolled downward. Jin Nasol watched the situation unfold with one eyebrow raised, then began to approach. 

It looked like she was going to help restrain them, but—

I screamed.

"Close your eyes!"

It was already too late.

"Hey!"

The flashlight, which had already left the struggling worker's hand, was already switched on.

<br>

*Ting.*

<br>

The flashlight hit the ground, scattering light across the floor.

<br>

*Ting-ground-roll…*

<br>

Damn it.

"Close your eyes…!"

"Ugh,"

After yelling at the worker pinned beneath me, I pressed his face hard into the dirt and shut my eyes tight.

"What's wrong with him? Is he insane?"

"Why is he telling us not to look? It’s completely bright and fine."

"Exactly."

"Wow~ even though it's the middle of the night, it's really bright."

"We can see everything."

"Great."

"Great."

"Gre,"

"AAAAAAAAAAAAAAARGH.!"

**"Uhhhhhh uhhhhhh uhhhhhhhhhhhhhh yeah great great great aaaaaah great"**

"Wow……."

"……."

"……."

"It was in the mountains."

<br>

Silence.

<br>

"……."

"……."

Now, only the sound of the wind could be heard.

The hands that had been trying to tear me off a moment ago had completely vanished. 

A stifled voice leaked from beneath me.

"Y-You there."

"……."

"What… what just happened…?"

"Do not open your eyes."

Sigh.

"Deputy Jin. Are you there?"

"Did you think I wouldn't be?"

It seemed she had followed my instruction immediately.

*‘As expected of an elite…’*

The next moment, I stumbled to my feet. 

And slowly, I began to move my feet… walking down the trail.

Toward the brightness bleeding through my eyelids.

"……."

My legs shook. But I didn't stop. After pacing back and forth a few times over the spot where the light blazed brightest behind my eyelids—

*Thud.*

My foot kicked something.

"……."

I bent over, found the flashlight with a palm that was slick with cold sweat, and clicked the switch off.

<br>

*Click.*

<br>

Had darkness ever felt this welcoming?

"Phew."

Only after slipping the flashlight into my jumpsuit pocket did I slowly, very slowly, open my eyes.

<br>

The dim mountain path came into view. That was all.

"……You may open your eyes now."

Drenched in cold sweat, I turned my head. I saw the worker I had pinned down, and Deputy Jin Nasol, who had already picked up the flyer and was reading it again.

Two people. 

That, too, was all.

"…Seong-gil, I mean, where did the others go…?"

"I don't know."

I wiped away my cold sweat.

"I don't see them."

Yeah.

I remembered. 

*‘There’s no way I wouldn't know.’* 

This particular ghost story where you must never turn on a flashlight.

## [Let There Be No Light]

An urban legend appearing in the 〈<span class="wiki-red">Darkness Exploration Records</span>〉. Its identification code within <span class="wiki-red">Daydream Corporation</span> is Qterw-C-1010.

An urban legend that operates in the **<span class="wiki-red">exact opposite way</span>** of typical horror game gimmicks where you are attacked the moment the <span class="wiki-red">lights go out</span>.

Anyone trapped in this ghost story **will go missing if they turn on a <span class="wiki-red">light</span>**—be it <span class="wiki-red">candles, flashlights, or glow sticks</span>—during their exploration.

The most terrifying part is that not only the person who turned on the light goes missing, but **<span class="wiki-red">everyone who sees what is illuminated by the light goes missing as well.</span>**

It remains unclear what exactly the explorers see or what kind of experience they undergo.

Explorers who enter will experience a courage test held at an abandoned training center on the outskirts of South Gyeongsang Province, and cannot escape the ghost story until the test is <span class="wiki-red">properly completed</span>.

This was insane. 

*‘So they intentionally didn't confiscate the flashlight.’* 

They left it on purpose because they wanted to use us as a control group to see what happens when someone brings one in.

And the result? 

*‘Two out of the five in this group went missing the moment we entered…’* 

Without a trace, leaving us with no idea what they saw or what happened to them.

"……."

It had been a while. 

The two living people I had just been talking to a moment ago vanished after making some bizarre noises.

"U-Uh, uh… that,"

"Take a deep breath."

But it wasn't my first time dealing with this.

"I'll carry this from now on."

Let's stay calm. 

Wiping my sweat, I removed the batteries from the flashlight in my pocket and put them back in separately.

The worker didn't ask for it back. Instead, he asked a question.

In the midst of shock and panic, human instinct drives people to rely on anyone who seems to know what's going on.

"S-So we're not allowed to turn on lights…?"

"Yes."

"Why…??"

By the way, Deputy Jin Nasol didn't ask how I knew.

Because she had already figured it out.

"You deduced it in reverse because they didn't give us flashlights, didn't you?"

"Yes."

"Your brain is as sharp as always."

After thoroughly reading the flyer once more, Deputy Jin Nasol began to move her feet.

"Shall we go then?"

"Uh, uh, shouldn't we look for the others…?"

"She means let's go get the stamps. Because that's the night shift task written on the paper the company gave us."

Before Deputy Jin could repeat that this worker was dead weight and suggest dumping him, I quickly answered in her place.

"And……."

*Sigh*. 

Mentally shutting my eyes tight, I tried my best to speak in a voice devoid of agitation.

"Yes. It would be best to move quickly, just as the Deputy said."

But I knew. 

For this ghost story to function properly, there was one more condition required.

*‘A situation will inevitably arise where you absolutely cannot endure without turning on a light…’*

Isn't it obvious? 

Otherwise, it would be a cakewalk of a ghost story where you could just naturally adapt to the dark and grope your way forward slowly.

*‘A case like that wouldn't be worth writing down as an exploration record.’*

To be entertaining, it had to be horrific and spine-chilling. I swallowed hard.

*‘No matter what… I just have to endure it.’*

I just had to tell myself I was enduring a horror torture session tonight. 

And this time, I had a member of the elite squad walking ahead of me—someone who wouldn't easily lose her mind.

That alone was a massive comfort.

"…Let's go."

Grit-teeth tight, I took a step forward. Into the darkness.
