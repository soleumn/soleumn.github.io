<br>A mountain deep in the night, where a courage test is taking place.

An old-fashioned telephone hanging on the cabin wall.

"A phone…?!"

"Don't touch it!"

I grabbed the silhouette of the worker who was about to move.

"If you pick up the receiver, won't a light turn on?"

"…Ah!!"

"And in the first place, we don't even know if it works properly in this kind of situation."

It makes sense.

*'How can you trust a telephone inside a ghost story.'*

Even if by some chance it actually worked… there was no need to risk the danger right now.

There's no need to.

*'…Let's just double-check.'* 

Just to see if there really is a device here that can connect a rescue request to the Disaster Management Bureau.

Though it was a case I hadn't seen in detail within the exploration records, just because it wasn't recorded doesn't mean there isn't always a probability of it existing in reality.

"Just a moment."

I stepped closer to the phone to inspect it. 

It was a classic, ordinary telephone dial with about six speed-dial buttons positioned above the number pad.

And on one of those speed-dial buttons, number 3, a label was attached.

It was phrased indirectly so the ghost story wouldn't notice, but anyone who had seen the flyer would intuitively catch the meaning. 

<br>

<br>

"……." 

This must be it.

I casually etched it into my memory. From behind me, I heard Deputy Jin Nasol's indifferent voice.

"If there's no danger, let's move. There's no need to dawdle."

"……."

"Why bother contacting the Disaster Bureau anyway? They'll only get in the way."

If we're strictly talking about escaping, she's right.

But… the very act of me enduring as a worker for two weeks at the Daydream Branch was a risk in itself.

*'You never know what kind of crazy situation might happen in the meantime.'*

Just in case, wouldn't it be better to have a lifeline to contact the Supernatural Disaster Management Bureau?

*'Let's look at it just one more time.'*

I looked back at the flyer.

<br>

<br>

But why on earth does it look like a trashy, shady flyer?

*'It looks just like one of those illegal debt collection agencies….'*

Of course, it was probably meant to blend in naturally among the other flyers in the cabin, but for some reason, it made me squeeze my eyes shut.

The phrasing is also truly… extreme. *'Immediate Safe Escape, Unconditional Availability'*? Just how badly do they want to induce someone to contact them to pull this off?

*'They packed it tight with promotional text.'*

Reluctantly, I scanned the text all the way down to the very bottom…….

I yanked my head back sharply.

"……!!"

"W-Why? What's wrong?"

The very bottom of the flyer.

A line of text written in tiny print.

<br>

<br>

<br>

<br>

***<span style="color: #ff5c5c;">The Disaster Management Bureau does not distribute flyers in the relevant area. If you see this phrase, please run away immediately or look for a phone...</span>***

<br>

<br>

<br>

<br>

It's a trap.

*'Holy shit.'*

It's mimicking the Disaster Management Bureau.

A chill ran down my neck. I instinctively stepped backward.

"Deputy, let's move!"

I shouted to Deputy Jin Nasol and started rushing out of the cabin.

*'Let's just get out.'*

"Uh, uh, you're not going to use the phone?"

"Yes. It's a trap."

"Hik!"

The worker followed me in a sheer panic. Instead of letting go of his clothes which I had grabbed earlier, I just dragged him along with me.

*'If someone saw that and hastily picked up the phone….'*

Would the light have turned on instantly, causing them to go missing?

*'Ha.'*

This is exactly why you can't let your guard down, even with cases that weren't in the exploration records…!

Thank goodness I noticed the warning countermeasure left by the Disaster Management Bureau. I wiped the sweat from my brow. But then….

*'…Why does it say to turn around and run away?'*

I was following it for now, but a doubt began to surface.

Given the context, it seemed like a warning phrase left by the actual Disaster Management Bureau using a similar method to prevent supernatural disasters from mimicking them…

*'Turning around and running away is far too active and conspicuous an action.'*

It wasn't an appropriate response for most ghost stories. It was a command that should only be directed when absolutely necessary.

*'What is it?'*

In this specific ghost story where 'you have to walk a mountain path at night while avoiding light,' isn't blindly sprinting just a foolish act?

The key here is to avoid the light, not fall for tricks and fear, and move carefully yet quickly.

Why did it ring the warning alarm so aggressively?

Can I trust this instruction?

No, was it already too late the moment I read it?

*'Stay calm.'*

Let's think about it again.

Does the phone suddenly ring and cause a light to turn on, or something like that? It didn't seem likely, considering there was still no reaction yet.

*'If it's not the phone, is the warning text itself dangerous?'*

Since the notice itself was a trap.

*'But… come to think of it, for a trap, it was placed in a spot that was way too inconspicuous.'*

It was a miracle I even discovered it in the pitch darkness of the cabin. Which means…

"……."

"A-Are you alright?"

It was this guy.

"I mean, why did you suddenly stop walking like that…."

The worker I am holding onto.

This person was the one who found the notice.

"……."

When we first entered this ghost story, this was the person who tried to turn on a flashlight to create light.

That's why I pinned him down so he couldn't see the light—because I figured out the gimmick of this ghost story.

<div class="wiki" markdown="1">

In this place, the most terrifying thing is that not only the person who turns on the light goes missing, but everyone who sees what is illuminated by the light goes missing as well.

</div>

Right.

That's why it's crucial not to see the light, even by mistake. All the exploration records were written with a heavy focus on that fact.

But here's the thing.

If we interpret that exact sentence from the Wiki, not as the underlying principle, but literally as it is written.

<div class="wiki" markdown="1">

Not just the person who turns on the light.

</div>

*'Doesn't it mean the person who turns on the light will unconditionally go missing…?'*

Whether they see the light or not.

Whether I instantly covered his eyes or not.

"……."

If that's the case.

*'The person I am holding onto right now is….'*

"……."

"……."

"Excuse me."

"…Yes."

"Did you happen to notice?"

"……Yes?"

"You figured it out."

I bolted.

The clothes I had assumed were a work uniform somehow slid effortlessly through my fingers with a smooth, sickening texture. The worker behind me lunged forward, trying to grab my arm. I shoved it away and made a break for the door. I grabbed the doorknob……

*Thud.*

I was slammed sideways.

"Kgh…!"

I barely managed to keep my balance from the erratic attack without falling over, but my body crashed violently against something….

*Click.*

The instant I felt something being pressed down hard against my back.

I realized.

*'…A switch!'*

"Eyes!"

I barely managed to shut my eyes in time.

At the same moment, a blinding light pierced right through my eyelids.

*'Shit.'*

Cold sweat soaked my back.

The flashlight switch had just been pressed right against my back.

I tried to reach back and press the switch again, but…

*Slap.*

As my hand fumbled along the wall, the worker gave me a high-five.

<div style="text-align: right !important; margin: 20px 0;">
   <i>"Done."</i>
</div>

Goosebumps erupted all over my body.

Keeping my eyes shut, I fumbled around, backed away, and grabbed a groove. It felt like the door. Beyond my eyelids, the light scattered intensely. I swallowed hard.

Wait, what about the other person?

"Deputy!"


<div style="text-align: right !important; margin: 20px 0;">
   <i>"Even if it's a courage test, I thought it was far too dark, so I turned it on."</i>
</div>

"Are you there?"

"Yeah."

In that instant, I felt an insane amount of relief.

Where is that voice coming from? It's not too far. I need to lure it out by keeping the conversation going… and open the door to get out first! I took a step…

*"I think people will be able to see this light* *and find their way safely! Right?"*

It's right in front of my nose.

I can almost feel its breath.

It is thrusting its face right up to mine.

*'Damn it, damn it!'*

Fumbling along the door's groove with both hands behind my back, I slowly located the doorknob. Then, I spoke as calmly as possible.

"If you turn on the light, won't the people who see it disappear?"

*"They didn't disappear! I think they went to a* *better place. After all, a bright place is much* *better than a dark place, isn't it?"*

I grabbed the doorknob……. 

*“Hahaha, why are you moving so uncomfortably* *like that? You're trying to find the doorknob, right?”*

"……."

*Creeeeak.*

*"I opened it for you!" *

I let go of the knob.

Someone opens the cabin door.

*"Shall we go get our next stamp then?" *

I can't see, but my movements are completely exposed to the entity.

A primal, biological revulsion wells up inside me.

*'What do I do?'*

With my vision sealed off, how am I supposed to deal with an unknown entity that has its eyes wide open?

*'…No.'*

That's not it.

"Deputy. Just ignore it and move!"

Don't deal with it at all!

*'The moment you lose your composure and react, you get dragged into its pace.'*

When a person loses their mind, they end up acting on instinct, and the probability of them looking at the light becomes extremely high.

Because the body subconsciously feels safer when its field of vision is secured…!

*'And if this "worker" intended to attack us directly, it would have done so a long time ago.'*

So I have to take the gamble. It's better to stumble and get hurt a bit than to open my eyes and vanish.

The problem is…

*"Are you going to walk a mountain path at night* *with your eyes closed?"*

Damn it. 

Ignoring it, I reached my hand out the door and took a fumbling step forward.

*Thud.*

*'I just need to follow the dirt path.'*

If I bump into a tree, I can just keep going while fumbling my way around. Right, slowly, slowly….

*"Your pace is way too slow though?"*

This thing right now.

It's speaking with its head thrust right next to my face.

It's walking right beside me, stalking me.

*"I'll guide you. A fork in the road is coming up very soon. If you go to the right there, you'll* *find the place to get your stamp."*

I'm seriously going crazy.

"Deputy! At the fork, we absolutely have to go to the left!"

"Yes?"

"The people from the first group who appeared behind us said they had gone to the spring to drink water, remember?"

They followed the path and caught up to us.

"That means this course is a loop."

Silence.

"So if we follow the path, going left just like we have been will lead us to the spring."

*"Wow, your brain is really working smoothly, huh?* *Once this courage test is over, I'll definitely* *treat you to some proper tteokbokki!"*

I really wish it would shut the hell up.

I strained every nerve to keep from faltering as I walked.

*Grab.* Someone grabbed me from the side.

"…!"

"It's me."

Deputy Jin Nasol pulled me along, walking forward without hesitation. I have no idea how she's managing this with her eyes closed, but I whispered back while following her lead.

"Ignore it, Deputy. If it intended to harm us, it would have done it already."

"I know, okay?"

…What if Deputy Jin Nasol had failed to close her eyes properly when the cabin light turned on?

What if this isn't actually Deputy Jin Nasol?

Well, if that's the case, it's already over anyway.

*'I have to assume it's her.'*

I took a deep breath.

But.

*"But… aren't you guys really anxious?"*

"……."

We keep walking.

*"You can't see what I'm doing right now anyway.* *I could literally trip you right now."*

Endure it.

"Should I?"

…!

"Should I? Should I?"

I squeezed my eyes tightly shut.

The voice drew even closer.

*"What do you think I'm doing right now? What do* *I look like? Where do you think you're going right* *now? Curious, aren't you? Aren't you curious?"*

I must not open them, even by mistake.

Even by mistake, I must not just start running…!

I wait.

I kept waiting, and then…

*"You're curious, right? Just a tiny peek…"*

Right now.

"……!"

I thrust my hand out violently and grabbed the thing in front of my eyes.

*"Huh?"*

We can't keep moving like this.

"Deputy! Now!"

The person who could move flawlessly even with her eyes shut.

"Go get your stamp…."

*Thwack!*

"……."

Just now.

An incredible sound of a physical beating came from right in front of me…?

"Keep your eyes closed exactly as they are."

Those were the last words from Deputy Jin Nasol.

After that, I could hear muffled groans, the sound of something dragging, and the sound of something being violently slammed down.

"Done."

A muffled, suffocated 'mmmph' sound could be heard from down near the ground….

"It was chattering away so damn much, it was driving me crazy."

"……."

I hesitated for a brief moment before opening my mouth….

"Did you… subdue it?"

"Subdue? No. I just blocked this bastard's field of vision too. While you were messing around looking at the phone, I ripped down the curtains."

"…!"

She covered it with the curtains to blind the entity's vision as well…!

"You said yourself that if it could harm us, it would have done it already, right?"

"…Yes."

That's true, but how on earth did you locate the entity's head with your eyes closed, throw the curtain over it, and tie it up…

*'Is this what it takes to be part of Team A…?'*

The standard of Daydream's elite team is terrifyingly high.

I quickly turned my mind to the next step while letting out a breath.

"Then it would be best to go back and turn off the cabin light first."

"Why?"

"It's so bright that I think it could be seen from quite a distance. We can't keep traveling with our eyes closed like this forever."

…And more than anything, I didn't want the innocent people in the other groups to go missing.

"Guh, annoying. Hold onto that thing. If it gets tricked and breaks loose, just ditch it and go."

"Yes."

And surprisingly, Deputy Jin Nasol headed back toward the cabin as if she were using some sort of reality-bending movement technique.

*'Whew.'*

Meanwhile, I was holding onto the head of *'the thing that had looked like a worker,'* which was currently squirming.

After a moment, the light that had been bleeding through my eyelids completely vanished.

"Done. Open your eyes."

Very cautiously, I looked down toward the ground and opened my eyes.

Fortunately, Deputy Jin Nasol wasn't thrusting her face forward asking something creepy like 'Did you believe me?'.

*'Phew.'*

Without another word, I stood up while holding the curtain that contained 'the thing that used to be a worker'…….

…….

"It's gone."

There was absolutely nothing beneath the curtain I was holding.

"……."

"Move."

"Yes."

As if bewitched by a ghost, the back of my neck went completely cold.

And after walking through the silence, we finally arrived at the 'Osaek Spring'.

"It's the stamp."

Finally, the final moment had arrived.

Besides, just having Deputy Jin Nasol here made me feel so incredibly reassured…

"Deputy, how do you move around like that with your eyes completely covered?"

"Can't you do it?"

"……."

"It's training."

What on earth did she do before joining the company?

I almost looked at Jin Nasol with a skeptical expression. Well, since it's pitch black, she wouldn't be able to see me clearly anyway, so I guess it doesn't matter…

*Splash.*

…?!

"Wh-what are you doing?!"

I was horrified.

Deputy Jin Nasol plunged her hands into the spring and was splashing the spring water all over her face…!

*'Wh… what is she doing?!'*

For a split second, I thought she might have transformed into a 'worker' too, and I nearly turned around to bolt.

A sign hanging above the spring.

"……."

The words of the worker from the other group who had visited the spring first flashed through my mind…

*—Oh, the spring. Take a sip of the water, man, it's so refreshing. It feels like your headache and toothache just completely vanish.*

*'No way.'*

She remembered that, read the sign just now, and decided to try it?

And…?

Because it has a pain-killing effect?

"This… the pain really is going away?"

What a psycho.

Hearing her make a comment that felt like finishing off a dying enemy, I clutched my forehead.

"Why did you do that…?"

"Why?"

Her face dripping with water, she turned sharply to look at me.

Her eyes looked wild for a second, then relaxed.

"This element has nothing to do with light. The bastard who drank it before was fine too. What's the issue?"

"……."

The issue is that if you were your normal self, you wouldn't have done something like this.

*'If she were still in her Team A days, she wouldn't have tested an unverified anomaly with her own body.'*

She would have made someone else drink it first, or at the very least, found a plastic bottle to collect the water.

But right now, she didn't do that.

*'She couldn't resist pouring it directly into her own body.'*

It felt as though the keyword 'painkiller' acted as a trigger for the current Deputy Jin Nasol.

To the point where it clouded her reason.

*'…For now, let's not touch upon it.'*

It's better to talk about it after we get out; doing it now would just be stirring up trouble. Let's not cause a scene and act like a B-grade horror movie extra who dies halfway through.

"…Yes, I understand. I was just flustered because I didn't expect it."

"Right? It can happen."

Fortunately, Jin Nasol was more generous than usual.

Probably… thanks to the 'painkiller' she just put into her stomach working well. *Phew.*

"Then finally, let's go collect the prize."

"We should."

With Jin Nasol, who had become strangely more relaxed, I left the Osaek Spring.

And instead of retracing the path we came from, we kept moving straight ahead. Because approaching the cabin again felt unsettling.

Eventually, we came across the first signpost we had seen.

**«— Fairy Rock**

**                      Shinmaru Training Center —»**

*Whew.*

I turned my steps to the right.

To receive the prize and get out of here.

And for once, I felt a slight sense of anticipation regarding this process.
