<br>I thought I was seeing things.

But no matter how many times I blinked or rubbed my eyes, the reality before me didn't change. 

The "crazy person" left behind in the Y-group dorm, Worker Y491... was indisputably Deputy Jin Nasol. 

She was lying on the floor in a wretched, ruined state, wearing a tattered, standard-issue worker's uniform.

*‘What on earth happened to her?’*

Reflexively, I dropped my stance and began to inspect her condition more closely.

*‘……The more I look, the worse it gets.’*

A heavy groan caught in my throat. 

I flashed back to my memory of Deputy Jin Nasol when I first met her as a new hire. 

Remembering her pristine, perfectly polished appearance—the kind of look that made it seem like she wouldn't bleed a single drop if pricked—seeing her now as a matted, wild-haired wreck was bordering on terrifying.

And those broken fingernails, those heavily scarred, calloused hands...

*‘Where her specialized equipment used to be…….’*

The spots that had once been adorned with flashy, heavily enhanced nail parts—powerful tools meticulously crafted for active field deployment—were now a bloody, shredded mess. The stark contrast left me completely speechless.

And then... her eyes.

*‘……They're completely out of focus.’*

Deputy Jin looked as if she had entirely lost her mind, or worse, like she was actively possessed by a malevolent spirit. 

She just lay there collapsed on the floor, her soul seemingly hollowed out, muttering unintelligible nonsense under her breath.

"……."

Why on earth was an elite deputy from corporate headquarters stuck in a miserable place like this, looking like a corpse? 

In a bottom-tier dump designed purely to hook debtors and desperate gamblers to bleed them for cheap labor?

*‘It's too early to definitively label this as standard anomalous contamination.’*

Even racking my brain for the historical logs involving Jin Nasol within the &lt;Darkness Exploration Records>, I couldn't recall any entry detailing a demise or a downfall that looked like this. 

Besides, if she *had* been contaminated during a mission, this facility wasn't the kind of location she'd just passively drift into. She should have been handled by the high-level security detail or simply listed as missing in action deep within the darkness.

*‘Something is incredibly wrong here.’*

I pondered my next move carefully. 

Ultimately, I decided to deploy the most direct, intuitive approach possible. 

Keeping my voice dropped to an absolute, microscopic whisper to ensure any hidden wiretapping or monitoring equipment wouldn't pick it up, I leaned closer.

"……Team A Deputy Jin Nasol."

In that exact split second, a sudden flash of sharpness returned to her eyes.

"……!"

Her narrowly slit eyes twitched frantically, taking in her surroundings as if trying to rapidly calculate her current scenario, before snapping directly toward me. 

Her jaw parted, and a harsh, violently raspy voice scraped past her lips.

"……R-Roe deer?"

"……!"

She recognized me.

"Why…… why the hell are you here?"

*‘Honestly, I was just about to ask you the exact same thing.’*

"Are you... are you alright?"

"Do I look *fucking* alright to you?"

The piercing, razor-sharp irritation that usually defined Deputy Jin's voice wasn't entirely there, but her words definitely carried a toxic edge. 

Her voice sounded completely hollowed out, empty.

*‘But at least she has enough awareness to identify me…….’*

If there was one massive, undeniable advantage in this utterly confounding situation, it was the fact that my physical body hadn't switched out. 

I wasn't possessing someone else's corpse; I looked exactly like Kim Soleum.

My physical identity and baseline appearance had remained entirely intact. 

And seeing a familiar face from her actual reality seemed to act as a stabilizing anchor, visibly clearing some of the heavy fog clouding the deputy's fractured mind.

"Um, for starters, let's try to get you up off the floor……"

"……."

Jin Nasol's sudden burst of frantic energy began to recede, the sharp, dangerous focus fading back out of her eyes. 

Then, her lips moved in a cold, numb whisper.

"Painkillers."

"Huh?"

"Do you have any painkillers?" she demanded, her tone completely deadpan and flat.

*‘Painkillers?’*

"Uh, no, I don't carry medical supplies…… but if we make it to the infirmary wing, they might prescribe some……."

"Shut up."

"……."

*‘Is she currently enduring severe phantom pains or neurological backlash?’*

I hesitated for a moment, then reached inside my bundle of belongings and pulled out one of the few loose items I had salvaged from my previous room.

"Uh... would this do instead?"

It was a beer can. 

Alcohol, in its crudest sense, could function as a primitive, makeshift numbing agent. 

To maximize that specific dulling effect, I had deliberately selected a high-alcohol-by-volume brew from the stash. I slowly extended the cold aluminum can toward Jin Nasol.

"How about this……?"

*Snap.*

A hand shot out with terrifying, feral speed, violently snatching the can right out of my fingers.

"……!"

Her other hand clamped onto the tab, tearing it open before she practically jammed the opening into her mouth, forcing the liquid down her throat in a violent, desperate chug.

*Gulp, chug.*

Her grip was so monstrously tight that her fingertips practically indented the aluminum can as she drank.

*Gulp, gulp…….*

"……."

With a heavy, ragged exhale, she tossed the empty can aside.

*Clatter, roll.*

"I was trying to stay sober down here," she muttered, slowly pushing her upper body off the floor.

Though her eyes still looked slightly bloodshot and glassy from the raw exhaustion, her voice had become profoundly more grounded and calculated compared to her incoherent state from just a few moments prior.

"That's a bit better."

Reflexively, I took a cautious step back. I barely managed to check my momentum and steady my posture.

"Wow, thank goodness……"

I deliberately kept my tone slightly clumsy and slow, playing back into my simpleton worker persona. Instantly, Jin Nasol's eyebrows knit together in deep disgust.

"Why the hell are you talking like that……?"

But almost immediately, a cold, self-deprecating smirk cut across her lips.

"Then again, looking at the absolute state of me, I'm really not in a position to criticize anyone else."

"……."

I offered a subtle, subtle shake of my head.

"Um…… I'm not entirely sure who you are, but if you're feeling okay……"

"The wires are dead."

"……!"

"W-What do you mean by that……?"

"They don't run continuous wiretaps in the worker blocks," she explained, her delivery slowing down, clicking back into a highly rational, analytical rhythm like a rusted clockwork mechanism finally snapping back into place. 

"If they kept the audio bugs constantly transmitting across every single room for these cockroaches, the data storage and operational overhead would be astronomical. They cycle them on and off manually. And right now? It's completely turned off."

She pressed a blood-stained hand against her forehead, gesturing toward the wall with her chin.

"Over there, near the door frame. When the audio tap is actively broadcasting, the hardware emits a high-frequency ultrasonic whine that drives me insane."

*‘Ah, so that's how she confirms it.’*

"Oh? So... the weird ringing noise isn't buzzing right now……?"

"Yeah. So stop making me repeat myself, it's making my headache worse."

"……."

"Understood."

I dropped the stammering act instantly, responding with the clean, crisp vocabulary expected of Kim Soleum, senior staff member.

"……!"

"Even if the audio feed is temporarily down, the standard surveillance cameras for tracking worker movement are undoubtedly operational, so let's keep our physical movements aligned with a casual conversation," 

I noted, locking eyes with her.

To my surprise, Jin Nasol actually averted her gaze slightly, looking off to the side.

"Deputy Jin. The moment my consciousness synchronized with this sequence, I found myself dropped into this facility as a disposable contract laborer. I've been spending the last few days mapping out the parameters of this site, but I certainly didn't expect to find you stationed here."

"……."

"Why are you down here?"

"And what exactly do you think you can do about it if I tell you?"

"What exactly happened to your squad?"

"I just asked you what your plan is if I actually give you those answers," she countered sharply.

"Because we are currently in a position where we can actively pool our resources and establish a mutual extraction vector."

"Ha, a mutual extraction vector? In a lockup like this……?"

Her mocking retort suddenly cut short. 

She stared intensely at my face, her eyes narrowing to tiny slits as if a buried memory had suddenly clicked into place.

"Wait... you. You were the top-ranked hire of your entry class, weren't you?"

"Yes."

And the individual who achieved the fastest corporate promotion path in recent history.

"……."

"“I regained my senses two days ago.” 

I said, pointing to the sticker on my neck. 

Z999.

“I was in this state then.”

"You……."

"And as of today, I am officially designated as Y492."

"……!"

In just two days, I had jumped from the very last number, skipped a whole group, and reached a front number. 

"……."

Jin Nasol's gaze turned calculating, her eyes scanning my frame to gauge the validity of my claim before she offered a slow, stiff nod of acknowledgment.

"……Fine."

It was an implicit pass. 

Her posture relaxed slightly, her tone turning decidedly more professional as she transitioned into a standard operational briefing format.

"To be clear, the reason I’m here is none of your business. My situation has nothing to do with you.”

"Understood."

I hadn't expected her to care anyway.

"What's your other question?"

"What exactly is the nature of this Namhae regional branch……?"

"Why is it a godforsaken dumpster overflowing with expendable human garbage, you mean?"

A sharp, mocking sneer split her lips.

“Can’t you tell by looking? This place was made to use these so-called workers once and then throw them away. If someone at headquarters wants to earn points quickly, they grab them and send them here as management.” 

"……!"

"That's what the 'Remote Duty Allowance' actually translates to down here."

If the local management team was bleeding these workers to rapidly skyrocket their own institutional standing...

"So the tracking armbands provided to the contract staff... they really are functioning as active Dream Fluid collectors, aren't they?"

“You’re still quick on the uptake. Yes. The Dream Essence collected here is consumed by the person in charge. Though they probably get commissions stripped every time it goes up the ladder.”

A cold realization settled deep in my stomach. 

That researcher bastard who was doing the announcements must have received more points than a field exploration position….

"But you don't get a salary instead."

Wow. 

*‘So it’s a choice between points and actual money.’*

I clutched my aching, throbbing head. 

Suddenly, a memory surfaced.

<br>

*<span style="color: var(--texto-secundario);">—……New hire Kim Soleum, did you happen to receive a business card or something similar?</span>*

<br>

It was the business card given to me by the orientation coordinator right after I finished the Daydream entrance exam. 

Kwak Jekang's voice echoed vividly inside my mind.

<br>

*<span style="color: var(--texto-secundario);">—If you ever want to earn points faster, contact that number.</span>*

<br>

*‘……No way.’*

Could that business card have been the very link that triggered a transfer to a regional branch……!

*‘It's almost terrifying.’*

That meant the route to dropping down into this place had been left wide open from the very beginning. 

I felt like I had speculated about this once before, but having my unpleasant prediction definitively confirmed left a bitter taste in my mouth.

Granted, the position I'd be sent to via that card would likely be a management track to grind down workers like expendable supplies rather than becoming a worker myself, making the roles entirely different. 

But considering the working conditions, it made total sense why the rumors labeled the regional branches as an absolute living hell.

But still...

*‘If that's the case, it makes Deputy Jin Nasol’s presence here even more bizarre.’*

As a high-ranking asset, shouldn't she be up top, actively grinding down the workers instead of being stuck down here?

*‘Was there some kind of special exception?’*

A situation like that would require…

……

*‘Wait a minute.’*

<br>

There *was* one massive anomaly.

<br>

"……Is your presence here perhaps related to the disappearance of Director Ho? "

"……!!"

Director Ho. 

The absolute powerhouse of Sewang Special City.

I flashed back to the Fox Counseling Center, which had been marked with a long-term closure notice. The counselor who was supposedly handling an incredibly packed, long-term consultation schedule alongside her personal clients...

*‘But looking at it from the perspective of Daydream Corporation…… Director Ho basically vanished thin into the air on that exact day.’*

And the elite Exploration Team A that Deputy Jin Nasol belonged to... was directly aligned with Director Ho's faction! 

They were so deeply intertwined that her entire squad had been explicitly drafted to work on Director Ho's specific project.

"Did the project's disappearance cause internal corporate blowback that structural—"

"Shut up."

"……!"

"I explicitly told you not to ask about things that don't concern your immediate survival. Are you completely deaf?"

It was a direct hit. 

*‘Her overreaction proves I struck the exact bullseye!’*

Staring at Jin Nasol as she glared back at me with dark, ominous eyes, I firmly planted my feet and stood up completely straight. 

She seemed to have barely recovered her baseline sanity; it was crucial not to agitate her further. If digging deeper along this vector was going to prove impossible, I needed to pivot to a different question.

It was something I had been desperate to figure out from the start.

"Then, how much time has actually passed since Director Ho disappeared?"

"What?"

"I am asking about the timeline."

Director Ho and I must have vanished around the exact same period. 

How much time had truly elapsed in the real world since I disappeared?

Her lips slowly parted.

<br>

"……3 years."

<br>

What?

"Yeah. It's been about that long."

Hold on. 

3 years?

*‘I only spent 1 year inside my home…….’*

But in the outside world, multiple years had already flowed past? 

A sudden, numbing shock struck the back of my head.

*‘Then right now……’*

Just how drastically had the outside situation changed? A 1-year gap and a 3-year gap felt completely different contextually. How much time was left before this entire world collapsed into total ruin? What happened to the others? Were they safe, or had they perhaps—

*‘No.’*

Dwelling on it now wouldn't yield any immediate answers. It was pointless to let anxiety paralyze me. I took a deep, stabilizing breath.

*‘At the very least, one thing is absolutely certain.’*

Even in this broken, tattered state, the woman standing before me was still the single most lethal and competent Darkness Explorer in this entire facility.

Looking at my former superior, who had lost her specialized equipment and her status alike, I finally spoke.

"I am going to endure for exactly 14 days and break out of this place."

"……."

"You still possess the capacity to do the same."

If there was one invaluable asset she still retained, it was her unparalleled experience navigating the darkness.

"I don't know what your ultimate goal is, Deputy, but there have to be alternate methods outside of relying entirely on Daydream's elixirs. Daydream isn't the only organization that deals directly with supernatural phenomena."

This wasn't just a hollow attempt at persuasion; it was a cold, objective fact.

"Let's focus entirely on escaping this facility first, and then look for alternative options. What do you say?"

"……."

"Until we make our exit, I believe it would be mutually beneficial for us to operate as a tightly knit team."

I waited for her response.

Jin Nasol stared down at the floor for what felt like an eternity before finally raising her head.

"Fine. Do whatever you want."

The fierce, lethal spark had completely returned to her eyes.

After slipping into the bathroom for a brief period, Jin Nasol emerged looking remarkably put together. 

The deep wrinkles in her tattered work uniform had been smoothed out, and her overall presentation had returned to a completely composed, normal baseline.

Furthermore, the wild, tangled mess of hair that had previously cascaded past her shoulders was completely gone.

"……!"

She had sheared it off. It was now cut into a razor-sharp, uniform bob that barely brushed against her jawline, framing her dark, intense eyes perfectly.

"It was getting in the way."

"What are you looking at?" she snapped.

"……I was just thinking that you still maintain a very clean, disciplined standard when it comes to your working environment."

"Ha."

Jin Nasol let out a sharp scoff, but she didn't flare up in anger.

<br>

*Beep.*

<br>

Right at that moment, the common room door chimed and swung open.

"……Seriously, how can a single cigarette cost five thousand won?!"

"That's exactly what I'm saying! This goddamn company clearly hates the idea of paying us out fairly, so they resort to these pathetic, petty tricks to drain our pockets…… wait, mapping?"

The Y-group 490-series workers were pouring back into the sector in a loud, boisterous crowd. 

Seeing us standing there, a cocky, middle-aged man in the front narrowed his eyes to tiny slits, immediately pointing a confrontational finger directly at Jin Nasol.

"Oh, look who it is! Did the psycho bitch finally wake up and snap out of it……?"

"Hey."

Ah.

"Shut your mouth. You're being entirely too loud."

"W-What did you just say?"

"Are you deaf?"

Pressing a hand against her forehead, Jin Nasol took long, menacing strides directly toward the man.

"She used to just babble to herself in the corner, has she gone completely off the deep end now……?"

"Can you literally not comprehend a single sentence the first time it's spoken to you?"

"What…… ack, *gaff!*"

I watched in sheer awe as Jin Nasol effortlessly clamped a hand around the throat of the Y-group worker, lifting him off his feet right there in the common area.

*‘Right, I completely forgot. This woman's natural personality was always incredibly unhinged……!’*

Even under the tattered fabric of her uniform, the raw, coiled muscle and pulsing veins in her forearms were clearly visible. 

Her physical condition was an absolute wreck, yet that terrifying aura was entirely unchanged…… wait, no, this was a disaster in the making!

I rushed forward frantically to intervene.

"Ah, wait! Please don't waste your calories like this, Deputy! The alcohol's buzz is going to wear off instantly! Please stop……!"

"……."

*Thud.*

Fortunately, Jin Nasol slammed the Y-group worker back down onto the floor, releasing her grip. 

Though she threw him down like a piece of garbage, it was infinitely better than snapping his neck.

"From now on, keep your pathetic mouths shut. Breathe quietly. Got it?"

"U-Understood……."

"If you make too much noise, I will personally burn your tongue out."

She looked like an absolute demon.

The worker crowd—who were fundamentally built on a classic hierarchy of bullying the weak while cowering before the strong—took one collective look at Jin Nasol's terrifying demeanor and immediately began slipping quietly back into their respective rooms without a fight. 

They chose immediate evasion over an active confrontation.

……Wait.

*‘Hold on. If things play out like this, doesn't it inadvertently shield us from any unnecessary, annoying hazing from the other residents……?’*

Since the workers who had just retreated to their rooms might still be within earshot, I decided to play up my character to establish a clear structural dynamic for any listening ears.

"U-Um... excuse me... if people hit me, it really hurts... so you shouldn't beat me up either……."

"Your accent is getting on my nerves, drop it."

"……."

Yes, ma'am.

Either way, I had successfully re-established my operational positioning. 

To anyone looking in from the outside, I was now officially the submissive lackey to a completely unhinged, violent thug.

*‘Perfect.’*

This was an incredibly comfortable spot to operate from.

"Um... in that case, should I head over to the infirmary to see if I can secure some proper painkillers for you……?"

Silence.

Shortly after, a flat, indifferent voice responded.

"I don't have any cash."

"……."

Just how brutally stripped of your resources have you been down here……?

"I-I'll just lend you the funds for now……."

"Do whatever you want."

And with that, I voluntarily made my way toward the infirmary wing.

Yes. 

Earlier, I had desperately scrambled for an excuse to avoid visiting the medical center when the lead researcher pressed me, but going there entirely on my own accord gave me a seamless, completely natural alibi to explore the wing.

Plus, to any external observer or camera feed, it would simply look like I was being extorted and sent on an errand by the local block bully.

*‘Let's use this opportunity to properly stock up.’*

Conveniently, there was absolutely no need to navigate any awkward interactions to purchase supplies.

Stepping through the threshold, I realized the entire medical center operated purely as an automated self-service zone. 

All pharmaceutical products and emergency medical supplies were strictly locked behind giant, heavy vending machines.

*‘Those absolute Daydream lunatics…….’*

They truly didn't offer a single piece of basic human infrastructure for free. Either way, I approached the glass and selected a high-grade painkiller.

*Clunk.*

Next, I went down the list, systematically purchasing a variety of emergency first-aid items to keep as a backup cache, alongside several medical staples that frequently served as standard triggers or highly utility-dense tools within urban legend protocols. I swiped my allocation without a shred of hesitation.

The final total price that flashed on the digital screen was nothing short of catastrophic.

"210,000 won……?!"

You would honestly think this place was a dystopian nation entirely devoid of a baseline public healthcare system. 

Swallowing the heavy lump in my throat, I pressed my trembling hand against the scanner to process the payment.

Still, my internal assessment of the situation felt incredibly clean.

Walking back down the long corridor toward the Y-sector dorms, I meticulously organized my tactical thoughts.

*‘I've successfully upgraded my housing assignment, and I've effectively secured a highly potent operational ally.’*

What kind of anomalous manifestation would management deploy against us next?

*‘After the wooden doll anomaly, what kind of entity will the research division select for the next shift?’*

Instinctively, I felt like they wouldn't drop us into a high-lethality zone right off the bat. Rather than a desperate, unhinged gamble, the local director seemed to operate on a long-term, calculated optimization plan to safely test his assets' output limits.

*‘There's a highly statistically probable chance that this next deployment will feature a ghost story with a relatively low danger index.’*

I genuinely believed it was a solid, well-founded hypothesis.
