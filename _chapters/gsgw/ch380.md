<br>Walking along a mountain path at night is a dangerous thing. 

That's why this whole "courage test" setup inherently carries an eerie sense of incongruity.

A training center event that forces people to go deep into the mountains in the dead of night... Why on earth would something like that exist in reality?

<br>

*Thud.*

<br>

*‘The kids who died at the training center... did they die in an accident during a courage test?’*

I couldn't help but imagine it. 

I tried my best to shake the thought away.

<br>

*Thud.*

<br>

The footsteps of the three of us echoed quietly across the dirt path and through the broken branches as we pressed forward into the dark.

We had gone up the slope for maybe about 70 meters.

*‘…A fork in the road.’*

My eyes, now completely adjusted to the dark, spotted something between the trees.

"It’s a signpost."

A grungy, green metal signpost was pinned to a tree. 

Stepping in very close, I was able to read it.

<br>

**<center>&lt;&lt; Fairy Rock</center>**

<br>

Jin Nasol moved to the left without a moment's hesitation. 

*‘Fairy Rock was one of the places where we need to get a stamp.’*

But right behind that sign, I caught sight of another signpost—half-corroded and barely visible…

<br>

**&lt;&lt; Fairy Rock**

<br>

<div style="text-align: right !important; margin: 20px 0; color: #ff5c5c;">
  <strong>Shinmaru Training Center &gt;&gt;</strong>
</div>

<br>

An unexplainable chill shot up my spine. 

*‘Ugh.’*

I practically lunged forward to keep up with Jin Nasol. The remaining worker also quickly glued himself to my side. 

He seemed terrified of being the one left at the very back.

I could deeply sympathize with that feeling.

"Excuse me."

"…Yes."

Right. I figured walking while talking like this would make things a little less terrifying.

“I think... someone is following us from behind... ”

“......”

“I’ve been hearing footsteps for a while now.”

<br>

*Thud.*

<br>

Instinctively, I strained my ears to listen. Beyond the overlapping footsteps of the three of us, from somewhere past that...

"……."

<br>

<div style="text-align: right !important; margin: 20px 0;">
   <i>Rustle.</i>
</div>

*Thud.*

<div style="text-align: right !important; margin: 20px 0;">
   <i>Rustle.</i>
</div>

<br>

"Did you hear that?"

I heard it.

"This is definitely…"

"It's probably the other people who came in with us." 

"Huh?" 

I answered without skipping a single beat. 

"Since multiple teams entered, there are bound to be people ahead of us and behind us on the path. It’s definitely one of those teams." 

"……." 

"R-Right. Yeah. A lot of people did come in." 

"Yes. That’s definitely what it is."

But that was just… 

*‘Seriously, I'm going crazy.’* 

The horror torture session was beginning... 

"So let’s walk a bit faster."

 "……."

The worker swallowed hard. 

I shut my mouth tight again and headed forward. 

"Wait a minute! Then wouldn’t it be better to wait for them and go together? I mean, in a situation like this, the more people, the better…" 

"Really? Then you stay behind." 

"…!" 

"Shut up and keep moving, whether you keep your mouth closed or leave it behind." 

Jin Nasol spoke without even turning around. 

"How many times are you going to whine about things before we get this over with and go back inside?" 

"No, I mean…" 

"Is your brain not working? Do you lack the ability to learn?" 

"……." 

"Just a moment, Deputy." 

This wasn't working. 

I approached Jin Nasol and whispered in a low voice. 

"It won't do any good to talk to him like that."

"……." 

"Our current situations aren't that different." 

"How are they different?" 

"Right now, both you and I are just workers under this branch office..." 

"Ah." 

The corner of Jin Nasol’s mouth twitched upward into a crooked smile. 

"Who’s going to listen to a cripple who can’t do a damn thing without her painkillers?" 

"…!" 

Her voice sounded incredibly dry. 

"Granted, it's true that telling them this or that isn't going to make them listen." 

"No." 

I replied calmly. 

"It’s because right now, you aren't their superior, Deputy." 

"……."

"If you want to persuade someone who's in the same position as you, I think you need to provide an explanation thorough enough for them to understand."

A brief silence fell between us. 

And then. 

"Fine." 

Jin Nasol muttered quietly. 

"You both have a point." 

"……Deputy." 

"Alright. From now on, you do the talking." 

Jin Nasol didn't get angry. 

With a strangely muted motion, she simply pulled out another painkiller and chewed it down with her teeth. 

"……." 

Amidst the terror, a strange, complicated feeling welled up inside me and then vanished. 

*‘Phew.’* 

I moved my feet once again.

The worker quickly glued himself to my side again. 

"Um, since things turned out like this, how about we introduce ourselves? I'm, well, Y463. I used to run a tteokbokki place back in Busan..." 

"I think it would be better if you save that for when we get out. Right now, I'm so scared I don't think I'd remember it anyway." 

"Ah, right..." 

The worker slurred his words, looking slightly embarrassed. 

"Haha... When this shift is over, I'll buy a pack of tteokbokki from the snack bar and whip up a proper plate for you." 

"……Thank you." 

Please, stop doing that. 

*‘Don't go laying down death flags like "He was a friendly neighbor, so his death hit harder" type of tropes…!’* 

Doesn't he look exactly like a supporting character in a horror movie who fails to survive until the end and gets eliminated halfway through...? 

"Let's talk more after we make it out safely first." 

Wait, is even saying *that* a flag?

*‘Ehh.’* 

Let's just keep our mouths shut. 

Barely managing to steady my feet, which wanted to break into a sprint, I kept walking slowly.

<br>

*Thud.*

<br>

*‘…Now I can't even see silhouettes clearly.’* 

The deeper we walked into the mountain, the darker it became. 

Trudging through the pitch-black darkness heightened both my tension and a suffocating claustrophobia at the same time. 

Trying my best to ignore the footsteps echoing behind us, we pressed onward. 

"……." 

"Stop." 

"Yes." 

The path suddenly opened up a bit, revealing what looked like a small clearing.

The dirt beneath our feet felt slightly easier to walk on. 

"Is it a clearing?" 

"Probably." 

And as we walked a little further, we confirmed that something was completely blocking the mountain path, cutting right through it. Something massive. 

"A rock. You check it too." 

At Jin Nasol’s words, I carefully reached my hand out and touched what lay in front of us. 

The rough texture of an old rope tied in a straw knot. And the freezing sensation of cold stone…

<br>

*Grab.* Something suddenly grabbed my wrist.

<br>

"……!!" 

I violently yanked my arm back. 

*‘What the hell!’*

The sensation of ice-cold, stiff fingers scraping against my arm fell away. In that sudden movement, my body collided with the worker behind me. 

"W-What is it?!" 

I mustn't let this show to him. 

Fear is contagious. 

"…I just misstepped. I'm sorry." 

"Oh……." 

I mustn't faint, I absolutely mustn't... 

*‘Pretend you don't know.’* 

*Phew*. 

Grit-teeth tight, I stared intently at the rock. 

…I couldn't see it properly. 

In the deep darkness, the surface of the rock was utterly drowned in shadows, remaining as a terror born of the unknown and imagination…

<br>

I began to crave light so intensely it was driving me insane.

<br>

"Check behind the rock. There's a stamp." 

"…Yes." 

Rubbing my arm, I moved my trembling legs.

Just as Deputy Jin Nasol said, behind the rock sat a lonely, old folding desk, and on top of it, a plastic model stamp and an explanatory notice were visible. 

<br>

<br>

It's the first location. 

Squeezing my eyes shut, I grabbed the stamp. 

Dust was piled up around it, and I could feel the gritty texture rubbing off on my palm. 

*‘…But the ink pad hasn't dried up.’*

What on earth is it made of?

Suppressing the goosebumps, I quickly pressed the stamp onto the designated spot on the guide sheet.

One finished. 

And then. 

"Hold out your arm." 

"Y-Yes?" 

"Because it never said the stamp *had* to be stamped on paper." 

I stamped it on top of the clothes of everyone in our group. It was just in case we lost the paper. 

…Maybe because my hand had been grabbed earlier, touching someone else's body felt strangely chilling. 

"Then let's move to the next one." 

"…Yes." 

Deputy Jin Nasol boldly stepped forward once again. 

"Since visibility is poor, you could go a bit slower..." 

"No problem. Walk." 

Yep. 

It was the exact moment I thought I might cry tears of gratitude. 

<br>

<div style="text-align: right !important; margin: 20px 0;">
   <i>Rustle.</i>
</div>

<br>

"…!" 

...The footsteps I had been hearing earlier returned. 

But they were incredibly close. 

*‘Right behind us.’*

*Rustle, rustle.* Swallowing hard, I prepared to grab the person next to me and bolt right away... 

"Oh? Aren't those people?" 

"See, I told you I heard footsteps!" 

*‘…!’*

I turned my head. 

The silhouettes emerging through the path... were wearing jumpsuits. 

"For a big corporation, they sure make us do all kinds of crazy shit, seriously." 

"No wonder their stock price is crashing lately." 

They were workers. 

*‘It really was another group.’*

In an instant, the tension completely drained away. 

*‘Phew.’* 

To think my predictive guess for the sake of an excuse actually hit the mark. 

"Oh? Over there..." 

"We're a different group." 

"Ah, yeah, good work." 

Since they didn't know who I was, there was no need to adjust my tone. 

I could see a faint silhouette waving its hand, loosely acknowledging us. 

Behind them, three other figures seemed to emerge from the path... 

"You guys must have arrived a bit earlier, huh?" 

Wait a minute. 

"Are you by chance the group that entered first?" 

"Yeah, that's right. Wait, are you guys seriously still stamping that single one? We already went and got one from another spot too." 

"…Where did you stamp it?" 

"Uh, the mineral spring. Drank a sip of the spring water too, and man~ it was refreshing. Felt like my headache and toothache just vanished instantly." 

...You're doing a courage test on a wild mountain and you actually drank the water flowing here? 

*‘They've got some serious guts...’* 

Or maybe I just know too much. 

Suppressing a sigh, I roughly checked the movements of the people who had completely emerged from the path. 

One, two, three, four. 

…Four? 

"Where is the other person?"

"What?" 

"Weren't there five people in a group?" 

"Ah, yeah." 

One silhouette shrugged its shoulders.

And made a smoking gesture with his hand.

"Well, he said he was gonna smoke a cigarette, but he was taking too long so we just left him behind."

…!

To smoke a cigarette, you have to strike a lighter.

*‘If so, then that person...’*

...must have turned on a lighter's flame.

While I felt relieved that it was only one person, it was a bitter and terrifying reality.

Especially because his companions had absolutely no idea.

"He's not a kid or anything, he'll find his way back on his own."

*Phew.*

"Anyway, this is why you can't stand heavy smokers."

…….

Wait.

No... it might just be my overreaction, but.

"…‘Smokers' (plural)?"

"Huh? What, you want us to use proper and polite language now? We're all in the same boat at the end of the line, what's the point of..."

"No. That's not it..."

I swallowed hard.

*‘Kids,’ ‘smokers.’*

"The plural suffix... seems to imply multiple people."

"Uh, yeah, right."

A casual, indifferent voice.

"Because the two of them went together."

…….

…….

I slowly turned my head.

One, two, three, four…

Still,

Four of them.

"Anyway, us too let's get a stamp before we go. There's a stamp over there, right? Now that there are more people, it's a relief for everyone."

They approach.

Four people are coming.

They get close enough for their shapes to be distinct.

Three silhouettes wearing jumpsuits.

And at the very back…

<br>

A silhouette not wearing a jumpsuit.

<br>

"……Stop."

One.

"Huh?"

"What did you say?"

I pointed my finger at the person at the very back.

"You, I said stop."

"What is this bastard talking about?"

"What is your worker number?"

"Ah~ what kind of bullshit is this, trying to get a guy's number."

"I mean your worker identification number."

"Worker number?"

"……."

"……."

<br>

"What's a worker number, mister?"

<br>

Fuck.

"What the fuck!"

A voice squeezed out high-pitched, as if forced to imitate a child.

My body freezes. The three people who were walking with it scream in horror as the fourth…

They look back at the figure.

"W-Who, who the hell are you?!"

<br>

<br>

"What is this guy? His voice…."

"Come over here! Get away from it!"

But the workers from the other group completely ignore my words and move frantically. The silhouettes violently mix together, swaying in mass confusion.

"Hey! Lighter, you have a lighter too! Flick it on!"

"Don't turn it o—"

Too far.

Too late.

"Hey."

Jin Nasol grabbed the scruff of my neck and whipped me around.

"Run."

I gritted my teeth and started sprinting around the back of the rock.

"Hik!"

"Wait…… uh,"

"Aaaaah!"

"Joong-seok!"

The sound of a violent stumble.

From beyond the rock, the sound of something falling, rolling, and a dull, crushing, shattering burst echoed.

"Uuuhuhu, uhuk,"

"L-Light, light…."

"……."

"Spectacular."

"Wow."

<br>

*Thud.*

<br>

A bursting sound, like a watermelon splitting open.

I try desperately not to imagine what it was that just broke.

I focus entirely on moving away from the light and from witnessing any of it.

By the time a sweet, metallic taste fills my mouth from sprinting.

"O-Oh, a cabin!"

Something else appears at the end of the path. That silhouette is….

*‘A building…!’*

Deputy Jin Nasol dashed forward and, like a possessed spirit, miraculously located the doorknob in the dark and flung it open.

I dove inside the open door immediately.

"Haaah,"

The doorplate brushed right past my face.

<br>

*‘Phew.’*

"T-That just now… what was that? Didn't someone just die? Huh?!"

"……."

"No, forget the stamps or whatever, I think we need to report, report this to the police… Hik!"

"It's here. Bring the paper."

In the meantime, Deputy Jin Nasol had walked further inside the cabin and found the stamp.

*‘Incredible….’*

Of course, she completely ignored the one remaining worker of our group.

"W-Wait just a minute, I said…!"

"Watch your step."

Without shaking off the worker clinging to me in sheer terror, I slowly walked toward the direction of Jin Nasol's voice.

Honestly, because what just happened out there was so horrifyingly gruesome, staying glued to another human being made it a little less terrifying.

Cold sweat poured down my spine. I took another deep breath.

As I drew closer, I could sense very faint natural light filtering through the window on the opposite side of the cabin. Near it, I could at least distinguish the positions of our group.

And even the stamp hanging on the wall next to the window, its silhouette is faintly visible.

*‘So that's how she spotted it.’*

There was no explanatory text like the one I saw at 'Sagyeong Mountain Fairy Rock' earlier.

*‘Let's get this done and get out fast.’*

Gritting my teeth, I moved my hands quickly to press down one more stamp.
