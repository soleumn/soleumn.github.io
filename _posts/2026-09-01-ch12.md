When playing games from the Archive, my primary goal was always to achieve every single ending.

I collected them all, even the ones I disliked, and played meticulously to uncover every hidden element.

The Cell X was no exception.

Though I had no interest in playing as the Warden or a prison guard, I still completed all the endings.

I remembered the things I had done as a prison guard.

I had emotionlessly carried out quests that required cruel actions.

There were many quests where I had drastically raised the Security Chief's affinity by pleasing him.

I was the Security Chief's most loyal and dependable subordinate and colleague.

> "Roasted Chestnut. You're the perfect prison guard. I can't imagine this prison without you."

He had even promised to keep me by his side when he eventually became Warden.

If you were to ask if I suffered through every single moment of that entire playthrough...

The answer would be no.

There were definitely moments when I felt genuine pleasure. I vividly remember the thrill of

executing a perfect play exactly as I had planned and completing quests with flawless precision.

But that was only because it was a game.

It was only enjoyable because it wasn't reality.

Even the fleeting joy I had experienced vanished completely once I uncovered the game's hidden story.

I tried to steady my ragged breathing.

The Security Chief, reading the emotions etched on my face, continuously triggered notifications of his rising affection level.

He was confirming that his dream was no mere delusion.

Now, I seemed to understand why the Security Chief and the other prison guards had immediately raised their affection levels upon seeing my face.

The other prison guards, just like the Security Chief, had seen "Prison Guard Chestnut" in their dreams, which was why they'd all taken a liking to me.

My mind went blank, but I couldn't just accept this. I forced myself to speak, desperate to find some way out of this mess.

"...I never knew you had such a vivid imagination, Mr. Security Chief."

Ignoring the status notification windows blocking my view, I asked him, "Do you actually believe your dreams are real?"

The Security Chief chuckled. He replied with an uncharacteristically generous tone.

"I didn't believe it at first. I was closer to being skeptical."

Then, he leaned in toward me.

"But I heard the Voice of god."

Octopus Leg...?

In that instant, a thousand thoughts raced through my mind.

The mention of Octopus Leg had already appeared during the new prison guard training.

<span style="color: var(--texto-secundario);">[ Neo is a resource of infinite potential, a stepping stone provided by god for humanity's new era. ]</span>

With a prayer room even available, the prison guards had received religious instruction, and the Security Chief and the guards possessed a fanatical faith in god.

And their god, Octopus Leg, was already interfering in the Trial.

After all, it had already altered my Medical Officer's weekly schedule.

It was certainly capable of exerting enough influence over the Security Chief, an NPC who served it.

But before that, a fundamental question remained.

How did the NPC remember Roasted Chestnut?

I pushed my thoughts a little further.

There had been three instances so far where the entities of the Trial knew "Roasted Chestnut," the character I had played in the game.

- The Butlers of Clean3.

From the very start, Clean3 was designed so that its NPCs remembered previous playthroughs.

At the time, I was shocked that the Butlers remembered Roasted Chestnut. I couldn't understand how reality and the game could be so intertwined.

And the master of Clean3, who commands the mansion and its Butlers...

is Dominic, an Outer god who is nothing short of a terrible calamity.

- Edel from Call of the void.

Just before the ending, she called me Roasted Chestnut instead of Little Lamb.

But she wasn't fully aware of Roasted Chestnut.

She had merely muttered it unconsciously.

At that time, Dominic and I were by her side.

Dominic had killed and revived me countless times, and since he had even interfered with the system, he must have used the power of a god.

- The Creatures and Prison Guards of The Cell X.

Here, the Octopus Leg was revered as a god.

The creatures and prison guards did not remember all of my past playthroughs, unlike the butlers in Clean3. However, they managed to revive fragments of specific memories through the medium of dreams.

Though their roles differed—some as medical officers and others as prison guards—it was clear that both were roles I had played in the game.

In Clean3, Call of the void, and The Cell X, the NPCs all remembered Roasted Chestnut.

While the form of their memories varied, they shared one definitive commonality:

It was a god.

Based on this, I could deduce a single fact:

*'In Trials where a god exists, there is a chance that NPCs will remember the actions I took during my playthrough.'*

Of course, Dominic wasn't physically present in Clean3.

But the butlers clearly recognized their god and master, so in a sense, he could be said to "exist" there.

Once I'd thought this far, the truth I'd only ever guessed at suddenly felt tangible.

If so, then the gameplay I'd experienced wasn't just illusory data...

Could it be considered real in some moments?

Isn't that what it means to exist in someone's memory?

The moment my thoughts reached this conclusion, a profound sense of vertigo washed over me.

For a fleeting instant, my breath seemed to catch.

*I stopped thinking.*

*Because this was a ■ ■ I wasn't yet ready to handle.*

"..."

I forced my mind to turn in another direction.

The Security Chief had been steadily building his fondness for me by recalling memories through his dreams.

Still, he must have considered those dreams and reality to be separate.

But this time, the Octopus Leg had sent him the "Voice of god," and he had fully realized the truth.

*'Wait a minute.'*

I slowly parted my lips.

"Mr. Security Chief... you aren't a member of the clergy. How did you hear the Voice of god?

Couldn't it have been an auditory hallucination caused by Neo?"

The Security Chief stared at me.

His gaze seemed to know I was trying a shallow trick.

Even though his eyes were hidden behind a metal mask, I could feel it.

"That Being always watches over me closely."

The moment I heard his answer, I was certain.

The Security Chief is a subordinate of the Octopus Leg.

The "Voice of god" he mentioned seemed to be the same kind of message I sent to those registered with me.

The Octopus Leg hadn't just changed my mission—it was even sending messages to its subordinates.

*'This is insane.'*

I didn't even know what kind of being the god was yet, and now a crisis was breathing down my neck. I was losing my mind.

I racked my brain.

There was at least one precedent I could compare this to: Call of the void, whom I'd met in Dominic's presence.

Back then, I couldn't execute a proper strategy. Dominic's influence had caused the System

Window's options to be swapped at random, and the major NPCs didn't behave the way I knew they should.

The current situation was similar, but there was one major difference.

The key difference was that I wasn't the only one who had broken in.

*'Could it be that everyone else's roles have changed, just like mine?'*

I could hear my carefully laid plans crumbling in real-time. Cold sweat trickled down my spine.

"Roasted Chestnut, I know what an excellent prison guard you are," the Security Chief said, feigning a calm and persuasive tone.

It was obvious he was prepared to take more drastic measures if words failed to convince me.

I reminded myself once again that I wasn't alone here. And I thought of what would happen if

I failed to achieve the True Ending.

Feeling the weight of the Pudding Slime on my shoulder, I addressed him.

"Mr. Security Chief."

I let my lips curl into a faint smile.

"Actually, I've always wanted to be a prison guard too."

The Security Chief stared at me silently, as if daring me to speak.

I spoke with a touch of sentimentality.

"I can't... remember my dream clearly, but ever since I first met you, Mr. Security Chief, I've felt a faint sense of longing. I also thought it would be wonderful to work under someone like you."

"......"

"I'm also interested in the god you believe in. I've always wanted to have faith, if given the chance. After all, why wouldn't I believe in a god who watches over me so closely?"

I paused for a moment, taking a deep breath in and letting it out.

After a heavy sigh, I continued, my voice laced with a profound sense of gloom.

"But there's a reason why I left the training center and became a Medical Officer."

"Reason?" the Security Chief asked curtly.

I hesitated for a long moment, as if I didn't know what to do.

"Roasted Chestnut. Speak up."

Only after the Security Chief's reprimand did I finally open my mouth, as if I had no other choice.

"Actually, I am..."

Then, I squeezed my eyes shut and confessed.

"...a spy planted by the newly appointed Warden..."

At my declaration, the Security Chief and the prison guards behind him couldn't hide their momentary shock.

I carefully opened my eyes again and pretended to gauge their reactions.

"The Warden has leverage over me, so I had no choice but to obey his orders."

The Warden wanted the prison to be filled with his own men.

He added that the Warden had forced him into the position of Medical Officer and that, after arriving, he had become truly terrified by the Warden's overwhelming power and influence.

This was the most crucial part.

I carefully threw out the bait.

"You are the Second-in-command, after all, Mr. Security Chief..."

The moment the words left my lips, the air around us turned frigid.

I blinked, then quickly corrected myself as if I'd made a mistake.

"Ah, I mean in terms of rank. I know full well that you're the one who actually holds the real power, Mr. Security Chief."

That was a lie.

The Security Chief was currently losing the power struggle within the prison.

Even though he was ranked second in both position and influence, I acted as if he were the one in charge.

"That is why I cannot become a prison guard immediately. But!"

I smiled from behind the metal masks that stared at me.

"I have an idea."

And then, I presented my so-called 'Plan for the Security Chief's Brilliant Future.'

It was a method I would never have used if the Security Chief and the prison guards' affinity levels weren't high. But at this point, it was a gamble worth taking.

Sure enough, the Security Chief and the guards listened intently to my words.

"I see. It is not a bad plan, but..."

The problem was that the Security Chief's suspicion level was also far from low.

"I will need some insurance of my own, won't I?"

Clatter. The sound of glass meeting the steel table echoed.

A vial containing a neon-green liquid rolled to a stop in front of me.

I swallowed a curse.

The prison guards approached and untied the ropes binding me.

I reached out and gripped the vial.

Just then, the Pudding Slime wriggled down toward my hand, which was still clutching the vial.

He looked up at me and said, "You may drink it."

I saw a blue light shining more brilliantly than Neo's.

While I felt a bit dazed, a pleasant voice offered me calm advice.

"Haven't you already done this once? The second time will be even easier. Perhaps it will feel like nothing at all."

Dominic's voice drifted away languidly.

"Because the young god is growing."

I drank the Neo in one gulp.

He couldn't even begin to guess what was going on.

He knew Han Goyo caused anomalies during the Trial, but experiencing it firsthand was almost unbelievable.

As he was staring at the pastoral schedule, the door to the prayer room swung open with a thud.

The sudden, unannounced intrusion made Won Taebeom think of Kwak Hanmuk, but the figure who appeared was completely unexpected.

A man in a white mask strode into the prayer room as if it were his own home.

"...Representative Cha."

The moment Won Taebeom spoke his name, Representative Cha cut straight to the point.

"The Warden's daily schedule seems to have changed. He's now spending his time playing around in the prayer room."

It seemed the other man's mission had also taken a strange turn. Since it involved the prayer room, he had likely come to check with Won Taebeom, the Correction Cleric. But Won Taebeom himself had been struck by the same bolt from the blue.

"I know nothing about it," Won Taebeom replied flatly.

"Oh, really?"

A brief silence hung in the air.

*CRASH!*

Won Taebeom blocked the white blade with his gauntlet, swallowing a bitter laugh.

He had donned the gauntlet just in case, and his instincts had proven right. A force so immense it made his arm tremble violently surged through the sword. If he gave in even an inch, the blade would slice right through him.

CEO Cha asked in a flat tone.

"You still don't know?"

"...I told you I don't know," Won Taebeom replied, glaring at CEO Cha.

Just then, the prayer room door burst open with a crash, louder than before.

Won Taebeom immediately turned his head.

Kwak Hanmuk stood there.

He whistled, "Oh," and casually closed the door behind him.

"Director Cha, stop throwing your tantrum."

CEO Cha didn't even respond, but Kwak Hanmuk pressed on.

"I'm here to deliver a message from Trainee Han."

Only then did CEO Cha slowly turn his head to look at Kwak Hanmuk.

He was still pressing Won Taebeom down with the Daeil Yeorae.

Kwak Hanmuk grinned.

"Trainee Han says he's teaming up with the Security Chief."

Before Won Taebeom could even grasp what that meant, Kwak Hanmuk dropped an even more insane bombshell.

"He's going to oust you from the Warden's position, Representative Cha."
