<br>“You died again. I’m disappointed in you.”

The woman who said it had a kind, beautiful face.

A face I knew all too well.

It was none other than Baek Sara’s.

She was even dressed in the long skirt she’d worn at the 「National Theater」.

I stared at her for a while, then lowered my gaze.

When I answered, my voice was dry.

“I think there should be a prior explanation before you express disappointment. If you suddenly say you are disappointed, I cannot reflect on it because I know neither the circumstances nor the reason.”

“That’s what you have to say right now?”

The woman wearing Baek Sara’s face laughed, bewildered.

“I thought you’d react a little more.”

“……”

“Or would this face have been better?”

The woman’s voice changed.

I looked up again and swallowed a breath.

Now she had become Lee Sol.

Lee Sol, dressed in a medical uniform.

“……”

I still didn’t know exactly what was in front of me.

But I was sure she had a nasty personality.

I glanced past her shoulder.

White everywhere.

A vast expanse of white, so empty I couldn’t even tell if it was a space or a thick fog.

I was sitting alone in a coffin in the middle of it all.

Just as I’d been before I died, buried in white roses.

White roses, not chrysanthemums or lilies.

Thinking that was a strange combination, I looked again at the woman perched on the coffin lid.

The woman with Lee Sol’s face gave a faint smirk.

“Fine. I’ll take a form that’s easier for you to deal with.”

As if doing me a favor, she changed her appearance once more.

Now she had a bob haircut.

But she was still tiny, like a child.

The loose hoodie she wore made her look even younger.

…It was the author Na Bit.

“This is the best one, isn’t it?”

The woman smiled with Na Bit’s face.

I quietly swallowed a breath as I looked at her.

It was better than before.

But it still felt wrong.

That arrogant expression on Na Bit’s face.

Still, seeing that face brought back the memory.

As the woman had said, we had met before.

This was the third time, and each time she’d worn Na Bit’s face.

But the first time I died, I’d been too shocked to recognize her.

It was only the second time that I’d recognized that face, and so I’d secretly left a mark.

And now, the third time.

I asked the woman who had brought me back from the dead and claimed she had “made me.”

“Are you really the author Na Bit?”

At my question, the woman smiled warmly.

It was as unfamiliar as ever.

The author I knew didn’t smile like that, as if she were looking down on someone.

“Who knows? If you were curious, you should’ve found out for yourself.”

The woman pulled on my left arm.

Then she rolled up my sleeve and traced with her fingertip the wound that was now nearly invisible.

“I even let you get away with secretly writing that down.”

At her whisper in my ear, I leaned back.

The place she’d just touched was where I’d cut myself with my fingernail.

Where I’d carved the letter B—or rather, the number 13.

“Coward.”

“……”

I didn’t bother to make excuses.

For the first time, I wondered if this woman might not be Na Bit, but I didn’t question her about it.

In truth, even the fact that I was dead felt strangely unreal.

If I had to describe how I felt, it was like dying in a difficult game and setting down the controller.

I wasn’t devastated or desperate.

I just thought, So that’s how it is.

The only thing that bothered me was…

“Am I dead?”

“Yes.”

“Can you bring me back to life?”

“What?”

The woman burst out laughing, as if my request were ridiculous.

Then she tilted her head and asked,

“What would you do with your life?”

“I want to finish what I was doing.”

“What were you doing?”

What I’d been doing.

The operation to suppress the 「Railway Station」.

Securing the core of the 「Giant Siren」.

Saving Lee Sol.

That had been the goal.

But the goals I’d once thought possible now seemed impossibly far away.

So I spoke miserably, like a gambler thrown out of a casino.

“I want to bring the Bureau agents back to life.”

It had already happened.

But if I could, I wanted to undo it.

“Quite the ambition. You want to bring the dead back to life? What are you, a god?”

But the woman mocked even that.

So I asked her,

“Is it impossible?”

“No, not exactly. But I’m undecided.”

Undecided?

“Whether or not to bring you back.”

“Please bring me back.”

I asked at once.

The woman’s eyebrows furrowed as she smiled.

“You weren’t listening to me, were you? I told you I was disappointed in you.”

“……”

“You lost this time. A complete defeat, with no excuse. And now you’re asking me to bring you back? Why should we?”

“……Wouldn’t it be a loss for you, too, if I died here?”

“Hm?”

“You used us because you wanted something. If I die like this, everything you’ve had me do up to now will have been pointless.”

“What? Pfft—Ahahaha!”

At my words, the woman suddenly burst into laughter.

Her loud laughter startled me despite myself.

With Na Bit’s face, she laughed like a child—or a demon.

After laughing for a long while, she brushed the tears from her eyes with her fingertips.

Then, her face flushed from laughing, she whispered,

“I don’t really care either way.”

What…?

“I don’t care who wins.”

What did she mean by that?

She didn’t care?

Hadn’t she been desperate, too?

As if she’d read my thoughts, the woman murmured lazily,

“My little sibling seems pretty desperate, though.”

Her little sibling…?

“You know, the one you call 「Big Cat」. That child is diligent. A little lacking, too. I wonder if the extraction went wrong.”

「Big Cat」 was her younger sibling.

The way she disparaged it for being a little lacking was sisterly, at least.

But this hardly seemed like something that could happen between the entities in the Special District.

The “four creatures” I’d seen in the play.

Were they not just allies, but more like siblings?

Then what exactly was their situation now?

“……That 「Big Cat」 bit you to death.”

“Oh, what are you even talking about? Honestly!”

The woman burst out laughing again.

That only made my thoughts more tangled.

As if enjoying my confusion, she chuckled and slipped a hand inside my clothes.

“Are you confusing this little one with me right now?”

Before I had time to react, her hand withdrew.

A black bird lay in it.

It was 「Crow」, still limp.

The woman cradled the bird in her arms.

That tender gesture only confused me more.

「Crow」.

The one who made me.

The bird that guides souls.

And you, standing before me.

I’d thought they were all the same thing.

Was I wrong?

No, even if they weren’t the same, shouldn’t they at least be on the same side…?

Swallowing my bewilderment, I asked the woman,

“What exactly is that 「Crow」?”

“You shouldn’t ask me.”

“What does that—”

“Enough.”

The woman had been answering playfully, but now she abruptly cut me off.

“If you come back to life, you’ll forget everything. If you die, you’ll learn everything. So stop asking questions and answer me. I’ll ask you one more time. Why should we bring you back?”

I couldn’t follow everything she was saying.

So I stared at her, bewildered.

The woman stared back at me.

Her eyes were black and deep.

Endless as an abyss.

That empty darkness began to choke the breath from me.

When I flinched, realizing I’d forgotten to breathe, the woman smiled again and hid her eyes.

“This is your last chance. Now, tell me.”

At last, I understood.

This woman held my life in her hands.

And her disappointment would work on me like either divine punishment or divine fortune.

“……You said you were disappointed in me.”

“Mm.”

“Then what did you expect from me before you became disappointed?”

At my question, the woman smiled faintly.

“An interesting story.”

A story?

“You were entertaining. So, for once, I enjoyed watching.”

At her words, my mind went blank again.

At the same time, I remembered.

<br>

— Everyone is watching you.

<br>

The message I’d found at the 「Hotel」.

Could that have been what it meant?

Had I been watched?

By things that enjoyed my struggles?

I stared, speechless, at the thing wearing Na Bit’s face.

My heart seemed to freeze at the realization that I wasn’t even someone to be used—I was merely a source of amusement.

The disillusionment I’d put off for so long surged up inside me.

But the woman continued speaking with an easy smile.

“So I brought you back to life several times. But this time, we couldn’t agree. You’ve already lost. Do we really need to bring you back again?”

The woman had taken on the appearance of someone special to me.

But I no longer felt any affection for the being in front of me.

I was filled with disgust, but I swallowed it down and answered.

“……Yes. Give me a chance.”

I said it almost like a plea to the woman looking down at me with such arrogance.

“I was supposed to get three chances. So please bring me back one more time.”

“Three chances?”

The woman burst into laughter again.

She laughed loudly, as if imitating a human, then asked me,

“How many times do you think we’ve brought you back?”

“……”

Then—

A conversation I’d forgotten came back to me.

<br>

— What do you mean, the fourth time?

— Are you saying I’ve already died four times?

<br>

Every hair on my body stood on end.

At the same time, I remembered all my deaths.

When I fell from the lobby of the 「Hospital」.

When I was shot just after entering the 「Warehouse Club」.

When the “three creatures” dragged me away.

And when 「Delta」 bit me and I was covered in 「Ant Lines」.

I had died.

I’d died each time, and each death had brought me unspeakable terror.

How could I have forgotten?

The moment I became aware of all my deaths, cold sweat poured from me.

I hurriedly covered my mouth.

To hold back the urge to vomit.

“You’ve been through a lot.”

The woman spoke in a gentler voice, as if she pitied me.

“So now you can rest.”

She said it as she laid a hand on my cheek.

She was offering me mercy.

But I pushed her hand away and forced my voice out.

“……I can’t let it end like this.”

“Do you really want to live?”

I was about to nod when the woman spoke first.

“What if you’re the only one who can leave here alive?”

“……”

“Would you still live?”

It was an absurd question.

I couldn’t answer.

The woman smiled as if she’d already gotten her answer.

“All right. I understand.”
