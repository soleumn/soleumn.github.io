<br>“I’m stopping the bleeding, so don’t worry about me. Just keep walking.”

Cha Sieon’s voice was calm.

But it wasn’t convincing in the slightest.

Blood was pouring out of him like water, and he claimed he was stopping the bleeding.

“Wouldn’t it be better to treat it properly before we move?”

“This isn’t bleeding I can stop here.”

With that, Cha Sieon kept walking.

A fresh bloody footprint appeared with every step he took.

But I was still nothing more than a frail little child. I couldn’t even support the wounded man beside me.

Just then, Cha Sieon, who had been walking ahead, stopped dead.

“We’ve got about eight hundred meters left.”

*Click—*

I heard the sound of a magazine being changed.

“If we keep going, we should find the 「Monks」.”

“What do you mean—”

I looked up at Cha Sieon as he turned around.

Why was he suddenly saying something like that?

That was when it happened.

“Mew—”

A kitten’s cry came from behind me.

That was the sound of a 「River Crosser」.

“Run. As fast as you can.”

After a brief warning, Cha Sieon held out his pistol.

I looked at him for a moment, then took it.

The gun, smeared with blood, felt much heavier than it usually did.

With the pistol in hand, I started running.

*Rat-a-tat-tat!*

Gunshots rang out behind me.

I didn’t look back.

When the gunfire suddenly stopped, I almost came to a halt.

But I pulled myself together and kept running.

Then—

*BOOM!*

An explosion went off.

A deafening roar, followed by a wave of heat at my back.

Then came silence.

A cruel stillness that announced how closely death and peace were intertwined.

In the end, I stopped.

I thought about turning around again and again, but I couldn’t bring myself to do it.

If I looked back now, it would all be over.

That was what my instincts told me.

I started moving again.

Then I ran, keeping my eyes fixed straight ahead.

I was almost there.

Eight hundred meters was a distance I could run in five minutes.

And yet I was already out of breath.

“Hah… Hah…!”

I hadn’t been running long, but my legs felt heavy.

Actually, they hurt.

Cha Sieon and Baek Sara had carried me without letting my feet touch the ground, but I was already injured from jumping into the river and getting caught in the explosion.

Every step felt like blades were stabbing into my knees and ankles.

Damn it. What was I supposed to do about that?

I was all that was left now.

If I wanted to save those people, I had to—

“Hah… Hic…”

My breath, ragged and caught in my throat, finally turned into a sob.

Damn it. Tears started streaming down my face.

They muddled my already uneven breathing.

Cha Sieon was probably dead.

Baek Sara was dead.

The others were probably dead, too.

I had to bring them back.

But why were you still lying in my hand?

The moment I looked at 「Crow」 in my hand, I finally broke down.

“Don’t die…”

Unable to run any farther, I collapsed right there.

Clutching 「Crow」, I murmured desperately.

“Don’t die and leave me all alone…”

Just take me with you instead.

Please, Mom.

I couldn’t tell whether I was a twenty-eight-year-old editor or a nine-year-old child who had lost her mother.

Whether this pain was grief over someone else’s death, or despair at being left alone.

If only I’d died a long time ago, too.

If I’d died without knowing why, swept away by the metal falling from the sky and the flames.

Pain belonged to the living.

The dead carried only their own deaths with them, but the living remembered every one of the dead.

That was why they lived with death.

Living was death.

Then, perhaps—

I could have a death that might be kind to me, too.

That was when it happened.

*Clack. Clack. Clack. Clack.*

As I lay there crying, I heard footsteps beside my ear.

I flinched and lifted my head.

Across the bridge, 「Monks」 with lotus-flower heads were walking toward me.

They were the ones I’d been waiting for all this time.

But for some reason, I didn’t feel glad to see them.

Instead of relief, all I could think was, Why did you come now?

I sat there and looked at the 「Monks」.

They stared at me indifferently, just as they had the first time.

Abruptly—

The last monk stopped in front of me.

It pressed its palms together and bowed its head, crowned with a blooming lotus.

I couldn’t understand what the gesture meant.

What could that grotesque being know of humanity, that it would imitate such a gesture?

“…”

And yet, for some reason, tears began streaming down my face again.

It was only an imitation.

A meaningless mimicry performed by a monster.

Even so, that gesture made me look back at myself.

At how carelessly I had wished for death.

I cried, regretting that I had almost given up.

And when those tears finally stopped, I was an adult again.

“Phew…”

I let out a long breath.

Maybe it was because I was an adult again, or maybe because I’d cried my heart out. Either way, my mind was calm.

I steadied my breathing and wiped my horribly tear-streaked face.

Then I straightened my clothes, which had ridden up in all sorts of places.

“…First, let’s get to the door.”

Everything was a complete mess, but it was too soon to give up.

Once we got there, I’d look after 「Crow」 or do whatever else I could.

“I’ve already saved Team Leader Cha a few times…”

At the very least, Team Leader Cha was important to 「Crow」, too.

So once it woke up, it would try to save Team Leader Cha again.

“Baek Sara or Jeong Jinu, too.”

There was a good chance it would bring the others back as well.

It had saved Director Go more than once, after all.

So get a grip.

I really was all that was left now.

Talking myself through it, I stood back up.

Then I started running again in my adult body.

The distance that had seemed impossibly vast when I was a child began to shrink in an instant.

I can do this.

In my current state, I can make it.

With every step, my confidence grew, and soon I reached the doors.

“Phew…”

I caught my breath and looked at them.

Could these even be called doors?

Wouldn’t “wall” be a better word?

Of course, it wasn’t a complete wall, either.

There was a gaping hole in the middle, open to the sky, while the remaining intact sections were covered in doors.

It looked like some inscrutable piece of modern art.

In any case, the center had been blown to pieces by a barrage of bombs, but there were still intact doors around the edges.

And up close, I could tell exactly what they were.

Every door here came from 「Yongsan Station」.

The glass doors leading into the station, the metal door to the station office, even the stall doors in the bathrooms.

It looked as though every door inside the station had been dragged over here and jammed into place.

Just how badly did the 「Big Cat」 want to hide the 「Railway Station」?

Admiring that sheer determination, I put 「Crow」 in my pocket.

Then, pistol in hand, I slowly approached the doors.

Fortunately, the area around them was quiet.

Maybe they’d already used the trick they’d prepared to wipe us all out. Now the place sat abandoned, like an empty factory.

Thanks to that, I was able to reach the doors without trouble.

And most of the doors at my eye level were still intact.

All I had to do now was choose one and go inside.

“…”

But I couldn’t bring myself to open a door.

I’d seen the bombs fall in there with my own eyes.

No. No, that wasn’t it.

That bombardment had happened because they’d opened and closed doors like mad, trying to match a particular date.

The chance that opening a door would summon that same day again was vanishingly small.

Of course, that wasn’t the only danger.

There could be other horrors stored in this place, too.

But those would be exceptional events as well.

Most days, other than that one, must have passed without incident.

There must have been far more days when ordinary people went about their ordinary lives.

Right. Unless God hated me enough to kill me—

Opening a door wouldn’t immediately lead to my death.

Trusting in the odds, I opened the door in front of me.

*Click—*

Or rather, I tried to.

Before I could even reach the doorknob, the door beside it opened on its own.

Something black appeared in the gap.

The moment I saw it, it swallowed me whole.

*CRUNCH!*

The shock came before the pain.

Then everything went dark.

<br>

That didn’t mean God hated me.

God probably neither hated nor loved me.

Humans searched for God to give meaning to their own miserable existence.

That was what humans had always done.

And as if mocking them, some things simply happened.

Defeat.

Failure.

Death.

Destruction.

Or an ending.

This, too, was just one of the things that happened.

<br>

Is death white?

It seems so.

Everything around me is white.

Is death a woman?

That seems right, too.

Because I keep hearing a woman’s voice.

<br>

—I hope my death isn’t a great sorrow to you all. If anyone else has met with misfortune, please accept my condolences and sympathy, along with those of their bereaved families.

<br>

Then is death the will I wrote?

That seems right, too.

The words I’ve been hearing match exactly what I wrote in my will.

<br>

—I was advised to make my will as embarrassing as possible. So I thought about how I could embarrass myself, and I even came up with a way. But then I wondered what the living had done to deserve learning the fetishes of the dead, so I decided not to go into it.

<br>

Oh, damn it.

I should’ve burned my will before I left.

I’d just left it there, having scribbled it down in the middle of the night, swept up in my feelings.

<br>

—Did you really write this as your will?

<br>

Death muttered, sounding incredulous.

Then, in a lower voice, it exclaimed in admiration.

<br>

—Not bad… This way, people will wonder about your fetish forever. You went and mentioned it, then died without saying what it was. How wicked…

<br>

No. That was a misunderstanding.

I hadn’t meant to do that.

No, more importantly—

What was this voice?

Surely death couldn’t have such a capricious personality.

The moment the question crossed my mind, my deadened senses came back to life.

“That’s it. Come on, remember.”

The sound that had been drifting aimlessly grew clear and struck my ears.

“This is already the third time we’ve met like this.”

I turned toward the voice and saw a woman.

A face I knew.

With that familiar face, she whispered to me.

“To think you’d die again. I’m disappointed in you.”
