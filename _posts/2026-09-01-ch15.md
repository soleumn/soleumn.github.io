<br>As soon as I spoke, Won Taebeom stared at me with the exact same look Mo Haein always did.

The look that said, *'Are you fucking crazy?'*

Maybe it was because they were both in Team 2, but seeing him always reminded me of Mo Haein in so many ways.

*'Come to think of it, I'd asked Captain Mo to do something similar back in DeZonDeal.'*

It felt strange to be making a similar request to Won Taebeom. I waited for his answer, my mind drifting to how Mo Haein was doing.

"No."

Of course, he refused.

I wasn't surprised; this was the expected outcome. I would have been more shocked if he'd agreed right away.

"It's too dangerous. Propose an alternative."

"There is none."

My blunt reply left Won Taebeom speechless for a moment. He clearly hadn't expected me to be this adamant.

But it was true—there was no better way.

"I know it's dangerous. But the brainwashing only lasts for two days, doesn't it? I'll return to normal after that."

I figured it was a relatively safe method since the brainwashing would wear off in just two days. Besides, it wasn't like we'd all become Octopus Leg fanatics together. I'd just be the one going in and coming back.

But I knew if I said it like that, my suggestion would be rejected.

I lowered my gaze and tried a different approach.

"This anomaly happened because of me, so I can't put the rest of you in danger. If only I get brainwashed..."

Won Taebeom cut me off mid-sentence and asked, "Are you saying one person should sacrifice themselves to save the rest?"

*'Sacrifice'*... That seemed like a bit much.

I didn't have such a grand resolve. My only goal was for all of us to get out of here alive, no matter what.

But I couldn't answer lightly.

It was because of Won Taebeom's gaze. He was looking at me with the utmost seriousness. The weight in his eyes kept me from speaking easily.

As I was pondering what to say to get Won Taebeom to agree, he spoke first.

"Trainee Han..."

But the words didn't follow. After a moment of silence where the sentence hung unfinished, Won Taebeom finally reached a heavy conclusion.

"...No. I will follow the operation."

It was a surprisingly easy agreement. I didn't know what had moved him, but for now, the most important thing was that I had his consent.

I let my lips curve into a slight smile and replied, "Thank you for trusting me. I will definitely clear this with the True Ending."

<br>Ever since I first decided to undergo the brainwashing, I had one major concern.

Would it even work on me?

Just as Neo's infection had been neutralized, the brainwashing might simply unravel.

But unlike Neo, who made direct physical contact, the Correction Cleric's brainwashing was a judgment rendered by the System.

So, I had expected it to work on me.

"...?"

The brainwashing failed.

Flustered, I told Won Taebeom that it didn't seem to have worked.

He asked me a few questions, and after hearing my answers, he confirmed that I had been brainwashed.

*'I don't think I have.'*

I didn't feel any particular awe toward Mr. Octopus Leg.

However, after Won Taebeom used his brainwashing ability on me, the Medical Officer's weekly schedule returned to normal.

Won Taebeom's own pastoral schedule also reverted to his original mission, no longer listing "Brainwash the Medical Officer."

Perhaps because he had used his ability on me, the mission was already marked as complete.

The brainwashing had failed, but I had still achieved my goal.

I was satisfied that things seemed to be going well.

It was a shame I couldn't perform the four-part worship set for Mr. Octopus Leg in the prayer room, though...

"I will also share Trainee Han's current status with Captain Gwak."

Since Je Hyun-oh didn't know my location, I'd leave him out and tell Kwak Hanmuk that I was temporarily brainwashed.

Even if nothing had actually changed, sharing the situation was a good idea.

I spoke with Won Taebeom briefly before parting ways.

Then, I headed to the Security Chief's office.

Strangely, my heart had been pounding for a while now. I tried to stay calm, but I couldn't help feeling excited.

I thought of Mr. Octopus Leg.

Does Mr. Octopus Leg dwell in the deep sea? Why does He remain there?

I wished to see Him in the flesh, just once. I allowed my imagination to freely conjure His form.

I could envision Him as vividly as if I had seen Him in a dream.

His tentacles, composed of squelching organic matter, were surely an infinitely regenerating tissue.

The perfect biological structure, transcending any crude specifications, was ecstatic to imagine.

I felt pity for those who remained ignorant of His gracious corruption.

*'I want to remodel the prisoners.'*

How frail are humans, and how frail are Creatures.

Their diseased minds, scarred bodies, and failing organs were all so utterly pathetic.

I wanted to break and reassemble their imperfect bodies and minds, so they could draw even a fraction closer to Him.

I quickly mentally reviewed the psychotropic drugs available in the infirmary.

Mind modification seemed entirely feasible. I'd even tried it in a game before.

With the right combination of drugs and religious broadcasts, it would be a simple task.

The problem was the physical modifications.

*'What if I got some deep-sea creatures and grafted them onto the inmates?'*

Mr. Security Chief is one of His subordinates, so he'd probably be open to discussing such matters...

I came to a dead halt in the hallway.

"..."

The moment I realized the absurdity of my train of thought, a System Window popped up.

I had, but I wasn't completely captivated like I was during the previous Clean3. After all, I could still recognize that I was brainwashed.

I had to hold out like this until tomorrow. I'd have to keep my wits about me to avoid causing any trouble.

*'If I start acting strange again, bring up a notification window.'*

Still, hearing the notification sound might be of some help.

The System Window didn't respond. But I figured it had probably understood me well enough, so I didn't repeat myself.

I headed back to the Security Chief's office.

I had planned to go there all along. For his coup, of course.

A short while later...

I gathered the Warden, the Security Chief, and three high-ranking prison guards in the conference room and began my presentation.

The conference room was equipped with the most advanced facilities in the entire prison.

The presentation went smoothly, thanks to the monitor I'd brought from the new prison guard training center.

"The current Creature Management Law is outdated," I began, starting with a provocative statement.

"The amount of Neo that can be extracted from creatures is limited, and that yield is steadily declining."

I displayed a graph showing the production levels over the past three years. The line plummeted sharply downward. It was an inevitable result of the imprisoned creatures' waning life force.

"Furthermore, managing creatures that constantly cause trouble is no easy feat. The unbelievers won't even watch the religious broadcasts properly."

That was a real problem. Mr. Octopus Leg had shown such boundless mercy by allowing even the unbelievers to remain within his domain, yet...

"Indeed."

"Therefore, I propose a new Creature Management Law!"

A photo of Number 6 appeared on the monitor.

"Number 6 is the only creature capable of communication, making it easy to manage. Furthermore, as it is humanoid, it is among the less repulsive of the creatures. Thus, I propose that all creatures except Number 6 be executed."

The plan was to execute the creatures to extract a large quantity of Neo, then feed the corpses to Number 6 to restore its vitality.

It was far more efficient to fully restore Number 6's vitality and obtain a massive amount of Neo than to extract small quantities from twelve creatures.

He displayed data on the screen proving that this method would result in higher production.

"At this point, you might naturally wonder: what happens if Number 6's production drops after we initially mass-produce Neo through executions?"

I smiled and changed the slide.

"We can use the Scrap."

Feeding the Creatures the Scrap—otherwise useless waste—would restore their vitality. It was a truly eco-friendly method.

Even if the Scrap ran out, it wouldn't matter. Since it was easy to get a steady supply of human inmates from the surface, we could just inject them with Neo to create more.

I also proposed a method for executing the Creatures.

"Holding public executions in the courtyard would surely please The One in the Deep Sea, and I believe it would provide a bit of entertainment for the prison guards as well. That concludes my presentation."

The conference room buzzed with murmurs after I finished my presentation.

The three high-ranking prison guards in attendance exchanged glances, their eyes meeting as they looked at one another. They seemed to find my proposal extremely appealing.

The Warden, however, remained silent.

Seated at the head of the table with his arms crossed, he had watched me throughout the entire presentation, his gaze fixed and scrutinizing. I couldn't fathom the reason behind his continuous, unblinking stare.

I waited patiently, meeting the Warden's eyes.

His white mask stood out starkly among the gray and black metal ones. Just as I was thinking how clean and white it was, a mechanical voice crackled through.

"When do you plan to hold the Creature Execution Ceremony?"

"How does two days from now sound?"

The Warden, who had been staring at me all this time, finally gave his approval.

"Go ahead."

"Thank you."

The moment I got permission, I went straight to find Mr. Security Chief.

'Mr. Security Chief!'

I gazed at him with pride.
