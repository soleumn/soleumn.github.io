<br>*'I'll need a researcher anyway to get the True Ending.'*

Since I was already here, I planned to clear the dungeon and shut it down by achieving the True Ending.

And judging by the message from the System Window, it seemed like my honor would take a hit if I failed to shut it down.

<div class="system-window" style="text-align: center;">
  <div class="system-content">
    <strong>You believe that merely shutting down a mere Trial like this will prevent the tragedy that is to come.</strong>
  </div>
</div>

*'Alright, I get it.'*

It seemed the System was quite displeased that I had entered a mere One-Star Trial.

It wasn't like I wanted to come in here, either.

I minimized the System Window and quickly sent a message to Je Hyun-oh.

<div class="system-window" style="text-align: center;">
  <div class="system-content">
    <strong>Lieutenant Colonel Je, if you're in the middle of cutting the building, please stop for a moment.<br>It's a One-Star Trial, so it won't take long to clear.</strong>
  </div>
</div>

The time flow in PTB was faster, so the wait outside would be short.

After roughly estimating the time it would take, I sent another message.

<div class="system-window" style="text-align: center;">
  <div class="system-content">
    <strong>Waiting outside for about five minutes will be enough.<br>I'll teach CEO Cha a proper lesson here before coming out.</strong>
  </div>
</div>

I had exaggerated in my message, but my heart was racing with anxiety.

Because he was wearing an item that transformed him into a human, Je Hyun-oh's reaction was a bit slower than usual, and he missed his chance to catch me.

*My subordinate would be worried.*

*He would definitely come looking for me.*

...But he probably wouldn't be too anxious yet.

And since this was a Trial limited to two people, he wouldn't be able to enter. But he would definitely be searching for me.

I resolved to shut down the Paseong at maximum speed before Je Hyun-oh could cut it all down.

Quickly formulating a strategy in my head, I looked at the button CEO Cha had smashed.

To prevent the researcher from appearing, we had to fail to cooperate for 30 questions. One person had to keep answering correctly, while the other had to get the answers wrong.

It was a given that we couldn't both get the answers right, nor could we both get them wrong at the same time.

But CEO Cha had just blown up all his own buttons.

From now on, he wouldn't be able to press a single button, meaning the System would automatically mark his answers as incorrect.

All I had to do was keep getting the answers right.

Of course, there was a way to restore the buttons. A panel to reset the cage environment was hidden in a corner of the lab. This was a built-in feature for when the test subjects in the lab got stressed and started smashing things.

CEO Cha must have known that when he blew up the buttons.

*'I'll just let him figure out how to restore them himself,' *I thought.

I stared at CEO Cha through the glass. After pulling such a mad stunt, he was calmly sheathing the Daeil Yeorae.

If only this glass window weren't in the way, I would have stormed over and punched him. We'd already come to blows several times before over his reckless, unilateral actions...

I was furious, yet my mind was a mess of conflicting emotions.


<span style="color: var(--texto-secundario);">“Do you still think this is just a game?”</span>

<span style="color: var(--texto-secundario);">“I'm only shaking because I don't want to regret letting a single NPC die.”</span>


When CEO Cha had directly refuted me in *The Cell X*—me, who wanted the True Ending—I think I was... devastated.

I had unconsciously assumed that CEO Cha would trust me implicitly and follow my lead.

It was the exact moment I realized, with crushing clarity, that the CEO in reality was a different being entirely—not the character from the game I had come to love.

*'And yet, it was so obvious.'*

I acknowledged and accepted that I had become someone meaningless to him.

But after that, CEO Cha grew even stranger, drifting further and further from the man I once knew.

He even went so far as to commit an act that caused himself immense harm.

He had become a complete stranger.

"..."

The glass window separating CEO Cha and me was so clean it was practically transparent.

I would have preferred it if I couldn't see him so clearly.

I glared at CEO Cha and spoke.

"I came to visit you in the hospital to talk, not for you to act out of control like this."

"You'll get plenty of talking done in front of Je Hyun-oh. With a box of cup noodles."

"Lieutenant Colonel Je knows everything anyway..."

"So I'm the only one who doesn't?"

CEO Cha cut me off with a sharp retort, and at that same moment, the floor lit up and a video began to play.

The game was played by watching videos on a floor made of monitor panels and solving problems.

The vast universe unfolded, and stars gathered to form letters.

I checked the problem displayed on the floor.

<br>

<div class="f-sixtyfour" style="border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave, rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.1)); border-radius: 12px; padding: 16px; text-align: center; margin: 20px 0;">
My will to choose the correct answer now is a free choice of mine, completely independent of the universe's causality.
</div>

<br>

Without hesitation, I pressed the X button.

Immediately, fireworks burst across the floor's screen, and the explanation appeared.

<br>

<div class="f-sixtyfour" style="border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave, rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.1)); border-radius: 12px; padding: 16px; text-align: center; margin: 20px 0;">
Correct! All human choices, hesitations, and even fears are nothing more than puppets under the threads woven at the beginning of time.
</div>

<br>

Electricity surged through CEO Cha, who had failed to get the answer right.

"..."

His fingertips trembled slightly.

If a subject answered incorrectly, the collar they wore would discharge electricity. It was a penalty similar to giving electric shocks to a test rat.

The first shock was a mere prickle of static electricity, but the shocks grew stronger with each incorrect answer.

Getting a single answer right would reset the intensity to level zero.

But CEO Cha had smashed the button.

As a penalty, the current started at a high intensity.

Moreover, he was destined to get the next thirty questions wrong in a row.

The pain would be excruciating after ten or so accumulated shocks.

He must have been prepared for that before committing such an act.

"Explain what a Young god is."

The cosmic imagery on the floor scattered to make room for the next question.

I answered, still staring at the floor.

"It is exactly what it sounds like. Whether you believe it or not is up to you, CEO."

The second question appeared.

The image on the floor now teemed with eyes of all colors, varying in size.

It reminded me of The Cell X.

<br>

<div class="f-sixtyfour" style="border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave, rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.1)); border-radius: 12px; padding: 16px; text-align: center; margin: 20px 0;">
The more truth one knows, the more complete and perfect human intelligence becomes.
</div>

<br>

This time, I pressed the X button again.

<br>

<div class="f-sixtyfour" style="border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave, rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.1)); border-radius: 12px; padding: 16px; text-align: center; margin: 20px 0;">
Correct! Humans are utterly weak. The moment they comprehend the truth, their brains melt.
</div>

<br>

CEO Cha was standing right in front of the glass window, watching me get the answer right.

"Do you remember what happened then?"

I turned to face him. A white Cognitive Decline mask stared back at me.

"...Yes, I do."

The problem was that the memory was too vivid. I remembered every single scene, every single thing I had done.

"It's true that I did it, but I didn't want to. I'll be careful not to let it happen again."

It sounded like a lame excuse, but I meant it.

The next problem flashed into my mind. As I focused on solving it, I felt a gaze fixed on me.

After leaving The Cell X, CEO Cha must have looked into the phenomena he had experienced—the one who called himself the Boss of The Cell X, ■■■, and the abilities I had displayed.

They were completely different from the entities he had faced in the Trials so far.

I was prepared to answer any question he might ask as honestly as possible.

After all, the dispatch contract bound me to enter the Trials with CEO Cha. He would also be a crucial ally in the events to come.

There was no point in hiding any more.

Yet, CEO Cha wouldn't leave me alone, taunting me instead.

"Playing god seems to suit you. You haven't even looked for a way to become human again."

I focused on answering the questions.

"I can't go back, and I don't want to."

I meant it. If I suddenly became human again, who would deal with the meteorites about to rain down on Earth?

CEO Cha's silence stretched on. He watched me press the O and X buttons before finally speaking.

"Je Hyun-oh is your subordinate, isn't he?"

"...Yes."

I had sent him a quest to assist the Young god's subordinate. He'd even been registered as my temporary possession, allowing me to use his abilities, so he knew exactly what being a subordinate meant.

CEO Cha fell silent again. Then, he slowly broke his silence.

"What if he dies?"

I whipped my head around to stare at him.

*That would never happen. Never.*

Meeting my gaze, CEO Cha let out a hollow laugh.

It was a smile that seemed to confirm something.

I felt my eyes turn gold.

CEO Cha stared straight at me and sneered.

"What, are you planning to die with me?"

I clenched my jaw at his relentless taunts.

I strode forward, pressing myself against the glass window as I glared at him.

"...Why are you doing this?"

This wasn't something a pragmatist like him would ever do.

And this time, it was happening right in front of my subordinate.

*My subordinate must be waiting.*

Worry for Je Hyun-oh mingled with a torrent of other thoughts.

He had destroyed the Era Agency at will, dragged me into a Trial right before Je Hyun-oh's eyes...

And yet, he was now trying to save me, even at the cost of his own life force.

Right now... he was enduring pain he didn't need to suffer at all, all to get under my skin.

I couldn't even recognize the figure before me.

"Why are you doing this to me? Why are you doing something so inefficient?"

The moment I asked, the time to submit the answer expired.

A powerful electric current surged through CEO Cha, who had been marked as an incorrect answer.

This time, the shock was strong enough to make him stumble.

He gripped the hilt of Daeil Yeorae's sword. A voice laced with mockery reached my ears.

"The one being inefficient is you, worrying about your subordinates. Even when you're putting your life on the line."

I had tried to handle this rationally, but I simply couldn't anymore.

The moment I saw CEO Cha enduring the pain, everything I had been suppressing finally erupted.

I slammed my hand against the glass window.

"Why do you keep acting like this?!"

The window rattled with a thud. I had struck it with all my might, yet I felt no pain.

Absurdly, CEO Cha seemed relieved by my outburst.

He stepped closer to the window, mirroring my position. If he were truly Daeil Yeorae, he could have shattered the glass effortlessly.

But he didn't.

"Then what about you?"

Standing behind the glass, his own face concealed beneath the Cognitive Decline mask, he asked the question, his gaze fixed solely on me.

"Why do you act as if you know me?"

We were right back where we started. Neither of us answered the other's question.

"Han Goyo. I'm done holding back."

He looked down at me, his voice a one-sided demand.

"Don't change any further. Stop right here."

*How foolish.*

*A mere human, trying to define a god.*

But I didn't reveal the truth to him. Instead, I met his demands with my own.

"Don't ever do this again. I'm being lenient with you too."

*Taming a self-centered human might be amusing after all.*

I dismissed the social misfit's mindset.

A heavy silence stretched between us, separated by the glass window. In that stillness, I slowly regained my composure. It was enough time to remind myself that clearing this was now my most urgent priority.

Just as my anger had finally subsided, the screen on the floor began to change.

Because of CEO Cha, I had left the previous problem unsolved.

Before I could even choose an answer, the screen skipped to the next question.

I forced myself to calm down and stepped away from the glass panel.

Then, I checked the screen.

A rainbow spectrum video with a metallic sheen appeared.

The question materialized over the ever-changing, multicolored hologram.

<br>

<div class="f-sixtyfour texto-rainbow" style="border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave, rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.1)); border-radius: 12px; padding: 16px; text-align: center; margin: 20px 0;">
  Samra Mansang of the planet 'Earth' is a different entity from us.
</div>

<br>

This... was related to the world outside the Trial.

I didn't know why a question about Samra had suddenly appeared, but if I got it wrong, I would have to answer 30 questions correctly in a row all over again.

That would be a problem, as I needed to clear this in the shortest time possible.

After careful consideration, I pressed the O button.

Samra had originally been an NPC, but he had escaped the Trial by turning a part of himself into an item. I wasn't sure why the NPCs always referred to themselves as "we" at the end of each route, but I chose O because I believed Samra was no longer an NPC.

<br>

<div class="f-sixtyfour texto-rainbow" style="border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave, rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.1)); border-radius: 12px; padding: 16px; text-align: center; margin: 20px 0;">
  Correct! Samra chose for himself.
</div>

<br>

I had managed to answer correctly, but the explanation was still vague.

The holographic image dissolved.

I narrowed my eyes, waiting to see if the next question would also be displayed abnormally.

Just then, a voice crackled through the speakers.

*"What's wrong? Why are the subjects' data values like this? Hold on, stop everything."*

The wall at the front slid open with a mechanical buzz. A researcher with shaggy hair appeared, holding a small device. It was the machine that controlled the electric current flowing through the subjects' collars.

The Researcher approached irritably, holding the means to subdue the subjects in one hand and a tablet in the other.

He turned toward me at the glass partition.

*'The Researcher is already here?'*

I had only answered about ten questions correctly. Even his usual entrance line was different this time.

Our eyes met.

The Researcher, who had been walking toward me without hesitation, stopped in his tracks.

His mouth and eyes widened slowly.

*'What's wrong with him?'*

My confusion over his strange reaction was short-lived. My gaze fell on the name tag pinned to his lab coat.

<div style="border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave, rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.1)); border-radius: 12px; padding: 16px; text-align: center; margin: 20px 0;">
  ■■■ Researcher
</div>

The name was blocked out.

*'But... I think I can see through that.'*

I peered into the Researcher.

As I lightly observed him, a groan escaped his lips.

"Ugh... Ugh..."

A terrified hiss leaked through his parted lips.

"Aaaaaaaahhh!!!!"

The researcher suddenly grabbed his head with both hands and screamed.

CEO Cha immediately shattered the glass window and leaped over to my side.

At the same time, the block obscuring my vision vanished.

<div style="border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave, rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.1)); border-radius: 12px; padding: 16px; text-align: center; margin: 20px 0;">
  Paranoid Researcher
</div>

It was a familiar name.

Amidst the scattering glass shards, I immediately recalled DeZonDeal's old diary.

<span style="color: var(--texto-secundario);">[Journal of the Paranoid Researcher]</span>

When I was conquering DeZonDeal, I hadn't given it much thought. I had simply dismissed it as a nonsense-filled diary that mentioned a cure.

But now, I knew.

<span style="color: var(--texto-secundario);">[The writhing pulsation of dimensions!]</span>

<span style="color: var(--texto-secundario);">How dare we gaze upon that wondrous void of disorder!]</span>

<span style="color: var(--texto-secundario);">Formless chaos!]</span>

<span style="color: var(--texto-secundario);">All is but a minor amusement for That Person!]</span>

The contents of the Paranoid Researcher's journal were praises to an Outer god.

I had guessed that the Paranoid Researcher must have worshipped Dominic as a god after entering CallOVo.

I figured it made sense, considering Dominic was such a formidable Outer god.

*'But how is a DeZonDeal NPC here?'*

This was impossible. DeZonDeal had been shut down...

Even if he'd escaped before the shutdown, the logic didn't hold. It would mean an NPC had moved to a different Trial.

I stared at the Researcher, who was now thrashing his head up and down, screaming and laughing maniacally.

"Aaaaargh! K-kgh? Ah, ahaha! Why! Here! A-ah, aaaaah!"

He was spouting nonsense, his eyes rolled back in his head.

I needed to get information from the Researcher, but looking at him now, he seemed unlikely to ever recover his sanity.

*I had looked too deep.*

*Humans are fragile; I needed to be careful.*

*But since he's already broken, maybe it's okay to look a little deeper?*

*Maybe I can uncover what I'm curious about.*

The tempting thought refused to leave my mind.

I stole a glance at CEO Cha. He was staring at the researcher, Daeil Yeorae in hand, yet his wariness was also directed at the Young god. Even as I realized I was behaving strangely in front of him...

I found myself curling the corners of my lips into a smile.

*He'll love me no matter what I do.*

*'But if I dig any deeper, can I still get the True Ending?'*

I was gazing blankly at the researcher, my mind a bit hazy, when a sudden, strange sensation washed over me.

*I felt the presence of my subordinate.*

Though I knew it was impossible, my eyes shot up to the ceiling.

"...!!"

I saw black metal slicing smoothly through the air.

A metallic hand reached out from the darkness, and soon, a black figure slowly emerged.

Unfurling its wings, the creature appeared silently and moved without a sound.

A single line was drawn across the researcher's neck.

"...Nothing? Why? The same? The same? The same? Ehih, khehih, stop, please, hihihi-1-2-3! Impossible!"

The researcher, screaming incoherently, saw his head fall with a dull thud to the floor.

The rolling head, still unaware of its own death, continued to shriek.

"Impossible! Impossible! Impossible..."

The cries ceased shortly after.

With a geyser of blood erupting behind him, the man in black stepped onto the floor, now pooling with gore.

The figure, draped in black from head to toe, looked at me.

"Goyo."

My face was reflected in his darkened goggles. A harsh, low warning was issued.

"I hate being kept waiting."
