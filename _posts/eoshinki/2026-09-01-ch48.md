<br>The man strolled along, humming a tune.

As he swept his hands through his hair, the previously shaggy mess smoothed into a neat style. His eyes, now fully visible, were narrowed like a snake's, and his ears were cluttered with piercings. A touch of arrogance laced his light, jaunty stride.

Walking down the dark corridor, the man licked his lips and smirked. He followed the faint strain of a violin.

When he opened the door, a classic horseshoe-shaped opera house revealed itself. The auditorium was packed with an audience, yet it was so silent that not even a breath could be heard.

On the stage, bathed in a spotlight, a woman played the violin. Wearing a black dress and a hat with a short birdcage veil, her face was as pale as a widow's at a funeral.

Under the white lights, the woman pressed the strings against the fingerboard with precise movements and drew the bow across them.

The piece she played on the 1700s Guarneri was Bach's *Chaconne*.

The *Chaconne* was a notoriously difficult piece, demanding extreme technical skill from any violinist. Yet, even amidst the suffocatingly dense chords, the woman remained utterly serene.

A melody so grand it seemed impossible for a single violin to produce continued to unfold.

Despite the flawless performance, there was no applause, no cheers from the audience.

For the dead are silent.

The man smiled as he watched the performer.

What displeased her so much that she'd create a scene from hell in such a short time?

And yet, look how neatly she's arranged them.

The victims, their eyes and hearts torn out, sat in the seats, posed with disturbing precision.

The blood draining from their bodies soaked the floor in a thick, viscous pool.

The one responsible for this scene was playing the violin in solitary elegance.

Yet, for once, she did not play to the end.

Normally, she would have ignored him until the piece was finished, but this time, the man had returned with the news she had been waiting for.

Lowering her bow, she asked, "How was it?"

"Absolutely perfect."

A flicker of emotion finally touched the woman's corpse-like, expressionless face. As she looked on in surprise, the man suddenly exclaimed, "Ah, I want to show you right now!"

The long shadow stretching out behind him writhed and bulged. It threatened to burst forth, swelling as it engulfed the floor.

The man strode down the stairs. With every step onto the blood-soaked floor, a wet, squelching sound echoed.

Leaping nimbly onto the stage, he threw his arms wide and proclaimed like an opera star, "Everyone will love this. All of us!"

The woman, who had been watching him silently, asked, "Even the Boss?"

"Yes. Absolutely."

The man grinned at her. A slow smile began to spread across her otherwise expressionless face. Her eyes, ablaze with a long-forgotten ecstasy, shimmered as she whispered tenderly, "When will you bring him to me? I know you said not yet... but I want to see him too."

The man approached her and wiped the corner of her mouth with his thumb. After licking the blood from his finger, he pulled his sleeve over his hand and gently patted her lips to clean them.

The woman smiled, her lips a vivid red. "Let's tell the Boss."

"What? Is the Boss back?"

"He's asleep."

The man ran a hand down his neck, a look of delight on his face.

"I'll have to wake him up and tell him."

Just then, a tattoo that had been invisible until now appeared on his skin. The tattoo, etched vertically and neatly on the man's neck, was revealed clearly under the stage lights.

The same mark was also engraved on the wrist of the woman holding the violin bow.

<span class="tooltip-container">金天<span class="tooltip-text" style="left: 0 !important; right: auto !important; transform: none !important;">It's a chinese term that means 'golden sky'</span></span>

Those who bore the mark of the Golden Sky looked at each other and smiled joyfully.

It was a moment that made them look forward to the day they would engrave the mark upon their new friend.

—It's Geumcheon.

"...Geumcheon entered the Era Agency as a recruit?"

Still, he didn't say those things out loud, and only explained to me the methods and items Angel Wings had used.

Angel Wings mainly used the tactic of appearing in the brief window just before entering a Trial and then vanishing immediately after it was cleared.

He also possessed an item that could distort memories in real-time, making anyone in the same space forget him. In fact, Angel Wings had even detonated a Popping Candy Bomb in front of the recruits, yet no one but me remembered it.

Samra brought up an image of a man on the screen.

—He is one of Geumcheon's key executives.

I stared at the screen with unfamiliar eyes.

*'This man is Angel Wings?'*

He looked similar at a glance, but the vibe was completely different. His ears, covered in piercings, and the Chinese character tattoo on his neck were utterly strange to me.

I didn't know much about Geumcheon.

While I was playing the Archive game, Geumcheon was mentioned, but he never appeared in the game itself, nor did I ever meet him as an NPC.

At the time, I hadn't given much thought to why Geumcheon didn't appear. I just figured he'd show up eventually if I kept playing.

But even by the day the broadcast ended, no game featured Geumcheon as a main character.

The Creator must have deliberately restricted that information. He probably wanted to hide Geumcheon's cruelty from me, showing me only the good parts.

—It might be safer to stay inside a Trial for the time being.

I agreed with Samra's assessment. After all, I had to enter a Trial for the Epic Quest anyway.

However, I was a bit disappointed that I was only just starting my Recruit Training in earnest.

—I will make arrangements for you to receive Recruit Training separately.

I was slightly taken aback.

He had spoken as if reading my mind, even though I hadn't said a word.

While I was marveling at the Android's technological advancement, Samra also said he would narrow down the list of Three-Star Grade or higher Trials for me to enter.

Then, he brought up another topic.

—The Asset Management Department has requested cooperation regarding the Clear items from &lt;Hop Drop Pop>.

Up until now, all the items I had obtained by closing Trials were bound items that only I could use.

But this time, I had Cleared Hopdpop with a Normal Ending.

It seemed they had contacted me thinking that since this wasn't a True Ending, I might finally be able to hand over the item.

When I signed the new contract with the Era Agency, I had added a clause stating that items I obtained from Trials were fundamentally my property. The Asset Department had no authority to forcibly seize them. Even now, they were probably just asking on the off chance.

I had no intention of giving it to them, but I pretended to check the Orca doll headband in my inventory.

Then, I discovered a reason why I truly couldn't give it away.

"Uh... Samra, it seems this item is also bound to me."

—Is it an item related to emotions?

"No. It is not."

Another exception had occurred.

A light flickered in Samra's eyes as he began to process the information. I recalled the new knowledge about items I had acquired during my training with Mo Haein.

When I checked low-grade items in the System Window, their usage was usually explained intuitively.

Conversely, the higher the item's grade, the more ambiguous its description, leaving more room for interpretation.

This wasn't a rule that applied to all items, but it was generally true.

For example, items like the Daeil Yeorae did not clearly state their purpose even after reading the description.

In such cases, the Adaptee must figure out for themselves how to use their item.

Furthermore, there were cases where an item's abilities changed or, very rarely, even evolved depending on the Adaptee.

Continuous and active research was being conducted on the potential of Trial Items.

The items I had received from NPCs all came with intuitive instructions on how to use them.

However, they possessed a unique rarity in that there had never been a single recorded case of an item linked to an emotion being discovered.

*'The Orca plushie headband I got from Hopdpop isn't an emotion-linked item, though...'*

I wondered if it was bound to me because it was an item created specifically for me.

Since I had already taken the headband out of my inventory, I tried it on for a moment. As I did, I noticed Samra's eyes darting around busily.

*'Does he even record things like this?'*

Anyway, nothing happened when I wore the headband outside the Trial.

I put the headband back into my inventory and examined the other items I possessed.

An item I had bought with Event Star Fragments at the Galactic Trading Post was tucked away in a corner.

I saw the items I'd received from NPCs, ranging from Happy Smile Candy to the Orca Band.

I'd been just hoarding them all.

The instructions—"Makes you feel emotions"—were too vague and seemed dangerous, so I'd never dared to use them.

But now, I thought it might be okay.

After all, I'd grown a bit.

I decided I'd try using the items soon.

After my conversation with Samra, I left work rather late.

By the time I'd cleared Hopdpop and come out, the usual clock-out time had already passed.

I'd secretly hoped I could leave with the Captains, but both of them were frantically busy.

I had no choice but to go home alone.

Still, being alone at home wasn't a bad thing.

I had work to do.

After finishing my shower, I roughly dried my hair and sat on the bed.

Then, I placed an interstellar call to Black Block, making him pay for the call.

It was under the pretext of asking what kind of food he liked before I cooked for him.

Of course, my real purpose was something else. It was Sa Gongyeon.

I planned to ask to be connected with Sa Gongyeon, Black Block's subordinate, to dig up information about Geumcheon.

For what purpose did Geumcheon approach me?

If he simply intended to sell me somewhere, there would be no reason for a high-ranking official like him to forcibly approach me while pretending to be a recruit.

It wasn't even a kidnapping—just a crude attempt to probe us, which only heightened our vigilance.

Geumcheon must have foreseen this outcome, yet he still sent an executive to meet me.

As if he were trying to confirm something.

The thought occurred to me that they might not simply view me as a being of monetary value.

Since this kind of information cannot be objectively quantified into data, it was difficult for Samra to uncover.

So, I tried to dig into Sa Gongyeon's background.

"...?"

I thought he'd pick up immediately since in Hopdpop he was watching.

*'Wasn't he an unemployed alien?'*

I couldn't just let it slide, thinking he might be busy. His track record made me suspicious.

Since I couldn't force him to accept the call, I just gave up.
