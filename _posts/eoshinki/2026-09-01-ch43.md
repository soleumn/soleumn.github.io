<br>As if to say,* 'Why would you even ask such a thing?'* he answered without a moment's hesitation.

The moment I heard his reply, I was struck by a wave of both bewilderment and doubt.

*'When exactly did he start watching me?'*

I couldn't fathom what basis he had for speaking with such absolute certainty.

I had suspected he might have been observing me since my days as a pro gamer. After all, Sa Gongyeon had requested an interview with me right after I retired.

But back then, I was definitely human.

I knew exactly what a god was, so I could judge with absolute clarity.

Honestly, Black Block's words sounded like nothing more than nonsense.

He was a being who could lie and manipulate me as much as he pleased if necessary, so his words carried even less weight.

Still, I kept his words in mind.

Perhaps it was just a difference in perspective.

Black Block was also an ancient god. It was possible he was speaking in a way that was completely different from how I, as a human, understood things. Just as Dominic had described the loss of a part of his being—to fit my human standards—as "losing his memories."

"Are you saying I was a god from the very beginning?"

I threw out a subtle lure to see if I could get even a little more information, but Black Block didn't bite.

Instead of answering, he just chuckled.

I pondered how to get Black Block to give me the answer I wanted, then asked again.

"Then is there no way to preserve my humanity?"

—Little Lamb. God is a being of absolute perfection. Anguish, attachment, sorrow, fleeting memories... To a god, all these are merely impurities to be discarded! The end of humanity is only natural. Just as humans cannot withstand a natural disaster.

Black Block whispered to me, as if sharing a profound secret.

—But perhaps it would be possible if you had a sturdy anchor that wouldn't be swept away by a tsunami!

*'Is he telling me to find something that can preserve my humanity?'*

I wondered if such a thing even existed in this world.

And then, as always, Black Block took the liberty of offering its farewell.

—Little Lamb, my star! I'll be in touch again. As your fan, I will continue to watch you. Well then...

The communication was cut off unilaterally before I could even try to stop him.

I pondered Black Block's words, my heart feeling utterly desolate.

An anchor that would hold me down and keep me steady, even against a calamity like a god.

Lost in thought over the vague concept, I turned to look at Dominic.

"Thank you for your help."

He, who had been on the desk all along, hopped up and onto my shoulder.

Come to think of it, I had promised to contract with Black Block right in front of Dominic.

As I belatedly tried to gauge his reaction, Dominic spoke unexpectedly.

"That one will be useful in the future."

His comment suggested he knew Black Block well.

I never expected him to tell me to use Black Block. I thought he would reject other gods.

As always, it was hard to guess Dominic's true intentions.

Anyway, I was just glad I'd found a way to earn Star Fragments. I didn't know how much money I'd get, but it was better than zero, wasn't it?

*'I want to unfreeze my account before I get the deposit.'*

Just then, there was a knock at the door.

I shot up from my chair.

The instructor who would train the recruits had arrived.

But the person who entered the meeting room was an unexpected figure.

"...Captain Mo?"

Mo Haein, who had clearly left to handle some urgent business, walked in holding a tablet PC.

She said as she closed the door to the meeting room.

"I dumped my work on Captain Kwak."

Another soldier was originally supposed to handle my training, but Mo Haein had come in their place. She also mentioned that Won Taebeom would be the instructor when I trained with the other recruits.

"I promised to teach you how to salute, recruit."

I looked at her, my eyes wide with admiration.

Mo Haein looked up at me and chuckled.

Her special tutoring was efficient and focused, cutting straight to the essentials. Since I already knew a lot of the material, we breezed through the theoretical part of the training.

The hand salute, however, proved more difficult than I expected. Even as I focused on the angle of my hand, the position of my fingers, and the shape of my arm, it still felt slightly awkward.

I couldn't quite pull off a posture as cool as Mo Haein's.

But eventually, I managed to get it to a level she seemed satisfied with.

*'I should show this to Lieutenant Colonel Je and Captain Kwak.'*

I recalled the time I'd saluted Je Hyun-oh, only to be met with a bewildered look. I vowed to restore my honor as a recruit.

After all the training was complete...

I'd learned everything—the tutoring, the proper way to salute—yet Mo Haein didn't announce the end of the lesson. It was as if there was still something left to do.

I waited patiently.

Mo Haein seemed to notice I was waiting. She hesitated for a moment before finally speaking.

"You don't need to worry about what Director Jang said."

She was talking about Jang Seokyun's attempt to hold me back by exploiting her personal history.

Recalling the pink meteorite embedded in Mo Haein's abdomen, I realized this was the moment.

"Captain Mo. I—"

"Han Goyo."

She said my name for the first time.

Before I could even react in surprise, she continued.

"I'll be honest. I'd like you to close the Trial embedded in my stomach. But I don't want to ask a recruit who just enlisted."

*'Then what if I'm not just a recruit?'*

I usually tried to keep a gentle expression in front of the Captains, knowing my face looked a bit sharp when I was expressionless.

But this time, I didn't look away. I met Mo Haein's gaze directly.

"It's probably a Six-star Grade Trial, but my clearing it was pure fluke... Even if the Special Team goes in and shuts it down..."

She watched me steadily, her lips moving.

"They might not be able to get my brother out."

Her words made no sense.

Unless someone had died, shutting down a Trial would release everyone trapped inside. Even the Infection would be reversed.

Mo Haein herself had experienced this firsthand when she and I achieved the True Ending at the &lt;Happy Smile Factory>, saving the employees who had been turned into toys.

I wanted to refute her immediately, knowing better than anyone else why she was saying this. But I held back.

I could tell she wasn't finished speaking.

She spoke, her eyes seeing someone else through me.

"My brother... is a Boss NPC."

The confidence I'd just been radiating—the absolute certainty that I would shut this place down—vanished. I was speechless.

It wasn't unheard of for Adaptees to become NPCs in a Trial. But a Boss Monster? I'd never heard of such a thing.

I couldn't even begin to imagine what Mo Haein must have endured in that Trial.

And in that instant, it all clicked. I finally understood why she'd reacted so viscerally to my friendly behavior toward the NPCs.

Mo Haein suddenly burst out laughing.

"Recruit, you've lost your nerve, haven't you?"

I stared at her as she joked, then slowly asked, "What do you mean?"

"Can't you at least tell me what kind of Trial it is?"

"No. I'll tell you once you've at least shed that 'recruit' tag."

Mo Haein spoke, a trace of a smile still lingering on her face.

"I'll show you a picture of my younger sister instead."

Acting as if it were no big deal, she pulled a photo from the inner pocket of her uniform and held it out.

The four-cut photo showed two women smiling and striking various poses.

"She doesn't really look like me. Not in face, nor in personality."

A tall woman had her arm draped over Mo Haein's shoulder, laughing cheerfully. It was a smile so bright you could almost hear it.

And in the photo, Mo Haein... had long hair.

Staring intently at the picture, I shifted my gaze back to the Mo Haein standing before me.

The woman with the short bob.

My subordinate, her eyes filled with anguish.

*Fury seethed within me.*

"...Excuse me. Just a moment."

I hastily covered my eyes with my hand and took a step back. I could feel my eye color about to change.

Just then, a small hand gripped my wrist.

"...!"

I flinched, but the hand holding me did not let go.

I had the strength to shake it off if I wanted, yet I couldn't move.

Mo Haein slowly applied pressure, pulling my hand down.

I looked down at her, my eyes now glowing gold.

Mo Haein gazed up at me silently and spoke.

"I actually looked it up."

I understood her meaning immediately.

She had looked up my past.

But I never expected what she said next.

"I don't know much about you, so I wanted to learn. You were impressive."

"..."

I froze, unsure of how to react.

It wasn't just because of her candid praise. I never expected Mo Haein to want to know me, just as I wanted to know her.

"It felt unfair that I was the only one who knew about you. That's why I told the recruit my story today."

She let go of my wrist.

"So don't let it get to you."

I didn't answer.

I knew it was disrespectful to a superior, but I couldn't help it. The moment I opened my mouth, I felt like I would swear to shut her out.

Of course, that was impossible for now.

First, I had to get promoted from recruit.

Since we would be operating as a team, thorough preparation was essential. Above all, I had to confirm if I could still pull her out of the Trial even if she became the Boss.

But no matter what, I was determined to be the one to close her Trial.

Mo Haein stared at me in silence. After gazing into my golden eyes for a long moment, she finally spoke.

"Recruit."

Then, as if she had never shared a personal moment with me, she reverted to her usual self.

"Let's practice your salute one last time."

After the training session, I immediately tried to use my newly acquired ability, "Awakening."

But only a notification saying it was unavailable popped up.

<div class="system-window" style="text-align: center;">
  <div class="system-content">
    <strong>This ability cannot be used.</strong>
  </div>
</div>

It seemed I had to meet a certain condition, just like with "Resurrection."

Back then, I had used the ability by breaking myself down.

I couldn't do that now. It was already dangerous enough with my ego being pushed aside.

I decided I'd have to try using Awakening again later, when I entered the Trial with the Special Team.

The housewarming party was held a bit late.

We had waited for Je Hyun-oh to emerge from the Trial, but as soon as he came out, he immediately entered another one.

Kwak Hanmuk, who delivered the news, wasn't even surprised.

"Lieutenant Colonel Je's time is always in high demand, after all."

So many places were requesting his dispatch.

Je Hyun-oh hardly ever took a break, and that was also his own choice. After all, as an Infected, he didn't even need sleep.

The time he had taken me to Paseong before was a truly exceptional case.

But now, I knew he was no longer an Infected.

*'Shouldn't he get some rest, even if it's just a little?'*

Even if his body didn't tire, the mental fatigue must be considerable.

I couldn't stop worrying about him, and the thought kept weighing on my mind, so I sent him a message.

<div class="system-window" style="text-align: center;">
  <div class="system-content">
    <strong>Lieutenant Colonel Je. Please travel safely. I'll buy you a cake when you return.</strong>
  </div>
</div>

After sending the message, a wave of regret washed over me again.

Samra would be absent too.

It was unavoidable, as Samra almost never left the Era Agency. Moving his main body out of the Agency for a housewarming party was, in fact, out of the question. Instead, Samra promised to attend briefly via video.

In the end, only the three of us—me and the Captains—gathered at the house.

Since the "party" was just ordering delivery food together, we decided to take a quick tour of the place before choosing the menu.

Mo Haein, dressed in casual clothes, looked around the house with a hint of curiosity. I had assumed she must have visited the residence at least once, since she lived in the same building and Kwak Hanmuk had originally lived here, but it seemed that wasn't the case.

Come to think of it, given how sparse his belongings were, I realized he probably never had a reason to invite anyone over.

Mo Haein's eyes widened as she saw the glass display cabinet.

I had originally planned to cover it back up with a cloth. But Kwak Hanmuk had asked why I was covering it, so I ended up just leaving it out.

Beside the admiring woman, Kwak Hanmuk said with his arms crossed, "Captain Mo, isn't Goyo-hyung truly respectable? To think we're serving someone like this as a recruit."

Then he turned to me with a mischievous grin.

"My Master is truly impressive."

My face flushed crimson. Mo Haein nudged Kwak Hanmuk's arm with her elbow.

As Kwak Hanmuk yelped as if he'd been struck, she wore an exasperated expression.

"Captain Kwak, stop bullying the recruit."

Then she turned to me and said seriously, "I really think you're cool."

Too shy to even meet her eyes, I murmured, "Thank you."

After that, the three of us spread out a feast and chatted about this and that.

When I mentioned that I'd never had a drink before, they both looked completely shocked.

"...Chestnut, you've never drunk alcohol?"

Ever since I became an adult, I'd been cooped up in my studio apartment, so there was never an occasion. I never had any interest in it, so I didn't even try drinking alone.

As soon as he heard that, Kwak Hanmuk shot to his feet and declared solemnly, "Tonight, I'm going to make Chestnut a true adult."

He rushed outside and bought a whole box of liquor.

—......

Samra materialized, projecting his image into the air. He found a table cluttered with liquor bottles, two captains passed out, and a single recruit sipping a glass of alcohol.

Mo Haein was already speechless. Kwak Hanmuk, however, snapped his head up and shouted at Samra.

"Samra...! That's a damn dangerous Chestnut...!"

Then he slammed his face back onto the table.

I drained the rest of my glass and thought, 'This tastes terrible.'

I couldn't understand why anyone would drink this stuff when it tasted so bad. Still, after this much, I was starting to feel a bit dizzy.

—Mr. Han Goyo, I recommend you stop drinking.

At Samra's advice, I stood up and faced him directly.

His pupils dilated and contracted like a camera aperture.

It seemed he was collecting new biometric data, having never seen me drunk before.

I looked at Samra and said, "It feels empty without you."

—I am right here in front of you, Han Goyo.

"But this is just a video feed, after all."

I reached out. Of course, I felt nothing; my hand passed right through Samra's image.

I gave a wry smile. "I just said it because I was feeling a bit lonely."

For some reason, Samra just watched me in silence.

*'We've had coffee before, so maybe we could have a drink sometime. An Android would be fine with alcohol, right?'*

Lost in these idle thoughts, I tidied up the house under Samra's watchful eye.

I struggled to help the heavy Kwak Hanmuk into his own bedroom. After a moment's thought, I moved Mo Haein to mine. Her place was in the same building, but I figured I'd take her back once she'd sobered up a bit.

Only after I finished cleaning did Samra end the call.

Standing in the middle of the now-spotless living room, I checked my phone.

After sending a brief reply to the message I'd received, I walked over to the window. The night sky was clear, making the moon shine brightly. The city lights drowned out the stars, but...

Drawn by some inexplicable impulse, I stepped out the front door.

I climbed the dimly lit stairs to the rooftop. The moment I opened the rooftop door, my lips parted in a silent gasp.

A man, clad in black, stood there.

He was someone who should not be here.

With his back to the city lights that replaced the invisible stars, the man gazed up at the night sky. He heard the door open, yet he didn't turn his head, his eyes fixed solely on the heavens.

A memory resurfaced—the moment I first met him on the rooftop of the DeZonDeal Broadcasting Station.

And just like then... Je Hyun-oh was still waiting for something.

And yet, *I was here.*

I squeezed my eyes shut, fighting to keep my pupils from changing color.

When I looked back at Je Hyun-oh, he was already staring intently at me. He had already taken off his goggles.

Je Hyun-oh took a step toward me.

I closed the rooftop door and stood there, watching him approach.

Strangely, he seemed unstable.

I felt that if he stumbled even a single step, he would tear off his own wings again.

He had been fine the last time I saw him. I had no idea what could have made him this way.

*'Was the Trial he entered today the problem?'*

It seemed he had cleared the Trial he entered this afternoon in a single go. Je Hyun-oh had always been one to clear them in the shortest time possible, but today, he had been so fast it could be considered a new record.

He should have been transported to the next Trial in a container, but it seemed he had escaped and ended up here.

The Era Agency would be in an uproar right now, as they never would have authorized an Infected's escape.

Yet, I had no intention of sending him back.

I watched Je Hyun-oh anxiously, then tried to say something casual.

"You've returned safely..."

I trailed off. Je Hyun-oh was walking toward me, wearing the Crow Gloves on one hand.

The memory of being slammed against a tree in SL2PL flashed through my mind, and I flinched instinctively.

Just then, he pulled an item from his inventory and held it out to me.

It was the Day News...

My eyes widened at the alien newspaper, which featured a large picture of my face.

*'Did he get this in the Trial he entered today?'*

Are the newspapers updated in real-time even inside the Trials?

How did Je Hyun-oh read the article? I'm only seeing it now.

Je Hyun-oh, seeing my confusion, curved his eyes into crescents.

"Goyo."

He leaned in close and asked, "Want to kill Sa Gongyeon together?"
