<br>As soon as I received Samra's message, I immediately checked Kwak Hanmuk's bedroom, but it was empty. It seemed he hadn't even returned home.

While preparing to enter the Trial, my mind raced with a flurry of thoughts.

When a Meteorite of Trial hatches, its BT Level can be measured. Based on the wavelength it emits, the Era Agency dispatches an Adaptee. The goal is to stabilize and lower the BT Level through periodic clears.

However, some Trials cannot be cleared. If stabilization fails, the Trial explodes, transforming the surrounding area into an environment identical to that of the Trial itself.

Korea has seen a relatively high number of meteorites fall compared to its land area, but fortunately, there has never been a case that led to an explosion.

It was all thanks to the Era Agency and Paseong.

However, there had been several cases of meteorites exploding overseas.

Because meteorites tended to fall in densely populated areas, severe damage was inevitable, and quarantine measures to prevent infection were always required.

Special items were used to create containment zones, but most of those areas were subsequently abandoned. This was because the possibility of the virus leaking out could not be ignored.

*'How can Sa Gongyeon exert influence over the Meteorites of Trial?'*

Contextually, this was clearly Sa Gongyeon's doing. But it defied comprehension that he could manipulate Earth's meteorites at will.

Even if Black Block was pulling the strings from the shadows, the situation remained the same.

If a being from outside Earth could detonate meteorites at will, the difficulty of defending my domain would skyrocket.

*'It shouldn't be such an impossibly one-sided defensive battle...'*

There had to be some kind of rule.

I felt like I could gather more clues once I entered the Trial.

I wanted to ask Dominic, who was hovering right above me, but Sa Gongyeon's message weighed on my mind.

<br>

<span style="color: var(--texto-secundario);">[In exchange for this information, I would like to request that the Little Lamb clear the Trial.]</span>

<br>

Sa Gongyeon had explicitly stated the price for providing the information.

Yet, I couldn't help but wonder if it was right to keep taking information from Dominic without giving anything in return.

It was only a short while ago that I had nearly gouged out Kwak Hanmuk's eyes after following his advice.

"Roasted Chestnut?"

He called my name as if asking what was wrong. I stopped changing into my uniform and replied immediately.

"Nothing, Mr. Dominic."

I was just briefly explaining that I would be entering a new Trial when the phone on the kitchen table started ringing.

I left the dressing room and checked the screen. It was an unknown number.

But somehow, I knew exactly who was calling.

I stared at the screen for a moment before finally answering.

"This is Han Goyo."

—......

The person on the other end said nothing.

*'He was the one who called, yet he remained silent.'*

That was just like him. I spoke his name softly.

"Representative Cha."

A voice, lower and more somber than usual, came through the receiver.

—Han Goyo. Do not enter.

*'How did he know?'*

I wondered if Paseong had been notified due to the emergency.

"We have to enter because of the explosion risk—"

—Sa Gongyeon did this.

As I was momentarily speechless, Representative Cha continued slowly.

—Let it explode. The Republic of Korea won't collapse over a single blast.

That was no way to speak to a recruit from the Era Agency.

I slowly closed and opened my eyes.

The brief flicker of warmth I felt at hearing his voice for the first time in so long was gone in an instant.

"...What the hell?"

CEO Cha hung up the phone.

The entire call hadn't even lasted a minute. He'd just said what he wanted and cut the line—still as arrogant as ever.

I stared at my phone in disbelief before letting out a sigh.

*'Did Sa Gongyeon contact CEO Cha too?'*

I knew Sa Gongyeon was interested in CEO Cha. After all, he'd tried to call him for an interview during the Trial.

But I couldn't fathom why he'd told him about the Trial I was about to enter.

As I headed toward the Era Agency, I tried to guess Sa Gongyeon's motives.

As for CEO Cha... I'd have to contact him again after shutting down COF <small>(City of Frame)</small> .

The Era Agency was ablaze with lights, as bright as day.

Everyone had rushed to work in the early hours due to the emergency.

As I stepped inside, the restless atmosphere was palpable.

I wove through the bustling crowd and headed straight for the Special Team's office.

When I opened the door, Kwak Hanmuk, Mo Haein, and Samra were already there.

Suddenly, Kwak Hanmuk sprang up from his chair.

Then, he declared in a solemn tone, "Captain Mo, Samra, Park Su."

*Clap, clap, clap, clap.*

Mo Haein and Samra joined in, applauding along with Kwak Hanmuk.

I stood there, blinking in confusion, wondering what was going on. Kwak Hanmuk snapped to attention and saluted me.

"Welcome, Sugar Champion."

They had heard the news that I'd cleared Hopdpop.

I never expected such a grand welcome.

I'd expected him to mention it in passing later, maybe asking if I'd cleared it well.

I immediately returned Captain Kwak Hanmuk's salute.

For some reason, he burst out laughing. After a hearty laugh, he said to me, "The recruits who just came out of Hopdpop were all busy talking about you, Goyo."

Kwak Hanmuk clenched his fist and feigned a jab.

"They said you beat everyone up."

Banana and his friends must have spread the word already. I replied, adding a slight exaggeration to the objective truth.

"It was just an inevitable part of the strategy."

"Let's hear more about how Chestnut 'inevitably' acted like a jerk."

"Captain Kwak Hanmuk."

Samra called his name. It was a sign that there was no more time for idle chatter.

Kwak Hanmuk replied, "Yes, sir," and took his seat.

Just then, Mo Haein, who had been sitting quietly, said something that could be taken as either a joke or a serious remark.

"Tell me more about it later."

*'Is she really curious?'*

Deciding he would explain if she asked again later, Kwak Hanmuk focused on Samra's briefing.

"The Trial rating for &lt;City of Frame> is four stars. This will be the Special Mission Team's first entry."

Originally, there had been a civilian Adaptee assigned to this Trial. He was someone the Era Agency had been managing under a freelance contract, but he had recently gone missing without warning.

The situation was highly suspicious.

Adding to his growing suspicions of Sa Gongyeon, Kwak Hanmuk listened to Samra's briefing.

&lt;City of Frame> was an extraction loop-style game. Players would act as stringers— independent freelance journalists—in a sprawling metropolis.

At the start of the game, you choose one of three themes and then find sensational stories to sell to the media. The goal was to start with a radio station and gradually expand into larger media outlets. Along the way, the player uncovers the truth about the being that rules the city.

You enter various districts of the city, secure stories, and then return to your base. I liked this format because each district had its own unique characteristics.

The player's occupation and the existence of a hidden truth were superficially similar to CallOVo, but the game's structure and setting were completely different.

However, now, this slight similarity felt ominous. 

Of all games, it had to be similar to CallOVo. Moreover, the fact that Sa Gongyeon wanted me to clear this Trial made me even more uneasy.

*'And the ending was a bit strange, too.'*

The True Ending of COF left a strange taste in my mouth.

It felt more like a teaser for a sequel, and I remembered asking the Creator about it.

What did the Creator say back then?

They definitely told me...

<br>

*<span style="color: var(--texto-secundario);">"Of course. A sequel will be released just for you, Roasted Chestnut. But it will take some time... So, how about playing other games in the meantime?"</span>*

<br>

But COF 2 never came out, not even by my final stream.

It was hard to believe the Creator had simply lied, especially since the phrase "just for you" kept gnawing at me.

There were too many unsettling details about this Trial.

"Lieutenant Colonel Je Hyun-oh will join us on-site."

The explanation continued: he was currently in another Trial but was expected to emerge soon. If he was delayed, he would join the team later.

I briefly shared the information I knew with Samra and the Captains, and we discussed which theme the Special Team would choose to tackle.

The Special Team reacted with slight surprise to my recommended theme. This was because it was a theme that the Adaptee who had originally managed COF had warned against choosing in the guide shared with the Era Agency. However, after hearing my explanation, everyone understood and agreed to proceed as I suggested.

The location of COF's meteorite was the new headquarters of a major broadcasting station in Sangam-dong, Mapo District, Seoul. Since it was concealed deep underground—a place even the employees were unaware of—the Broadcasting Station Director personally guided us. As a precaution against any unforeseen circumstances, all people from the broadcasting station and its surrounding area had already been evacuated.

The broadcasting station, which should have been bustling with activity, was eerily silent as the Special Team and I descended into the basement with the Director.

After the meteorite fell, the building had been repaired, and the area where COF's meteorite was kept had been disguised as a special soundproof room.

The door to the soundproof room was secured with multiple locks and a sign that read, "Authorized Personnel Only."

As the Director's hands trembled while he unlocked the door, Samra's voice came through my earpiece.

—Han Goyo.

Before I could respond, Samra continued.

—I'll take care of things while you clear &lt;City of Frame>.

*'Take care of things? What did he mean by that?'*

It seemed Samra was truly furious because of Geumcheon.

But as for me, I wasn't particularly worried about Geumcheon.

*They clearly love the golden sky, so they must be my believers.*

...Not for such a strange reason, though.

It was because of my well-founded confidence that they wouldn't pose a significant threat to me.

Since Geumcheon was based overseas rather than in the Republic of Korea, it was difficult to respond immediately. However, once the international organization currently being formed was fully established, we would be able to confront them in earnest.

Furthermore, Samra was in the process of creating an even stronger security system due to this incident.

With my subordinates by my side, I was thinking about it rather leisurely, but Samra didn't seem to be.

I was worried that they might end up putting all of humanity into capsules.

*'When I close this and come out, my body won't be inside a capsule, will it?'*

With a hint of worry, I said to Samra, "I will contact you through the hologram badge."

I felt I should also check in on things myself while reporting to Samra from time to time.

...Whether the Androids were trapping people inside the capsules.

Samra fell silent for a moment after my words, only replying belatedly.

— ...I will wait.

I ended the communication and entered the special soundproof room.

The meteorite embedded in the center of the soundproof room caught my eye. It was as black as a camera body. A white light occasionally flashed through the cracks, each time looking like a camera flash going off.

After observing the meteorite for a moment, Mo Haein offered her opinion.

"Lieutenant Colonel Je says he'll arrive within an hour, but since that's not certain, I think it's best for us to enter first and assess the situation."

Both Kwak Hanmuk and I agreed with her.

Mo Haein placed her hand on the meteorite as a representative and reported briefly.

"Special Team entering."

After the world melted away in fluorescent colors, we entered COF.

I sat alone in a single-person sofa.

On the red fabric, I watched a small TV screen.

I pressed the remote, and the movie began.

A bouncy clarinet sample pierced my ears with a cheerful intro.

Amidst the swing rhythm and decadent bassline, an exaggerated voice shouted a welcome.

<br>

<div class='f-cormorant'>
  <i>"Welcome! To Hyper Eden, the media city of pleasure!"</i>
</div>

<br>

The crisp, snapping hi-hats characteristic of swing music broke into finer pieces, building the tension.

Flashing neon signs, champagne popping with white foam, the noisy spin of a casino roulette wheel, and glimpses of someone fighting behind a fast-food joint—all flashed by in rapid succession.

As the music reached its build-up, a heavy bass gradually swelled.

<br>

<div class='f-cormorant'>
  <i>"Hyper Eden is a place filled with the most stimulating and thrilling moments!"</i>
</div>

<br>

*Boom!*

With the drop, an explosive surge of electronic bass and a brass section poured out.

The screen widened, revealing a sweeping panorama of Hyper Eden.

The dazzling city lights were a fleeting glimpse before the scene shifted to a gunfight erupting in the heart of the metropolis.

Amidst a barrage of curses and screams, reporters swarmed the scene to cover the shootout.

Gunfire tore through the air, and pools of blood from the casualties stained the ground, yet the photographers pressing their shutters remained completely indifferent.

The flashes of gunfire and camera strobes pulsed in time with the upbeat brass melody—*pop, pop*—as even the bloodstains shimmered brilliantly under the lights.

The scene shifted. With the kick of a swing beat, billboards covering the entire exterior of a skyscraper came into view.

The images on the billboards began to flicker and change in real-time.

The chaotic stream of advertisements vanished in an instant. In their place, sensational breaking-news headlines and footage of the gunfight were broadcast live.

Beneath the neon lights, the crowd erupted in laughter and cheers. Their faces flushed crimson with rage. They wept in despair.

Everyone was spellbound, captivated by the glow of the billboards, completely dominated by the information they spewed.

It was a horrifying sight, yet the upbeat music playing throughout made it all seem perfectly natural.

The scene shifted again.

No, it wasn't a scene anymore.

"I" realized I had somehow entered Hyper Eden, now trapped inside the very TV screen I had been watching.

I had just been a spectator, consuming this space as a mere spectacle.

Startled, I spun around, my eyes darting everywhere. Then, I noticed a voice recorder in my hand.

As I stared blankly at the unfamiliar device, a voice laced with ridicule echoed through the air.

<br>

<div class='f-cormorant'>
  <i>"In Hyper Eden, you begin as miserable trash."</i>
</div>

<br>

The cyberpunk cityscape that had dominated my vision was gone.

Instead, I stood in a back alley overflowing with garbage and vomit, where rats scurried past my feet.

My gaze was forcibly yanked away.

I looked up at a building in the heart of Hyper Eden—a tower that soared alone, dwarfing the skyscrapers around it.

<br>

<div class='f-cormorant'>
  <i>"Cover the story! Get the public's attention! And then, climb higher! That is the only way to survive in Hyper Eden."</i>
</div>

<br>

The instruments that had been playing so cheerfully all at once stopped.

The jazz brass and electronic bass that had been pounding in my ears vanished as if by magic, replaced by a silence so heavy it was suffocating.

The voice that had been chattering so loudly just a moment ago now asked a question, its tone calm and low.

<br>

<div class='f-cormorant'>
  <i>"It is time to choose, Stringer. How will you survive?"</i>
</div>

<br>

And then, options appeared before me.

I had barely regained my free will, yet I stared at the choices with bewildered eyes.

COF's options were always three.

But on the screen before me, a fourth had been added.

<div class="system-window" style="text-align: center;">
  <div class="system-content">
    <strong>> Sports<br>
> Crime<br>
> Mystery<br>
> Adult</strong>
  </div>

Adult?

I tapped the unfamiliar option to read its description.

*Beep!* A warning window popped up.

<div class="system-window" style="text-align: center;">
  <div class="system-content">
    <strong>Age verification required!</strong>
  </div>
</div>

I couldn't even read the description.

*'Is this also because I'm registered as a child?'* I wondered.

Just then, the Pudding Slime appeared at the perfect moment.

"Mr. Dominic!"

The Slime, who had just materialized out of thin air...

...Was the most adult being I knew.
