<br>Mo Haein looked up at the figure encased within the glass pillar.

It was an unfamiliar experience.

The one who had always been asleep was her.

Whenever she opened her eyes, Ham Jiwol was always there, recording data. Sometimes Kwak Hanmuk was with her.

Enter the Trial, survive, enter again, survive again.

Whenever a moment came that she felt she couldn't endure, she would fall asleep and wake up.

And then repeat. It was a monotonous life, if one could call it that.

She lived clinging to the hope that if she kept entering the Trial, she might one day find a way to shut it down.

She knew all too well how futile it was, yet she couldn't let go.

She figured that since letting go wouldn't change anything, she might as well hold on.

Then, a meteorite named Han Goyo crashed into her life.

After staring at the glass pillar for a long time, Mo Haein lowered her head.

He took a shallow breath, exhaled slowly, and turned around.

Je Hyun-oh, Kwak Hanmuk, and Samra—they were all still there, rooted to the spot in the lab where the glass pillar stood.

The directors of Paseong had agreed to wait just outside the Era Agency for 72 hours. They had been so reluctant to leave that he'd only managed to get them out after making them sign contracts reserved exclusively for Qualifieds.

Ham Jiwol was still prowling the Research Department, her eyes gleaming like a zombie's, but Samra had sealed the entrance to prevent her from coming in.

The Special Team could afford to have one or two members absent until the promised three days were up. Yet, none of them could bring themselves to leave, remaining completely fixed before the glass pillar.

While Samra could handle his duties from anywhere, it was a different story for Je Hyun-oh, who had to enter the Trial.

The loss incurred by his mere presence here was incalculable.

Mo Haein and Kwak Hanmuk knew this all too well, which was why the Era Agency had desperately tried to persuade them to move.

But the attempts at persuasion were short-lived.

The moment Je Hyun-oh flapped his metallic wings twice, everyone who had come to "persuade" him fled in terror.

Surprisingly, Chief Jang Seokyun did not step in. He seemed to have judged that it was best not to provoke Je Hyun-oh any further.

Once the commotion subsided, only the Special Team remained.

In the newfound silence, Kwak Hanmuk flopped onto the floor. He ran a hand through his hair, messing it up, and asked Je Hyun-oh, "How did we get out of there?"

*Clack, clack.* The sound of metallic wings folding echoed.

Hiding his wings, Je Hyun-oh clicked his metal hand and replied, "Thanks to Trainee Han's efforts."

"Lieutenant Colonel Je... Han Goyo has just been pronounced dead. Is that all you have to say?"

Kwak Hanmuk's voice was harsh, but Je Hyun-oh didn't respond.

Mo Haein studied the silent man. She knew well that he was the type to withhold what needed to be said. Even the Era Agency left him alone, never asking the questions they should have.

He had always existed on a precarious edge. Everyone took great care not to accidentally provoke him, yet even when left undisturbed, his illness would occasionally flare up.

*'He could just be having another episode,'* she thought, *'and that's why he's silent.'* But this felt different. It was as if he were deliberately hiding something.

*'But for what? Why would he need to keep secrets even now, when things have come to this?'*

And it didn't feel like an episode at all. In fact, Je Hyun-oh looked much healthier than usual.

I was glaring at him, trying to gauge his intentions, when he spoke.

"I have received a detailed report from Lieutenant Won Taebeom regarding the circumstances of the Trial."

Kwak Hanmuk and Mo Haein both turned to look at Samra.

Samra had already given up hope on Je Hyun-oh and had contacted Won Taebeom instead.

"However, the report was severely lacking in objectivity and credibility. Captain Kwak Hanmuk, please report what you remember."

*'Won Taebeom was rambling...'* I couldn't believe it, even though Samra was the one saying it.

*'His condition seemed strange earlier, too.'*

While Mo Haein was briefly thinking about Won Taebeom, Kwak Hanmuk still hadn't started his report.

It was completely unlike him.

When he finally began, his words were far from clear. He stumbled several times, his voice trailing off as he recounted the final moments.

"How the hell did he deal with that tentacle spawn..."

Kwak Hanmuk muttered, his brow furrowing.

"It was similar to the one sealed inside me."

"...!"

Mo Haein knew why Kwak Hanmuk had the tattoo.

But she only knew that it sealed some kind of Transcendental Being; she never knew exactly what it was.

Kwak Hanmuk had kept that a secret from everyone.

Samra’s eyes flashed even more brilliantly.

The man, whose eyes glowed so intensely she wondered if he was about to overload, spoke.

"Before entering this Trial, Han Goyo defined himself as the System in a conversation with Captain Kwak Hanmuk."

Since Samra had been carrying an electronic device at the time, their entire conversation had been transmitted to him.

Hearing those words, Mo Haein finally understood the strange occurrences she'd witnessed. It seemed plausible that Han Goyo could have used his abilities so recklessly against the Special Team during the SL2PL boss battle if he were the System himself.

But Samra was formulating a different hypothesis.

"However, many things still remain unexplained, Captain Kwak Hanmuk. Was the prediction that he would be revived after 72 hours a message conveyed by Mr. Han Goyo using his abilities?"

"...That's right."

"Confirmed."

The holographic glow in Samra's eyes, which had been flashing continuously, slowly faded—as if he had finished his calculations.

"Taking into account all the current events, the abilities Mr. Han Goyo possesses, and the fact that he can interfere with the Trial and its Qualified candidates, the definition of a Transcendental Being is not appropriate. For Mr. Han Goyo, the most accurate definition is—"

Samra declared, his eyes glowing with a faint, ethereal light.

"A god."

A chilling silence enveloped the laboratory.

*'A god...?'*

The word was too vast, too incomprehensible. Mo Haein found herself speechless.

But Samra wasn't finished.

"If Mr. Han Goyo truly returns to life, we will have no choice but to definitively identify him as a god."

Samra's conclusion was absolute.

"This is my judgment as Samra."

At that moment, a wave of emotion surged through Mo Haein. It was a visceral rejection of something beyond human comprehension.

The very concept of the System was difficult to grasp, yet it didn't feel entirely alien. After all, it was something she encountered every time she entered a Trial.

If you were a Suitable Candidate, you were bound to receive quests, use items, and see the System Window.

After all, the Trial Response Agency was a national organization established to counter national disasters and threats caused by Transcendental Beings.

Han Goyo himself had been brought to the Agency at the very beginning because he was suspected of being a Transcendental Being.

So, if the System was described as a Transcendental Being, similar to the Trials or the NPCs within them, it could be roughly understood.

But a *god*... That felt too different.

The very concept was so uncertain, so immeasurable and unknown, that it even evoked a hint of terror.

"What the fuck kind of bullshit is this?"

Kwak Hanmuk's foul language spoke for Mo Haein's feelings.

He, who had been sitting on the floor until then, shot to his feet.

"Samra. I'm not cursing at you. And I'm not questioning your judgment."

Kwak Hanmuk took a few deep breaths, as if trying to calm his emotions.

Even so, his words came out in a disjointed mess.

"How is Han Goyo a god? It doesn't make any sense. I mean, what kind of power? Sure, he used it a little, but it wasn't even that impressive... Just sending messages and veiling heads, that's all. And he's only twenty-four. Don't you need to be thousands of years old to be a god or something?"

Kwak Hanmuk, who had been speaking in a rapid, harsh tone, swallowed another curse.

"Recalculate, Samra."

"That is unnecessary. All possibilities have already been examined."

"Hah..."

Kwak Hanmuk strode toward the laboratory door.

He seemed to be going outside to clear his head for a moment.

But he just stood there, rooted to the spot before the door, before finally turning back around.

Even now, he couldn't bring himself to leave.

Standing there in a daze, Mo Haein looked up again at the being she had been staring at just moments ago.

Of course, nothing had changed.

Han Goyo, a twenty-four-year-old recruit of the Era Agency.

He was exactly the same.

Then, Je Hyun-oh spoke.

"Captain Kwak, Captain Mo."

He pushed his goggles up, revealing his eyes. Then, he met their gaze with those golden-pupiled eyes.

It was an unfamiliar sight.

Je Hyun-oh rarely showed his eyes unless he was in front of Han Goyo.

A slow, deliberate voice flowed from beneath his gas mask.

"Are you okay with the dead coming back to life?"

Only then did Mo Haein realize.

The very fact that he was so certain the dead could return to life was not normal.

Je Hyun-oh lightly tapped the glass pillar with his metallic hand.

"It's not that big of a deal, so don't worry. It's still nothing much."

He spoke with a trace of a smile in his voice, as if it were no matter at all.

"Look at it now. It's just trapped inside a glass pillar. If it weren't for us, it would have been sliced into pieces. It's weak, young..."

*Screech.* His sharp fingertips scratched the surface of the glass pillar.

Mo Haein tensed instinctively. She felt as if he might shatter the pillar and drag Han Goyo out at any moment.

Je Hyun-oh spoke softly, leaving a long scratch behind.

"It's a recruit that the captains need to protect."

Mo Haein felt as if she were being torn in half.

The moment she heard Samra's words, she felt a strong aversion to the being called a 'god.' Yet, at the same time, she felt an overwhelming protective instinct to save Han Goyo by any means necessary.

As if it were a completely separate issue.

The origin of this irrational state was easy to guess.

Something had gone wrong from the moment the System Window appeared, announcing that Han Goyo had registered her, and she had been draped in the golden veil.

Of course, she had been mindful of him ever since she first brought him to the Era Agency, but it had never been this intense.

*'Then are all these feelings caused by the god's power?'*

But...

Mo Haein knew Han Goyo.

The face that looked at her with eyes full of affection, letting it be known that he was overjoyed to be on the same team.

The strange kid who even liked Je Hyun-oh, whom everyone else avoided and shunned.

A bit of a fool, someone who often did things that were out of his mind, but...

A recruit who could find a shockingly bold and original way out of any crisis, yet would be the first to consider abandoning others if they were all cornered together.

*'Han Goyo had been the reason they had closed the Trials.'* That was an undeniable truth.

Because of Han Goyo, Mo Haein was gradually finding a glimmer of light in a hope she had once considered futile.

Mo Haein, lost in thought, snapped back to reality at the sound of a voice.

"The recruit even took a deathly blow to protect his seniors," Je Hyun-oh muttered, looking at Kwak Hanmuk with a deeply regretful expression. "You're both being so cold, hyung, noona."

Kwak Hanmuk, who had been clutching his tattooed left shoulder, let out a hollow laugh.

Mo Haein could tell that he, too, was wavering between the same feelings she had: a mix of repulsion and a protective instinct.

Seventy-two hours passed in a blur of confusion.

In the slow-drifting time, both Kwak Hanmuk and Mo Haein agonized. No matter how much they pondered, no clear answer emerged.

Meanwhile, Je Hyun-oh and Samra dropped hints, trying to sway them.

"We suspect there are beings beyond Earth that should be defined as gods," Samra said. "Han Goyo himself couldn't have appeared without cause."

"..."

"Han Goyo is essential."

Samra continued to present objective information, emphasizing Han Goyo's importance.

In contrast, Je Hyun-oh acted as if it were a simple problem.

"In the end, it's all about Han Goyo."

He spoke as if the matter of gods were a trivial detail, acting as if he couldn't understand why they were even hesitating.

"It's not a matter of choosing one of the two. It's all Han Goyo. What's the problem?"

His attempt to sound rational only made him more suspicious.

Kwak Hanmuk was acting as if he'd half-lost his mind.

Sometimes he would just stand there, his head pressed against a glass pillar. Other times, he would spend hours blankly rolling a single prayer bead.

But Mo Haein was in no state to look after him. She was already struggling just to process the information she had been given.

When the remaining time dwindled to single digits, Kwak Hanmuk was the first to question Je Hyun-oh.

"Lieutenant Colonel Je. Tell me everything you know. What's going to happen to... Han Goyo?"

Je Hyun-oh chuckled darkly, as if he'd just been asked a fascinating question.

"I don't know either. He's still young, so wouldn't he be heavily influenced by his surroundings?"

Mo Haein frowned at the subtle answer.

It was an odd choice of words to use for a grown man. It sounded as if a divine ego existed separately from him.

Je Hyun-oh, who had been leaning against a glass pillar, unfurled a single metallic wing and wrapped it around the pillar.

"If the captains refuse, I'll stay alone."

At that moment, Kwak Hanmuk and Mo Haein locked eyes.

They still couldn't fully understand or accept all of this, but one thing was certain: they could not leave Han Goyo in Je Hyun-oh's hands.

"Captain Kwak Hanmuk, Captain Mo Haein."

At the same time, Samra called their names.

"I propose a plan to protect Han Goyo."

Sensing the shift in the atmosphere, he made his offer at the most opportune moment.

"The Special Team must be preserved."

"..."

Kwak Hanmuk said nothing. Instead, he stared at Han Goyo, who was encased in the glass pillar.

The man who had been looking up at the being defined as a god suddenly spat out a curse.

"Ha, fine, fuck. I guess we'll have to lock him up on the right side this time."

Then he turned to Mo Haein and asked, "Captain Mo. Are you alright?"

"...I."

Mo Haein slowly spoke.

After being tormented by countless thoughts for 72 hours, she had ultimately returned to her original starting point.

It was an irrational, illogical, and unscientific conclusion.

"I just want to stay by the recruit's side."
