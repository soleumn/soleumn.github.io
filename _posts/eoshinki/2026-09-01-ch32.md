<br>It took me a moment to process the words that had just reached my ears.

I stared blankly at Je Hyun-oh for a few seconds before grabbing his wrist.

I pulled him with all my might, and he came along without a fight. I dragged him into the room and slammed the door shut behind us.

Je Hyun-oh stood by the door, exactly where I'd pulled him.

His face remained impassive, betraying none of the weight of the words he had just uttered.

Meanwhile, my heart was pounding wildly against my ribs in shock.

I pulled out my phone and showed it to him.

When I showed him the screen, Samra's name visible, as if to ask if it was okay for him to hear, Je Hyun-oh replied simply, "It's fine."

My lips parted slightly, and I asked in a trembling voice, "What do you mean by that? Not infected?"

Je Hyun-oh just stared at me, his eyes saying 'I meant exactly what I said'.

I resented his personality, which always made him so reluctant to explain things.

But my resentment wouldn't suddenly transform him into a helpful, explanatory crow.

I tried to process the given information as calmly as possible.

"Your current human form... you used an item, didn't you?"

Je Hyun-oh gave a slight nod.

That meant his true nature was a body transformed into metal.

If so, why had everyone—Ham Jiwol, the Research Analysis Headquarters, and even Samra—concluded he was an infected?

On reflection, it was a situation where anyone could have made that mistake.

Je Hyun-oh had never reported anything about &lt;Zero> to the Era Agency, and all the other compatible individuals who entered &lt;Zero> afterward had died.

With the sole survivor providing no information, the Era Agency couldn't obtain any data about &lt;Zero> at all.

Furthermore, the infected were originally required to stay in the Research Department for a certain period and cooperate in live experiments for virus research.

It was common to develop vaccines and cures based on this.

However, Je Hyun-oh refused to let anyone lay a single finger on his body and even attacked researchers who became a nuisance.

Samra had no choice but to make a judgment based on the big data of other Trials, and thus defined Je Hyun-oh as an infected.

Since Je Hyun-oh himself said nothing about it, the wrong judgment became solidified.

If Samra had just heard Je Hyun-oh's words, his CPU would probably feel like it was burning up.

This was because he had researched various things based on the premise that Je Hyun-oh was an infected.

The fact that the Laws of Trials did not apply to Je Hyun-oh alone was also considered to be because his level of infection was severe.

But on second thought, there had never been a case of an infected person as severely mutated as Je Hyun-oh entering another Trial—not in the entire world.

Mutations of that degree resulted in extreme aggression and a complete loss of reason. Just like the zombies of DeZonDeal.

Until now, Je Hyun-oh had been considered a special exception who retained his sanity. But it made more sense to assume he had never been infected at all.

That wasn't the only thing that was strange. When a person became infected, the infected individual would exhibit the characteristics of that Trial.

Yet the lore and settings of &lt;Zero> that I knew didn't really match Je Hyun-oh's black metal.

After processing all these thoughts, I reached a clear conclusion.

*'Je Hyun-oh is not infected.'*

This inevitably led me to a new question.

What had transformed his body?

If it wasn't the Infection, then it couldn't have been a &lt;Zero> item either.

Knowing he wouldn't answer, I still spoke.

"Then why has your body changed, Lieutenant Colonel?"

As expected, Je Hyun-oh remained silent.

I gritted my teeth.

More than the question of what had changed his body, another thought kept surfacing, growing stronger with each moment.

I had seen him tearing at his own metallic wings in a self-destructive frenzy.

Yet, even while suffering so terribly, Je Hyun-oh had said…



<span style="color: var(--texto-secundario);">“I like &lt;Zero>, Goyo.”</span>



He had said he *liked* &lt;Zero>.

Until now, I had secretly assumed his incomprehensible affection for &lt;Zero> was a result of the Infection.

But if it wasn't the Infection, then why did he like &lt;Zero>?

*Why?*

When it had so utterly ruined his body and mind.

*'Could the god Je Hyun-oh is searching for be inside &lt;Zero>?'*

If he wants to enter &lt;Zero> with me just to meet that god...

*No!*

*I can never allow it.*

*My subordinate must look only at me.*

*Even if it is out of hatred.*

The thoughts that flooded my mind were agonizing. I tried to shake off this socially maladapted way of thinking, but it was useless.

The best I could do was keep my thoughts to myself.

I bowed my head low and closed my eyes, hiding the changed color of my pupils.

But Je Hyun-oh wouldn't leave me alone.

"Goyo."

His voice, which I still wasn't used to, called my name.

I replied, my eyes squeezed shut.

"Yes."

"What's wrong?"

"...I'm afraid you're about to say something strange."

At that moment, I felt the presence of the person who had been standing by the door draw closer.

Then came a light tap on my chest.

It was a human hand, not a metallic one, so the fingertips were rounded and warm.

A wave of unfamiliarity washed over me as I was forced to open my eyes.

Je Hyun-oh was looking down at me, his expression strangely satisfied. Staring into his dark purple eyes, devoid of any gold, I couldn't hold back any longer.

"Tell me why you switched bodies. If you just drop a bombshell like this and say nothing else, I'll be thinking about it all day..."

"Perfect."

"Lieutenant Colonel Je!"

He said the one thing I least wanted to hear.

"I'll tell you once we're inside &lt;Zero>."

"...No."

I glared at him, fighting to keep my surging emotions in check.

"Tell me now."

Instead of answering, Je Hyun-oh simply crinkled the eyes visible above his mask.

The moment I saw that smile, the words finally escaped my lips.

*"I'm the master, after all."*

And I regretted it instantly.

*'I've lost it.'*

I'd been defeated by a social misfit.

I immediately bowed my head and apologized to him.

"I'm sorry."

A low laugh fell upon the back of my head.

He was laughing. As if he were genuinely amused and enjoying himself.

*'Why is he laughing?'*

Even if he hadn't been infected in &lt;Zero>, his mind was clearly broken.

I stared at him as he continued to laugh silently, then rolled my eyes away to avoid his gaze.

I figured Je Hyun-oh was partly to blame for my crazy outburst.

I didn't want to rush into &lt;Zero> and risk losing my subordinate. I wanted to learn everything about Je Hyun-oh—every single detail—and fully understand what had transformed his body and what kind of god he was searching for before I entered.

Honestly, judging by Dominic's and ■■■'s cases, the god Je Hyun-oh was seeking didn't seem like the type to treat its subordinates well.

Sure, he might have more Star Fragments than I do.

*'I can just earn them later.'*

I still don't know how to earn Star Fragments, though... or how to open a Cosmic Account...

Still, considering my future potential, I was confident I would become greater than the god Je Hyun-oh was searching for.

As I was recalling the Servant Items I had added to my cart at the Galactic Trading Post, Je Hyun-oh called my name.

"Goyo."

"Yes..."

"Let's go."

In the end, I had learned nothing except the fact that Je Hyun-oh was not an Infected.

I followed him, feeling a bit gloomy.

*'What do I have to do to get him to tell me more?'*

I had so many questions.

*'I want to know why he wears the Crow Set.'*

I was curious why he insisted on wearing the Crow Set even though there were items that allowed him to take on a human form.

I suspected there were more effects beyond just physical changes and stat boosts, but since the Crow Gloves were the only item I'd actually read the description for, I couldn't even begin to guess.

I headed down to the underground parking lot with Je Hyun-oh, my mind racing with a hundred questions I wanted to ask him.

Yet, on the way down, no one recognized him. Passersby merely glanced at him with the curiosity they'd show a complete stranger. Everyone only knew Je Hyun-oh as the man with the metallic hand. It seemed that seeing him use a humanoid item was an extremely rare occurrence.

The car we were taking was a black sedan. It looked ordinary enough, but as a military vehicle operated by the Era Agency, it featured deep tinting and seamless bulletproof armor plating across the entire body.

As I pondered how to get more information out of Je Hyun-oh, I typed the address into the navigation system...

I punched in the address.

Then I experienced a ride so intense it wiped every other thought from my mind...

*Screeech!*

The sedan slid into a perfect parallel park, its tires leaving a faint skid mark as it slotted into place like a puzzle piece.

Finally out of the car, I clung to the passenger-side door with trembling hands.

At this moment, there was only one person I desperately missed.

*'Captain Kwak!'*

I yearned for Kwak Hanmuk, who had driven with such stability, even drifting through hordes of zombies. He was a Formula One driver in every sense of the word.

Je Hyun-oh, on the other hand, was a Formula One driver in the worst way possible.

I never wanted to ride in a car driven by him ever again.

As I stood there, feeling the ground sway beneath me, Je Hyun-oh stepped out of the driver's seat and gave me a puzzled look.

Only then did I remember—he was a crow that flew like a fighter jet.

Still, I never imagined he'd drive a car like a fighter jet.

I desperately needed the physical buffs granted in the Trial.

Forcing my trembling legs to hold, I walked forward.

The building I lived in was quiet, with few tenants. It was a relatively new construction with reasonable rent, but for some reason, the number of tenants had plummeted after the landlord changed.

Then again, the neighborhood itself was always a quiet place.

I punched in the passcode and opened the steel door.

I scanned the familiar space with a strange sense of detachment.

In reality, I'd only been away from home for a few months. Yet, it felt as if I'd returned after several years.

The time I'd spent in the Trial probably contributed to that feeling.

The studio apartment, with its living and sleeping areas separated, was the place I'd hidden away in for the past four years. A large cabinet covered with a cloth to hide its contents took up one entire wall of the living room, and in the corner, a small single-person table was placed in the corner.

In the bedroom, there was a bed, a computer, a small wardrobe, and a single chest of drawers. That was the extent of the furniture I owned.

*'I'll just take my clothes and get rid of the rest...'*

I stared blankly at the cloth-covered display cabinet before turning around.

Je Hyun-oh was so tall that his presence seemed to fill the entire studio apartment, all the way to the ceiling. With his arms hanging at his sides, he habitually tapped his fingers a couple of times, as if playing a piano.

I asked him, with a hint of ulterior motive, "Would you like a glass of water?"

In response, Je Hyun-oh looked at me with an expression that would have surely displayed a "^^" emoticon if he had been wearing goggles.

I quietly slipped into the bedroom.

Samra had instructed that nothing be touched except for light cleaning, and indeed, everything remained exactly as it was.

I picked up the phone that had fallen next to the computer chair.

I had vanished for seven days, starting from February 29th—the Creator's final broadcast day.

Then, on March 7th, I entered &lt;Happy Smile Factory> and met Mo Haein.

The memories of those seven days were a complete blank.

I couldn't find a single clue about what I had done.

Thinking about that distant past, I plugged the dead phone into the charger and turned it on.

I didn't expect to be flooded with missed messages...

But a barrage of message previews popped up. They were all from the same person.

I hesitated before checking them.

I had left him unblocked, just curious how long he would keep remembering me and reaching out.

*'But why did he send so many messages?'*

From February 29th, the day I disappeared, to March 6th, he had sent a message every single day.
