<br>His eyes were smiling, but it wasn't hard to read his mood.

Because his gaze wasn't smiling at all.

I held my breath as I looked up at Je Hyun-oh.

Je Hyun-oh just stared at me, not even blinking.

It seemed he would keep staring like that until I said something.

"Um... Lieutenant Colonel Je. Please calm down for a moment..."

Je Hyun-oh's smile deepened.

The fact that he looked particularly *not* okay made me even more worried and scared.

*'Should I cast Golden Veil on him?'*

I was seriously breaking out in a cold sweat.

I felt as if the alcohol that had been circulating in my body just moments ago had completely vanished.

I had intended to have a proper conversation with Sa Gongyeon.

I wondered how long he had been watching me and wanted to ask about Geumcheon.

But killing Sa Gongyeon was never an option. I never even considered that a low-level god like me could take on a subordinate of the Black Block. My only plan was to manipulate the Black Block and use Sa Gongyeon.

*'Lieutenant Colonel Je actually read the Day News...'*

My mind went blank, completely blindsided by a situation I'd never even considered. Besides, he knew too much. I couldn't even guess the extent of his knowledge, making it impossible to know what to hide or how.

As my silence stretched on, Je Hyun-oh placed his metal hand steadily against the rooftop door.

*Creeeeak...*

The sound of something slowly scraping down the door echoed eerily right beside me.

For a fleeting moment, I wanted to cast a restriction spell and flee. But no matter how hard I tried to escape, he would catch up to me in just a few wingbeats.

I gave up on all hope and answered.

"You can't kill them..."

"Why?"

I felt as if the metal hand would scratch *me* this time if I said something he didn't like. I forced out a reply, trying to downplay the truth as much as possible.

"I gave them permission."

It was the best answer I could come up with on the spot, but it didn't elicit a positive response. The metal hand scraped the rooftop door once more.

Groaning under the silent demand for an explanation, I continued.

"It's just... it was necessary for the operation of Earth..."

I glanced at the metal hand resting right next to my face before adding, "I made a deal to provide photos in exchange for a small reward."

As I explained that the contract was already finalized, Je Hyun-oh tilted his head slightly.

*'I'm sorry, Lieutenant Colonel. I need to earn those Star Fragments.'* I apologized fervently in my head.

Just then, a low voice called my name.

"Goyo."

"Yes...?"

"Should I do the same?"

"What?"

My voice pitched up in surprise.

He turned and strode across the rooftop, leaving me behind. The moment I saw him preparing to unfurl his metal wings, I knew.

He was about to lose his mind.

I rushed after Je Hyun-oh, grabbed him, and shouted, "I didn't actually want to do this!"

Je Hyun-oh stopped in his tracks. The truth immediately began to pour out of my mouth.

"It's just... the circumstances really left me with no choice. There was this god who had been watching me for a long time..."

In the end, I confessed everything about the Black Block. After a lengthy explanation, I added a plea, trying to sound as pitiful as possible.

"I'm still so weak. I lack the ability... I couldn't do anything about it, so I just thought it would be good to at least earn some money to use in the universe."

"..."

Je Hyun-oh didn't answer. He kept his back to me for a moment before finally turning around.

The moment our eyes met, my heart sank.

It was because I had realized I'd made my subordinate anxious.

Without thinking, I found myself speaking.

*"I'm sorry..."*

Then, I draped the golden veil over him.

Je Hyun-oh said nothing. He simply closed his eyes slowly and opened them again.

Usually, whenever I cast the Golden Veil, he would stare at the veil itself. But this time, he looked at me.

Meeting his gaze, I wondered.

What is Je Hyun-oh?

Did he learn everything he knows from &lt;Zero>?

But Je Hyun-oh knew too many truths, and he possessed abilities I didn't even know about.

Everything was ambiguous, yet one thing was certain: Je Hyun-oh was my subordinate, and I had to take responsibility for him.

I made a quiet promise to him.

"I'll get stronger quickly, so this won't happen again."

The Golden Veil dissolved. Even amidst the shimmering golden dust, Je Hyun-oh's gaze remained fixed on me.

A strange sense of satisfaction washed over me, knowing his eyes were no longer searching the sky.

As our gaze lingered, a screen materialized in the air.

—Lieutenant Colonel Je Hyun-oh. You must return now.

I had wondered why the Era Agency was unusually quiet. It seemed Samra had been cooperating.

Je Hyun-oh donned his goggles once more.

Just as I breathed a sigh of relief, finally escaping the crisis, a low, rough whisper reached my ears.

"What about the cake?"

Startled, I looked up at him.

I had sent a message promising to buy him cake upon his return. But I never expected him to come for it so soon, and I was empty-handed.

I hadn't even imagined he would remember my message, let alone ask about it.

"I don't have any right now. Should I go buy some? If you could just wait a moment—"

I was about to rush off to a nearby bakery when he reached out and blocked my path.

Bumping into his solid forearm, I looked up at Je Hyun-oh.

*'...Ah.'*

His words from before—how he hated waiting—finally came back to me.

I hesitated before speaking. "I'll have some ready next time."

An emoticon appeared on Je Hyun-oh's goggles as he took a step back.

Then, he turned and walked toward the railing, spreading his wings.

I imagined him soaring into the city's night skyline.

But instead of taking flight, Je Hyun-oh vanished, plummeting straight down the side of the building.

I rushed to the railing and scanned the area below, but saw nothing.

I was left alone on the rooftop.

I stayed on the rooftop for a long time to clear my head. When I finally returned home, Kwak Hanmuk was already awake.

He told me he'd just gotten back from dropping Mo Haein off at her place.

"Where were you?" he asked.

Unsure if I should mention Je Hyun-oh's unauthorized departure, I simply replied that I'd gone out for some fresh air.

Kwak Hanmuk didn't press further. Instead, he praised me for tidying up the house so thoroughly.

We drank hangover cures together and went to bed early.

When I woke up the next morning, I felt perfectly fine.

I wasn't entirely sure, but it seemed I had a high tolerance for alcohol.

Seeing me so refreshed and hangover-free, Kwak Hanmuk added a new entry to his "night series."

"So you're not a chestnut, but an alcohol bomb..."

He shook his head in disbelief as he brought out a dessert for his morning hangover cure.

I thought he was more impressive for eating dessert first thing in the morning, but I kept the thought to myself.

I had to report to the Era Agency today, as the recruit training was officially beginning.

As I dressed in my uniform, I checked my phone. I had thought the messages would end last night, but another one had arrived.

*'What a piece of work,'* I thought, replying before heading out with Kwak Hanmuk, who had already finished getting ready.

As we walked side by side toward the Era Agency, people stole glances at us. They seemed to be staring because our uniforms stood out.

Everyone in this area knew there was a military facility here; they just didn't know exactly what it did.

Since the existence of the Trials had to remain a secret, I'd heard the Era Agency was posing as a new military branch to the public.

"The Era Agency uniform is pretty cool."

It wasn't because I liked archive games; objectively, it was a stylish uniform.

Kwak Hanmuk, walking beside me, muttered in a mournful tone, "I'm gonna have to pull a shit-ton of overtime starting today..."

That meant all the work he'd put off while partying yesterday had piled up. He'd been complaining about being busy lately, but I wondered when he'd ever get a break.

After we arrived at the Era Agency, Kwak Hanmuk patted my shoulder and said, "Get your training done well. If anyone gives you a hard time, report them."

"Yes, sir. I'm off."

Seizing the opportunity, I performed the salute I'd hurriedly learned yesterday.

The moment he saw my salute, Kwak Hanmuk burst out laughing like a bag of chips popping.

I blinked, wondering if it was still clumsy, but he just chuckled and returned the salute.

Then he patted my back hard, saying I could be a Captain now instead of a recruit.

*'Is that a compliment?'*

My whole body felt like it was shaking from Kwak Hanmuk's large hand.

After the brief commotion, I parted ways with Kwak Hanmuk and headed to the recruit training area.

The Pudding Slime above my head asked, genuinely curious, "Roasted Chestnut. How can a human train a god?"

To him, this must have seemed like some bizarre behavior. I explained it from an alien's perspective.

"I'm pretending to be human to gather necessary information. It's needed for managing the planet. As you know, Dominic, I haven't been born for long, so there's a lot I don't know."

I went into a long explanation, arguing that I needed to understand humans to rule over them.

The Pudding Slime still seemed to not understand, but it didn't say anything more.

In the midst of this, the System Window suddenly appeared.

I've been thinking about this too, and I'm trying to figure out how to enter the Trials while completing the recruit training.

*'But they haven't mentioned registering lately?'*

Since it had appeared anyway, I asked what I was curious about, but it remained silent again.

Anyway, it only appeared and disappeared when it needed something. Now that I was used to it, I didn't say much either.

I also had a hunch.

I had consumed both Infernum and ■■■.

As the two mixed, the digestion speed had definitely become faster than before, and just handling this alone was overwhelming for me now.

Moreover, he seemed to think that there was no need to register right away, as my power of god would increase significantly once the assimilation was complete.

Even now, I was struggling to handle the Young god. I thought that after the assimilation, it might become truly unbearable.

I had to find a solution before then, no matter what.

Lost in thought about what the anchor might be, I arrived at the training location.

"..."

The moment I opened the door, I was met with a barrage of stares.

Inside the classroom-like space, about a dozen or so recruits sat in rigid, formal postures.

I had arrived ten minutes early, yet everyone was already there.

The gazes fixed on me held a mixture of emotions, from curiosity to displeasure.

It seemed... few, if any, were looking at me with any kindness.

Yesterday, Mo Haein gave me a brief rundown of the rumors circulating about me within the Era Agency.

The higher-ups, who knew all too well what the Trials were, regarded me as a monster. Given the sheer amount of trouble I'd caused, it was only natural.

The lower-ranking agents, however, who lacked such knowledge and experience, were far less impressed. They were resentful of a recruit receiving special treatment.

The general consensus among them was that my ability to close the Trials was entirely thanks to the Special Team. After all, the team was composed of Je Hyun-oh, the Captains, and Samra—how could a mere recruit be solely responsible for such a feat?

My decision to join the Era Agency as a recruit seemed only to lend more weight to their theory.

If I had come in as a Captain, they would have just torn me apart for that instead, so I hadn't given it much thought.

*'I would have stood out no matter what.'*

What mattered to me was the Special Team. My goal was to be a "rough stone" that wouldn't become too much of a stumbling block to the team's name.

I was about to take the empty seat in the very back row when Won Taebeom entered the classroom, holding a tablet.

Our eyes met immediately.

*'Is this the moment?'*

I snapped a salute, and he stared at me for a moment before acknowledging it with a nod.

I still hadn't apologized to Won Taebeom after the closure of The Cell X. Samra had offered to erase Won Taebeom's memories if I wanted, but I'd refused, feeling that it wasn't right. I'd been looking for a chance to talk to him ever since, but it hadn't been easy.

To begin with, a recruit couldn't just approach a Lieutenant—a vastly superior officer—and ask to speak with him.

Nor would it have been right to ask the Captains to arrange a meeting.

As I was pondering this, Won Taebeom happened to be assigned to our recruit training.

This is the perfect chance, I'll find an opportunity to speak with him after today's session.

*'I'm coming to get on my knees soon.'*

With that thought, I fixed my gaze on Won Taebeom, trying to look as innocent as possible.

For some reason, he couldn't meet my eyes for long.

He lowered his gaze slightly.

Then, after checking his watch, he began the class the moment it struck the hour.

"Today, we will be conducting a practical training session."

Won Taebeom opened the metal case resting on the podium.

Inside the case lay a Meteorite of Trial. It was a low-grade one, specifically designed for recruit training.

The meteorite's vibrant, multicolored glow cast a tense atmosphere over the recruits.

Just as I was thinking its cute appearance reminded me of a child's toy, Won Taebeom announced the Trial's name.

"The Trial we will be entering is &lt;Hop Drop Pop>, a Two-Star grade."

*Hop Drop Pop*—the name filled me with delight. It was a game I had thoroughly enjoyed playing.

*Hopdpop* was a massive multiplayer game with a casual vibe. Players started simultaneously and competed in a series of mini-games, a survival-style challenge involving navigating obstacles and engaging in lighthearted duels.

The fact that players could both cooperate and hinder each other made it fun, but there was an even bigger advantage.

"There's no death penalty."

In Hopdpop, if a player falls off the stage, they lose a heart. After losing all six hearts, they switch to Spectator Mode.

If even one person among the participants completes the course, the Spectators also get to Clear, allowing everyone to escape the Trial.

It was one of the safest Trials. Even if all the recruits became Spectators, the Trial would still be cleared as long as the Instructor finished the course. This made it an ideal Trial for training purposes.

However, the real reason I loved Hopdpop was something else entirely.

The main reason was that you could customize your character in so many different ways.

I loved how you could choose various options at the start of the game to personalize your character. I wondered if this customization would apply to the Pudding Slime too.

"Evaluation scores will be assigned based on your finishing rank," Won Taebeom added in a stiff tone after explaining the Trial. "Recruits are expected to cooperate with one another to complete the course."

*'Cooperate...'*

Judging by the atmosphere, it didn't look like many people would be willing to cooperate with me.

Well, it didn't matter.

I was confident I could reach the finish line even if every single recruit here ganged up to stop me.

However, since this was a training simulation by the Era Agency, I had to be careful not to achieve a True Ending and get the simulation shut down.

*'Let's just aim for the top five.'*

I set a modest goal in my mind and steeled my resolve.

Won Taebeom, having finished explaining the final precautions, announced the start of the Trial.

"Entering now."

As he placed his hand on the meteorite, the world was flooded with fluorescent light.
