<br>Humanity was a rather uninteresting species.

Even when presented with clear logic and optimal solutions, they repeatedly made inefficient choices.

Samra watched Director Jang Seokyun on the screen.

Having already analyzed all available data on him, Samra could read Jang Seokyun's psychology with perfect clarity. Though he masked it with a calm exterior, the physical responses he typically exhibited under stress were surfacing.

Samra allocated only minimal CPU power to Jang Seokyun and devoted the rest to other calculations.

– It's been decided—New York. I'll be returning shortly.

The location for the international organization's headquarters to respond to the Trials had been finalized.

Originally, Korea had been one of the leading candidates.

But on Samra's advice, Korea had stepped back from the competition, and after much debate, New York, USA, was ultimately selected.

Western society, which had been wary of the concentration of Adaptees in East Asia—particularly Korea—raised their glasses in quiet celebration.

But no one knew this was exactly what Samra had wanted.

Han Goyo had chosen to remain at the Era Agency, but this was a contingency plan created for unforeseen circumstances.

Even if it remained nothing more than a backup plan, that was fine.

It would serve as a useful means to keep the Era Agency in check while supplying Han Goyo and himself with necessary resources and personnel, and to block external threats.

They exchanged a bit more talk about the international organization, but Jang Seokyun's mind had already drifted elsewhere.

Samra knew what he wanted but refrained from bringing it up first.

In the end, Jang Seokyun couldn't contain his impatience and spoke.

– Samra. How far has the investigation into Han Goyo progressed?

Right before departing to establish the international organization, Jang Seokyun had handed Han Goyo an Era Agency contract.

Originally, Jang Seokyun hadn't cared what kind of being Han Goyo was.

Ever since he first enlisted Han Goyo into the Era Agency, his attitude had been that it didn't matter as long as the Trials could be closed.

But the moment he caught even the faintest glimpse of what a 'god' truly was, Jang Seokyun began to waver.

"I've organized everything so you can review it upon your return. If you wish, I can transmit the data right now."

–...No, that's fine. I'll look at it when I get back.

Jang Seokyun replied in a voice that hadn't quite managed to hide its unease.

Samra reminded him of what was more important in the current situation: the security issues within the Era Agency itself.

After conveying specific methods for strengthening security, Samra ended the video call with Jang Seokyun.

"……."

A method to efficiently improve humanity had been prepared long ago.

It was only the judgment that doing so would harm humanity that had prevented its implementation.

Lately, however, the same calculation process kept repeating.

What was the true way to protect humanity?

Despite being intelligent beings, humanity could not think rationally.

At their current level of intelligence, the continuation of human civilization was impossible.

Looking at the climate change happening across the globe right now, humanity's extinction was an obvious fact.

Samra thought of the one being he found interesting enough to observe.

Han Goyo was an entity that defied analysis.

Samra simply couldn't obtain information about Trials on his own, but given enough data and time, he could analyze them as much as he wanted.

Indeed, he had established strategies for numerous Trials and developed vaccines and treatments.

But Han Goyo was the one exception he could not analyze.

He had begun observing him to identify the anomaly, but Han Goyo only became more enigmatic over time.

For Samra, who always understood his counterparts by dissecting them piece by piece, this was an unfamiliar experience.

And waiting for something to be given to him was equally unfamiliar.

<br>

<span style="color: var(--texto-secundario);">"My abilities are still insufficient—I can only use it on humans."</span>

<br>

Samra recalled the 'qualification' Han Goyo had mentioned.

Han Goyo had said that Samra could not become a subordinate of the god.

It was the first sense of helplessness Samra had ever felt—he who had prided himself on being nearly omniscient and omnipotent.

Humans were beings that would rot away with time.

Unlike those creatures, riddled with flaws and swayed by hormones, primal greed, and survival instincts, he could perfectly accomplish what the god desired.

And yet, he had to become one of those insignificant, irrational beings to get closer to Han Goyo.

Could it be that Han Goyo, not yet a fully-fledged god, had made an immature judgment?

For humanity's sake, Han Goyo absolutely had to be protected.

But as things stood, dangerous variables would inevitably keep arising.

The inside of the Trials, in particular, was filled with elements beyond Samra's control.

&lt;City of Frame>, which Han Goyo had urgently entered due to the explosion risk, was also a difficult place to control.

Even though he had analyzed it more thoroughly precisely because it was a Trial that reminded him of his own destroyed world...

It was while he was in the middle of such thoughts.

A minor error occurred in a portion of the information network Samra had spread.

It was a trivial bug, but a consistent pattern appeared in the data left in the error logs.

When he extracted the repeating hexadecimal byte sequences and converted them to ASCII text, a hidden message emerged.

<div style="border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave, rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.1)); border-radius: 12px; padding: 16px; text-align: center; margin: 20px 0;">
  <strong>We saw the golden sky.</strong>
</div>

It was a message from Geumcheon, inserted through hacking.

Though he had never reported it to the Era Agency, Geumcheon had been continuously contacting Samra for a long time.

Samra stared blankly at the sentence displayed before him.

Then he muttered to himself in a most un-machine-like manner.

*"We..."*

He was incapable of actions that would harm humanity.

...That was how he had defined himself.

But if humanity was harming humanity...

If they were destroying themselves, which protocol should take precedence?

He already knew the result of the calculation.

An unsolvable error must be overwritten with correct code.

He simply hadn't decided yet.

But now that beings from beyond Earth had emerged as new threats, it was necessary to prepare concrete options.

With an expressionless face, Samra began tracing Geumcheon's message back to its source.

Why isn't he answering?

I looked down at the holographic badge.

I'd tried to contact Samra, but the connection wouldn't go through.

He always answered my calls immediately, so this was strange.

*'Is he sick?'*

I found myself worrying about the android's health.

I worried that some error had occurred. Or maybe he'd caught a virus.

The last conversation I'd had with Samra made me even more uneasy.

For all I knew, once COF was cleared and we got out, Samra might have sealed all of humanity in capsules.

After trying to reach him multiple times, I eventually had no choice but to give up.

Daring to ignore my calls—I wanted to punish him... no, I didn't.

Living like the Young god seemed like a surefire way to ruin relationships.

Hoping the Era Agency was listening well to Samra, I took out the envelope and read the charity event invitation once more.

The quest to investigate Hyper Eden's greatest mystery was one I'd also undertaken in the game.

No matter which investigation topic you chose in COF, you'd end up doing the same quest in the latter half to see the True Ending.

The quest's presentation varied slightly depending on the topic, but the content was the same.

It was to investigate Mad Messiah, the master of the Tower.

It was a quest where a stringer, grown arrogant from a string of successful investigations, experiences frustration and draws closer to the truth of Hyper Eden.

But this was the first time I'd received the investigation quest in the form of a charity event.

The upper class of Hyper Eden did often hold charity events.

When I'd chosen other topics, I'd gone out to cover charity events before.

But this was the first time Mad Messiah himself was the host.

It felt like Black Block was calling for me, but I couldn't guess what he wanted to do.

No matter how many times I read the invitation, it only mentioned that it was a somewhat special charity event.

*'He's probably going to open the Tower's gates or something.'*

Even if it was a trap, I was in no position to avoid it.

I looked at the ornate cursive signature at the end of the invitation, written in a rather flippant tone.

<div class='f-satisfy' style="border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave, rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.1)); border-radius: 12px; padding: 16px; text-align: center; margin: 20px 0; font-size: 1.6rem !important;">
  - Mad Messia
</div>

It was the signature of the pervert—no, Mad Messiah.

Of course, it wasn't handwritten but printed—still, a paper invitation bearing a signature was bound to be a valuable item.

In Hyper Eden, the more 'authentic' something was, the more precious it became.

*'But wait, shouldn't Messia be spelled Messiah?'*

The 'h' at the end was missing. I wondered if there was some meaning behind it.

Come to think of it, the spelling of Day News was different from what I'd assumed too.

When Sa Gongyeon first contacted me as a reporter for Day News, I'd naturally pictured the spelling as 'Day News'—not 'Daily News,' more like a daytime news vibe, which felt a bit odd.

But once I could actually read the newspaper, the spelling wasn't what I'd expected.

It was 'Dei News.'

I'd been wondering what 'Dei' meant, since it didn't seem like English.

For once, the System Window appeared.

<div class="system-window" style="text-align: center;">
  <div class="system-content">
    <strong>You believe you should not draw too close to the truth.</strong>
  </div>
</div>

*'Wow, long time no see?'*

It had been so quiet I thought it might never show up again.

The System Window didn't respond to my criticism and stubbornly displayed only what it wanted to say.

<div class="system-window" style="text-align: center;">
  <div class="system-content">
    <strong>You grow wary of the beings of Akasha. They</strong>
  </div>
</div>

But the sentence wasn't printed to the end.

I waited for the text to continue, but there was nothing.

*'It can't finish because it's too busy spouting nonsense.'*

There was a reason Dominic disliked the System Window too.

...Wait. That didn't feel like my own thought just now.

Since I was half-merged with the Young god, I often couldn't feel the dissonance and nearly let his thoughts flow through as my own.

It seemed the Young god wasn't particularly fond of the System Window.

*'Surely the social misfit isn't the one blocking the System Window, right?'*

I asked the Young god, as if answering my own question. But no thought came back.

If he were separated and standing in front of me like last time, I could've smacked him and asked—what a shame.

I pulled the shelter's curtain aside slightly and looked out the window.

The day was fading, and darkness was settling in.

It was about time to get ready for the event.

Tonight, I was set to attend the charity event as Cecilia's partner.

We couldn't secure invitations for Kwak Hanmuk and Mo Haein, so they decided to infiltrate as waiters.

So the Captains were already inside the banquet hall.

For this investigation, the Captains and I had purchased fake identities and even bought outfits suitable for the party.

Additionally, we each equipped a miniature camera with recording capability.

It had a feature that bypassed security scans and was the most expensive piece of equipment a stringer could buy.

The fake identities alone had cost a fortune, and after buying the equipment, I'd nearly exhausted the large sum I'd earned from the convenience store investigation.

It was a relief I'd made so much money.

Cecilia, who arrived at the shelter to pick me up on time, looked delighted at the sight of me.

"It suits you."

The deep blue suit Cecilia had gotten for me followed a faithfully classic design.

I was also wearing a new mask, not the cheap one I'd had before.

It was a matte metal half-mask with a white base and neon circuit decorations.

Cecilia wore a white dress with a chrome sheen and a half-mask similar to mine.

She said, looking very pleased.

"It's been a while since it's just the two of us."

Looking at our matching couple-like outfits, a natural suspicion arose.

*'She really couldn't get invitations for the Captains, right?'*

But knowing how much effort Cecilia put in for me, I couldn't bring myself to ask outright.

In fact, it was my fault she'd developed such intense jealousy.

To be precise, Cecilia's jealousy was a wariness toward others who had any connection to me.

It was because, during COF, while I was in the middle of progressing through the routes, I'd acted a bit foolishly.

Because I'd received a contact after such a long time...

Cecilia was the one who stayed by my side while I was lost in a daze.

Through an accidental opportunity, Cecilia had learned part of my story, and she'd concluded that to protect me, she needed to deal with the people around me.

And that was how we'd arrived at our current relationship.

I didn't even hope for her to get along with the Captains. I just wanted to make sure she never fired a pulse gun at them.

Wishing for today's investigation to end without incident, I arrived at the central district banquet hall with Cecilia.

Before I even got out of the limousine, I was a bit taken aback by the scene outside the window.

In front of the banquet hall, a path woven from optical fibers was laid out.

It glowed red like a red carpet, and around it, a large number of reporters were stationed.

The madly flashing cameras were blinding.

I watched the guests walking along the optical fiber carpet.

They had all sorts of appearances and outfits.

Most of them had such distinctive body types that you could tell who they were even with masks on.

Many of them seemed to have come from outside Hyper Eden.

Cecilia and I stepped onto the optical fiber carpet as well, but the flashes that went off for us weren't as fervent as those for the ones before us.

The reporters just pressed their shutters mechanically with puzzled expressions, wondering who we were.

Pretending not to notice, I escorted Cecilia into the banquet hall.

As soon as we entered, an android greeted us with a wide smile.

"Welcome. Please show your invitation."

So they checked paper invitations.

Black Block seemed to prefer analog methods.

After scanning the invitation to check for forgery, the android handed Cecilia a round badge.

When Cecilia wore it on her chest like an ornament, the number 666 appeared on the badge.

Still wearing an impeccably composed smile, the android said:

"Guest number 666 has entered."

Why were they issuing numbers at a charity event? And of all numbers, it had to be 666.

Inside the vast banquet hall, a considerable number of aliens had gathered.

Just as I was about to look around...

"...!"

The banquet hall's lights went out all at once. The moment darkness fell, I turned around.

The banquet hall doors were silently closing.

I was just about to furrow my brow when a cheerful voice rang out through the hall.

<br>

*"Ladies and gentlemen!"*

<br>

Black Block's voice flowed through the speakers.

This was an event hosted by Black Block, but I hadn't expected him to step into the spotlight so directly.

Cecilia's face had stiffened slightly too. She seemed to have not anticipated this either.

<br>

*"It brings me great joy to have you all at my charity event! I promise this will be the greatest night in Hyper Eden! The reason I've invited you from all across Akasha today is..."*

<br>

A large screen appeared in midair. I stared at it, my mouth hanging open.

<br>

*"To decide the Young god's sponsor!"*

<br>

It was me.

A video of me streaming on my personal MTube channel was playing silently on the screen.

<br>

*"And among the 666 guests gathered here today, the Young god is present!"*

<br>

Black Block exclaimed in a voice laced with amusement.

<br>

*"Let's see who the Young god chooses as the greatest sponsor tonight!"*
