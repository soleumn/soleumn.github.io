<br>I knew the Trial Response Agency was under the Ministry of National Defense.

I also knew that while it operated on a headquarters-and-team system, its essence was still the military.

But all I knew about the military was what I'd seen in the media and games.

Even as I addressed them as "Lieutenant Colonel" and "Captain," I hadn't truly grasped the weight of those titles.

Lately, I'd been aware that I wasn't acting like a proper recruit.

But I never expected to end up in a situation like this.

My heart sank.

I froze, unable to even blink, before my lips finally moved.

"I'm sorry..."

It didn't seem like the right thing to say in this situation.

But it was all I could manage.

As I stared at the floor, a sharp command dropped from above my head.

"Get down."

I quickly dropped to the floor in a push-up position.

Staring at the stone floor of the Medical Officer's Quarters, cold sweat trickled down my spine. I couldn't see the other man's expression, but the suffocating atmosphere was palpable, making me even more tense.

Kwak Hanmuk crouched down in front of me and reached out.

The image of Won Taebeom getting punched by him played back in my mind with vivid clarity.

*'Am I about to get hit too?' I wondered. But instead of a blow, he grabbed my head.*

I felt his hand rummaging through my hair.

Kwak Hanmuk was checking the wound on my head.

*'Lieutenant Won... You really reported everything...'*

In truth, it was only natural that he had reported it like that.

After hesitating, I tentatively spoke up.

"Um, the wound has completely healed."

But he didn't respond. Only after he had personally confirmed with his own eyes that the wound was gone did he finally remove his hand from my head.

"You're always getting your head cracked open, aren't you?" Kwak Hanmuk muttered lowly as he looked at me. Our eyes met by chance, and I hurriedly looked down again.

Still sitting in front of me, he asked in a voice devoid of any trace of a smile, "Trainee Han, do you think your superiors are a joke?"

"No, sir."

"Why? Is that not what's happening right now? Just follow my orders without question?"

"No, sir..."

"But why do you keep lying? Saying you'll handle it all on your own?"

"I'm sorry."

"Trainee Han, is 'I'm sorry' and 'No, sir' all you know how to say?"

"..."

I was about to say 'I'm sorry' again but stopped.

*'Should I say, 'I will correct my behavior' or something like that?'*

No matter how much I thought about it, I couldn't come up with a good answer.

It felt like soloing a Six-Star Trial would be much easier than this.

Then, a petty thought crossed my mind.

*'Should I check his Status Window?'*

If I knew Kwak Hanmuk's current status, I might get a better idea of what was going on.

But I only thought it; I didn't act on it.

Actually, it wasn't just the Status Window.

Even if I wasn't an NPC, the Medical Officer role allowed me to check Suspicion and Affection Levels.

Just as I had checked Dominic's stats first.

From the very beginning, I'd been tempted to check Je Hyun-oh's and Kwak Hanmuk's stats too.

But I knew I couldn't do that.

Checking their current status through the Status Window occasionally was already creepy enough.

I had at least enough self-awareness to know that going any further would make me a criminal.

...Or maybe I already was one.

I tried to think of a plausible answer, but my mind was a complete blank.

Sweat beaded on my forehead. This time, it wasn't cold sweat—it was from the sheer effort.

As I floundered, Kwak Hanmuk spoke again.

"Answer me properly. Why are you lying?"

He looked like he wouldn't let me off the hook until I gave him a straight answer.

My arms trembled violently as I racked my brain for an answer.

*'Why did I keep lying?'*

The answer was simple: I didn't think my risky plan would get approved.

But saying that wouldn't work on Kwak Hanmuk. He'd just scold me even more for making a decision alone without consulting anyone.

The strain of the disciplinary posture made it hard to think straight, but I kept trying to come up with a more plausible excuse.

Long ago, when faced with a difficult problem, I think I used to be honest and ask for help. At first, everyone would worry and try to work together to solve it.

But as with most problems, they were rarely resolved quickly. New problems would just keep piling on.

As the situation repeated, everyone showed signs of weariness and fatigue.

*'The atmosphere just turned sour.'*

In that case, it was much better to lie and handle it alone. I didn't want to make the people around me anxious.

I always solved things on my own, and as long as the results were good, others were fine with it. The majority of them implicitly accepted this peace.

But now... everyone except me was criticizing my actions.

As I lay face down, Kwak Hanmuk's words fell upon the back of my head again.

"Acting like this means you can't trust your superiors, right?"

Chewing on Kwak Hanmuk's words, I finally realized a plausible answer.

Feeling the sweat trickle down my temple, I opened my mouth.

"It's not that..."

I hesitated, finding it somehow difficult to speak, before continuing.

"I just didn't want anything to happen to the team..."

If I had to keep facing Trials, I wanted to stay with the Special Team.

That was why I wanted to do better. I thought everyone would be happy with the outcome in the end.

Kwak Hanmuk fell silent after hearing my answer.

Then, he slowly spoke, his voice cold.

"And what about you?"

I wondered what kind of expression he wore as he asked me that. But I couldn't bring myself to look up, so I just stared at the floor and answered.

"I just... thought I'd be fine. I figured it would work out somehow."

This part was true.

I had carefully considered my actions before doing this. If it had been a truly life-threatening matter, I would have chosen a different path.

*'My arm hurts.'*

I forced my collapsing body upright and glanced at Kwak Hanmuk.

He seemed lost in thought. He showed no sign of telling me to get up.

I slowly shifted my gaze to check on Je Hyun-oh.

"...!"

And then, our eyes met squarely.

Even through the goggles, I could feel his unwavering stare. He must have been watching me the whole time.

As if he were observing me.

A strange, unfamiliar feeling washed over me.

Because I was the one who had always been the observer.

I had always wanted to know more about Je Hyun-oh.

I was curious about the face beneath the gas mask, his past, and everything else I didn't know about him.

That's why I'd always watched him with such curiosity.

But now, it felt like Je Hyun-oh was the one observing me.

As I glanced at him, my arm buckled, and I nearly collapsed.

I quickly regained my composure as if nothing had happened, but Kwak Hanmuk caught it immediately.

He stared at me with a mixture of disbelief and bewilderment, his eyes saying, 'Is this really what it takes to break you?'

Though I was exhausted, I felt I could still hold on, and the look stung. But I kept my mouth shut.

"Get up."

Of course, I wasn't so indignant that I'd refuse an order to stand. I scrambled to my feet.

Kwak Hanmuk looked down at me silently. With my face flushed red from being face-down, I could only roll my eyes.

After staring for a long moment, Kwak Hanmuk finally turned to Je Hyun-oh.

"Lieutenant Colonel Je."

With that, Je Hyun-oh, who had been keeping his distance and merely watching, stepped forward and stood before me.

As Je Hyun-oh and Kwak Hanmuk flanked me, I felt as if a wall had been erected. I looked up at them with eyes filled with the respect and deference due to my superiors.

Je Hyun-oh, gazing down at me, whispered as if to himself, "Here we go again..."

The moment I heard those words, the memory of him throwing me against a tree during SL2PL <small>(&lt;Slay to Plate&gt;)</small> flashed vividly through my mind.

I watched him with anxious eyes, but to make matters worse, Je Hyun-oh began to fidget with his metallic hand, the clicking sound only heightening my unease.

Then, he said something completely unexpected.

"You shouldn't do anything dangerous, Goyo."

His rough, whispered words seemed to carry a gentle concern at first glance.

But I knew better.

The meaning behind Je Hyun-oh's words was unmistakably clear.

I had promised to enter Zero with him.

It meant it would be troublesome if I died before then.

Now, Je Hyun-oh had to keep me alive at all costs.

Kwak Hanmuk, unaware of this hidden circumstance, stared at Je Hyun-oh with a bewildered look. 

His gaze was filled with a desperate need to confirm if this was the same Je Hyun-oh he knew.

Je Hyun-oh extended his hand. A table sat nearby.

The fingertips of his metallic hand touched the sculpture of Mr. Octopus Leg with the lightest of pressures.

I couldn't hide my wide-eyed shock.

"Uh, wait, Lieutenant Colonel Je, just a moment—!"

I stammered, but Je Hyun-oh crushed down with his hand.

The wooden sculpture split silently under the black metal.

I screamed inwardly as the sculpture cracked in half.

I trembled with the urge to grab Je Hyun-oh's metallic hand.

Je Hyun-oh stared straight at me, moving his hand with agonizing slowness. Finally, the vertically split statue toppled sideways with a dull thud.

I couldn't bear to look at the horrific sight and closed my eyes.

*Klang, klang.* The cool sound of metal echoed, followed by a gentle question.

"Why? Can't you watch?"

I forced my eyelids open, only to witness an even more gruesome scene.

The metallic hand that had split the statue was now playfully tapping the severed wood. With each gesture, the wood splintered and crumbled.

*'Mr. Octopus Leg... I'm so sorry...'*

As I offered a silent apology for the blasphemous act, I heard Kwak Hanmuk sigh.

"What the hell are we going to do about that?"

While Kwak Hanmuk sighed repeatedly, Je Hyun-oh hacked at the statue until it was unrecognizable.

Only then did he speak again.

"Captain Kwak."

"Yes, sir."

"From now on, Trainee Han Goyo is forbidden from acting alone. If this happens again, treat it as insubordination."

"Yes, sir."

Kwak Hanmuk replied instantly, as if he'd been waiting for the order.

I felt relieved that the situation had finally settled, but I also realized that if this happened just one more time, I might have a truly *incredible* experience.

Something far worse than mere punishment drills.

After swearing I would never act alone again, I cautiously spoke up.

"Um, but..."

It was an important matter, so I figured I might as well bring it up while we were all gathered.

"About the Warden... no, I mean CEO Cha."

The moment I mentioned CEO Cha, the atmosphere between Je Hyun-oh and Kwak Hanmuk shifted instantly.

Though we had entered the game together, it was clear they now viewed CEO Cha as little more than an obstacle.

And in truth, that was exactly how things were playing out.

"He was supposed to support me... but it seems he's changed his mind. We need to consider the possibility that he won't follow the plan."

I recalled the words CEO Cha had said to me.

<span style="color: var(--texto-secundario);">"I don't need the True Ending."</span>

He was more than capable of clearing the game with any other ending.

Assuming CEO Cha was acting on his own, I mentally reviewed the Normal Endings the Warden could see.

*'If it's CEO Cha, he'd definitely choose the most efficient and fastest ending—'*

"Wait a second."

Kwak Hanmuk suddenly spoke up, narrowing his eyes.

"A prison guard just died."

"...!"

The prison guard's ability was Colleague Connection.

It allowed him to track the number of prison guards within the facility. As the ability evolved, he could sense his colleagues' level of injury or their stage of Neo Poisoning, and even summon them.

At Kwak Hanmuk's current level, he could perceive the real-time status of the prison guards.

And yet, one had died.

Just as his mind went blank from the sudden, inexplicable turn of events, a System Window appeared.
