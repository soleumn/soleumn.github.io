<br>As soon as I read the words, I reacted a little strangely.

*Why would he think that?*

That he doesn't want to be left alone. When I'm clearly right here.

But I was a generous master.

Therefore, I decided to overlook a minor mistake made by my subordinate.

Kwak Hanmuk, who had returned to the Rallica, was looking down at the ground.

I didn't like the sight of him staring at the floor, but I waited with a broad heart and scanned the hostages again.

I looked thoroughly, just in case, but the man and woman in blue uniforms didn't seem to be from the Special Team.

No matter how pixelated the graphics were, the vibe they gave off was completely different.

I still couldn't figure out who the boy was, either.

Other than his hair being black, there was almost no information to be gleaned from the pixelated graphics.

While I was watching the boy, the opposing player approached Kwak Hanmuk.

He said something to him.

<br>

  ■■■ asks Kwak Hanmuk if he's going to play a match he's bound to lose anyway.<br><br>

He demands that if Kwak Hanmuk admits defeat right now, he will try to negotiate with the Mafia.
</div>

<br>

It was a laughable proposition.

A speech bubble filled with curses appeared above Kwak Hanmuk's head.

The opposing player kicked Kwak Hanmuk's rally car and walked away.

Speech bubbles with "HAHAHA" appeared above the Mafia's heads.

I didn't know the game's story, but one thing was clear.

Just like with Mo Haein, no one expected Kwak Hanmuk to win.

In fact, it seemed they didn't even want him to.

The difference in the opposing player's car and outfit was so stark that it wasn't a fair fight to begin with.

Kwak Hanmuk slowly turned his head to look at the boy.

The Mafia then tapped the boy's head with their gun muzzles, flaunting their cruelty.

It was clear that continuing to pay attention would only lead to worse bullying.

Forcing himself to look away, Kwak Hanmuk stared up at the sky.

<br>

<div class='f-vt323' style="background-color: var(--bg-card) !important; border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave); font-size: 1.5rem !important; padding: 10px !important; margin: 0 !important;">
  Kwak Hanmuk knew this was all a lie. Yet he still desperately wanted to win.
</div>

<br>

*'I can grant him that much,'* I thought. *'I will not betray my subordinate's faith.'*

Kwak Hanmuk climbed into the rally car.

Immediately, the vehicles he and I had chosen filled the screen.

The hot hatch's angular white body featured long blue and red stripes running along its side.

I read the car's name, displayed in a bold font at the bottom.

Lancia Delta HF Integrale.

The long name made it sound impressive.

The racing games I'd played focused on the latest models, so I wasn't familiar with classic rally cars like this.

Kwak Hanmuk seemed to know a lot about cars, so there must have been a reason why he chose this one.

Before starting the race, I could make some simple adjustments to the settings. The default was set to 3 laps per race. Since my goal was a quick clear, I changed it to 1 lap. After switching the gear to manual, I pressed the brake and accelerator pedals to get a feel for them.

Once I finished preparing, I started the engine. The moment it roared to life, the car's body shuddered violently. The seat I was in also vibrated, realistically conveying the tremor. A low, rumbling engine sound echoed from the speakers mounted behind the backrest. It truly felt like I was sitting inside a real rally car, driving it.

Two rally cars took their places on the starting line. Standing side by side, the difference was even more apparent. In terms of performance, my car was clearly at a disadvantage.

Still, I had no intention of losing.

A large white number appeared in the center of the screen and began the countdown.

3, 2, 1, and...

<br>

<div class='f-vt323' style="background-color: var(--bg-card) !important; border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave); text-align: center; font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.5rem !important; padding: 10px !important; margin: 0 !important;">
  GO!
</div>

<br>

I floored the accelerator.

The jolt of the car lunging forward shot straight through my seat. I could feel the wheels tearing into the dirt and the impact of gravel spraying in all directions, battering the undercarriage. The speedometer's numbers soared dangerously high, yet my opponent had already pulled ahead.

Even as the gap widened, I felt no panic. On the straightaway, I was bound to fall behind due to the difference in our cars' performance.

The off-road terrain was desolate. The only building in sight was a dilapidated, abandoned gas station in the distance. We flew past it in an instant—two rusted pumps, a shattered neon sign, and windows boarded up with plywood—leaving only the vast, empty wilderness ahead.

Only two cars kicked up dust as they sped across the landscape.

Even though this was a world of pixel graphics, for a moment, I felt a strange sensation.

<br>

<div class='f-vt323' style="background-color: var(--bg-card) !important; border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave); font-size: 1.5rem !important; padding: 10px !important; margin: 0 !important;">
  Kwak Hanmuk thought the view before him was magnificent. He wondered if god was watching this too.
</div>

<br>

The thought of my subordinate brought a smile to my lips.

I wanted to tell him that I was watching with him, but I had to take a corner soon.

An arrow indicating the first turn appeared at the top center of the screen.

It was a gentle right-hand curve.

I tapped the brakes briefly, and as the weight shifted forward, I downshifted.

As I turned the wheel, the rear of the car slid slightly outward, drifting toward the edge of the corner.

I immediately counter-steered. The nose of the car was already pointed toward the exit.

The moment I cleared the corner, I floored the accelerator.

The car, which had been drifting sideways, straightened its posture and surged down the straightaway.

<br>

<div class='f-vt323' style="background-color: var(--bg-card) !important; border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave); font-size: 1.5rem !important; padding: 10px !important; margin: 0 !important;">
  Kwak Hanmuk liked the god's touch.
</div>

<br>

I smiled faintly.

I now understood the characteristics of the Rallica I was driving. The key was not to force the sliding car to a stop, but to let it keep running.

Once I got a feel for it, I began the chase.

Whenever the opponent pulled away on the straights, I repeatedly closed the gap in the corners. As I clung tenaciously to his rear, the opponent's movements grew increasingly desperate.

Unlike his anxious rival, Kwak Hanmuk pursued him with calm precision.

The scenery changed.

They left the wilderness and entered a valley flanked by sheer cliffs. The valley, a barren expanse of stone without a single tree, offered no margin for error—one wrong move could send them plunging off a precipice.

Given the terrain, it was bound to be full of sharp corners.

*'I'll settle this here.'*

He planned to overtake on this corner and secure the win for the first race.

<br>

<div class='f-vt323' style="background-color: var(--bg-card) !important; border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave); font-size: 1.5rem !important; padding: 10px !important; margin: 0 !important;">
  Kwak Hanmuk wanted to pass his opponent here. He was hoping the god would grant his wish.
</div>

<br>

My subordinate was thinking the exact same thing I was.

I was just starting to find that amusing when—

"...!"

I frowned.

The opposing car suddenly slowed down, closing the gap to a dangerously short distance. Then, it deliberately twisted its tires right in front of me.

A thick cloud of dust erupted instantly, blanketing my entire screen.

As I drove blind, a left-turn arrow flashed on the display.

It was a hairpin turn—a sharp, nearly ninety-degree corner.

Taking a hairpin turn without any visibility was madness.

His plan was clear. He wanted to scare me into slowing down so he could pull far ahead.

But I had no intention of playing along.

Our cars' performance gap was already significant. If I slowed down now, overtaking would be impossible. I'd have to concede the first race.

*'Absolutely not.'*

<br>

<div class='f-vt323' style="background-color: var(--bg-card) !important; border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave); font-size: 1.5rem !important; padding: 10px !important; margin: 0 !important;">
  Kwak Hanmuk refused to slow down. In fact, he saw this as his chance.
</div>

<br>

My subordinate shared his resolve.

I watched the opponent's car vanish into the massive cloud of dust.

Through the haze, their brake lights flashed once.

They seemed to be taking the corner wide, planning to exit on the outside.

I didn't hesitate. I moved, using the last glimpse of their taillights as my guide.

I tapped the brakes lightly, shifting the weight to the front wheels.

I swayed the car to the right to shift its weight, then immediately yanked the steering wheel to the left.

At the same time, I shifted gears. The gear indicator on the screen dropped to 2.

*K-boom!*

The engine roared, and the car on the screen spat out backfire.

The vehicle slid sideways, plunging into a cloud of dust.

At first, nothing was visible, but soon the opponent's car appeared. It was taking the outer course, pulling away smoothly.

Its movements were relaxed, as if certain that Kwak Hanmuk was still struggling behind.

But then, Kwak Hanmuk's car suddenly burst from the dust.

As it sliced into the inner course, five exclamation marks popped up over the opponent's vehicle.

The gap between the other car's side and Kwak Hanmuk's front bumper was barely a fist's width.

<br>

<div class='f-vt323' style="background-color: var(--bg-card) !important; border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave); font-size: 1.5rem !important; padding: 10px !important; margin: 0 !important;">
  ■■■ spat out a curse, screaming for him to go die alone.
</div>

<br>

Speech bubbles filled with profanity erupted frantically over the other car.

At the perilous angle just before impact, I floored the accelerator.

The skidding chassis dug into the gap on the inside of the corner, altering its trajectory.

The slightest mistake would have sent both Kwak Hanmuk and the other driver plummeting off the cliff to their deaths.

But I couldn't bear to see a single scratch on my subordinate.

*'I will make this work.'*

At the precise moment the skid began to subside, I unwound the steering wheel.

The instant the chassis straightened, I slammed the accelerator to the floor, unleashing an explosive burst of speed.

The rally car shot forward.

Kwak Hanmuk overtook the opposing car.

He had completely dominated the acceleration out of the exit.

The top of the screen functioned like a rearview mirror, showing what was behind. In it, the opponent's car was visible, small and staggering as it desperately tried to counter-steer.

A thrilling pleasure surged through the hand gripping the steering wheel.

<br>

<div class='f-vt323' style="background-color: var(--bg-card) !important; border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave); font-size: 1.5rem !important; padding: 10px !important; margin: 0 !important;">
  Kwak Hanmuk felt the joy of a successful overtake. He placed absolute trust in the divine hand that moved him.
</div>

<br>

Seeing my subordinate so satisfied brought me joy as well.

Though he had passed the opponent, the gap wasn't very large.

But this was enough.

With every winding corner, he continued to pull further ahead.

By the time they left the valley and returned to the open plains, the opposing car was nowhere in sight.

Kwak Hanmuk and I raced across the wilderness once again.

I found I quite liked the feeling of driving through a world with no one else in it.

It wouldn't be bad to live in a place like this with only my subordinates.

Before I knew it, the finish line marker appeared at the top of the screen.

There was no need to look back.

Kicking up a long trail of dust, the Rallica crossed the finish line.

The entire screen flashed.

<br>

<div class='f-vt323' style="background-color: var(--bg-card) !important; border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave); text-align: center; font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.5rem !important; padding: 10px !important; margin: 0 !important;">
  FINISH!
</div>

<br>

The engine sound cut off abruptly. The ranking appeared in the center of the screen.

<br>

<div class='f-vt323' style="background-color: var(--bg-card) !important; border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave); text-align: center; font-weight: bold; font-size: 1.5rem !important; padding: 10px !important; margin: 0 !important;">
  1ST<br>

COURSE RECORD!!
</div>

<br>

Below the letters announcing the new course record, the lap time floated up.

Since I'd broken the record, it seemed I'd played quite well.

I let go of the steering wheel.

"Whew..."

Only now, as the tension eased, did I realize my heart was pounding.

In place of the engine roar that had been deafening just moments ago, only a low vibration emanated from the backrest.

Inside the screen, chaos had erupted.

As Kwak Hanmuk crossed the finish line in first place, the Mafia members all erupted in a flurry of question and exclamation marks above their heads.

<br>

<div class='f-vt323' style="background-color: var(--bg-card) !important; border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave); font-size: 1.5rem !important; padding: 10px !important; margin: 0 !important;">
  They couldn't believe his victory, convinced he must have cheated. 
</div>

<br>

They even swarmed his Rallica, inspecting the car.

Kwak Hanmuk stepped out of the driver's seat, shrugged, and left them to it, as if to say, *'Do whatever you want.'*

Only after some time had passed did the opposing driver finally cross the finish line. The stark difference in their times made the Mafia's mood even more ominous.

The second race began.

Now more accustomed to the Rallica, I broke the course record again. The opposing driver crossed the finish line much later, the gap in our times even wider than before.

Though one race remained before reaching three wins, it seemed I would clear the game without any trouble at this rate.

I was waiting with Kwak Hanmuk for the third match to begin.

Suddenly, a speech bubble filled with curses appeared over one of the Mafia's heads, and he pulled out a gun.

*Bang!*

The opposing player screamed, clutching his arm. Pixelated blood sprayed into the air.

That was the signal. The other Mafia members drew their guns in unison.

<br>

<div class='f-vt323' style="background-color: var(--bg-card) !important; border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave); font-size: 1.5rem !important; padding: 10px !important; margin: 0 !important;">
  They had realized that the match was pointless now. They were breaking the rules, intending to kill everyone.
</div>

<br>

*Why aren't they following the rules?*

The game wasn't over yet. And yet, they were recklessly destroying all the rules.

What's more, they dared to touch my subordinate.

My face hardened.

*'I don't have to hold back this time...'*

I reached toward the screen. Then, I reached inside.

The Mafia, who were about to open fire, screamed at the sight of the hand appearing in the sky.

The arrogant thugs scattered and fled like ants under a torrential downpour.

*'It would have been easier if they'd stayed together.'*

Though it was a nuisance, I crushed them one by one with my finger.

The sensation of insects being crushed wasn't exactly pleasant.

Several bloodstains appeared on the screen.

In the process of killing the Mafia, I also crushed a few Rallicas.

I quickly checked to see if I had damaged any cars my subordinate might like, but fortunately, that wasn't the case.

I moved my hand to crush the last remaining Mafia member.

Seeing the hand descend, the Mafia member trembled violently and fell to his knees.

He collapsed to the ground, screaming, while speech bubbles continuously popped up above him.

The speech bubbles were calling out to god. I crushed him along with the bubbles.

After dealing with all the Mafia, I checked on the opposing player.

He looked too injured to drive, but the game wasn't over yet.

Just as I was about to nudge him with my finger toward the Rallica, Kwak Hanmuk's character rushed over and blocked my hand.

I wasn't trying to kill the other player...

It seemed my subordinate had misunderstood.

A speech bubble with a chestnut-shaped pixel graphic appeared above Kwak Hanmuk's head.

<br>

<div class='f-vt323' style="background-color: var(--bg-card) !important; border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave); font-size: 1.5rem !important; padding: 10px !important; margin: 0 !important;">
  Kwak Hanmuk had witnessed the god's touch. He had gained a deeper understanding of what kind of being his god truly was.
</div>

<br>

I felt a faint sensation.

Kwak Hanmuk's hand was touching the tip of my finger.

It was a completely different feeling from when I had crushed the bugs earlier.

I liked the soft, warm heat.

But I remained completely still, my hands resting idly, terrified that I might accidentally harm my subordinate.

<br>

<div class='f-vt323' style="background-color: var(--bg-card) !important; border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave); font-size: 1.5rem !important; padding: 10px !important; margin: 0 !important;">
  Kwak Hanmuk accepted his god. He also understood how he should raise the young god.
</div>

<br>

My subordinate is raising me?

And here I am, the one taking care of them.

It was a bit strange, but I figured this was just another form of loyalty toward one's master.

Afterward, he grasped my finger with both arms and gently pulled. I followed him where he led.

Kwak Hanmuk stopped in front of the hostages—a man and a woman—and looked up at me.

<br>

<div class='f-vt323' style="background-color: var(--bg-card) !important; border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave); font-size: 1.5rem !important; padding: 10px !important; margin: 0 !important;">
  He is introducing his former team to his god.
</div>

<br>

I blinked.

*'So they were Captain Kwak's former teammates.'*

Now I finally understood why they were wearing Era Agency uniforms.

Then what about the little boy?

I let my finger hang limply. He'd said he would explain everything if I waited, so I decided not to rush him.

Kwak Hanmuk untied all the hostages and scooped the young boy into his arms.

The boy was so small I wondered if he'd even started elementary school yet, and he remained perfectly still.

He just held his breath, cradled in Kwak Hanmuk's arms.

Kwak Hanmuk's gaze flickered between the child and me. For some reason, it felt like he was comparing us.

Just as I was about to get annoyed by the way he hugged the boy so tightly.

<br>

<div class='f-vt323' style="background-color: var(--bg-card) !important; border: 1px solid var(--borda-suave); font-size: 1.5rem !important; padding: 10px !important; margin: 0 !important;">
  Kwak Hanmuk thinks the Young god is the most important being to him right now.
</div>

<br>

"..."

My heart softened a little.

A speech bubble with 'HAHAHA' floated above Kwak Hanmuk's head.

Then, it slowly faded away.

An ellipsis bubble—'...'—took its place.

Kwak Hanmuk gently set the boy down on the floor.

When the boy hurriedly grabbed Kwak Hanmuk's sleeve, he stroked the child's head soothingly.

Then, he turned to his teammates and touched his wrist—the same wrist that used to hold his prayer beads.
