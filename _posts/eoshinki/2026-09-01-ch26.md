<br> *'Destroyed...?'*

For a moment, I forgot even the pain as I stared at Dominic.

He slowly released his grip on my heart.

My body trembled as I felt his hand slip out. When the last fingertip finally withdrew, I couldn't hold it back and retched.

Dominic grabbed my forearm to keep me from falling.

I had eaten nothing, so nothing came up.

I wiped my wet lips with the back of my hand.

*'To reach inside a living person's body...'*

It was something that went far beyond anything I could comprehend.

The sensation of him burrowing in was still vivid, yet the fact that not a single scratch remained on my chest made it all the more horrific.

Still, I couldn't bring myself to protest to Dominic.

Because he looked even angrier than I felt, despite being the one who had suffered.

Dominic seemed genuinely furious that I had been hurt and scarred.

I had also seen my own reflection—a shattered porcelain vase glued back together—and found myself speechless.

Besides, hearing the word *annihilation* had honestly terrified me.

After a moment's hesitation, I asked him, "What exactly do you mean?"

"Do you remember the day we first met?"

Of course I did. It was an unforgettable memory.

Back then, Dominic had looked into my eyes.



<span style="color: var(--texto-secundario);">"Little Lamb?"</span>

<span style="color: var(--texto-secundario);">"With all due respect, that name doesn't suit you."</span>



Then, he had pierced through my disguise and spoken my real name.

I remembered vividly how I had desperately tried to pass him off as a human.

"Roasted Chestnut was already in this state back then."

"I was... already broken like this?"

"Yes. There were many wounds, not just on the outside but on the inside as well. It only got worse after that."

He explained it briefly, but I immediately understood the cause.

It was because, in the ending of *CallOVo*, he had killed me and brought me back to life over and over again.

But after that, I had made a contract with Dominic, and Dominic wanted to help me in many ways as his contractor.

*'So that's why he fed me Infernum.'*

He had tried to heal me with Infernum's flames.

Thanks to that, my wounds were gradually healing, but it seemed that using Resurrection had completely messed me up.

"Punishment wouldn't have even scratched Roasted Chestnut, and it's a skill you would have found useful in the future. The same goes for Blind Faith and Summon."

Seeing how Dominic had summoned me here, it certainly seemed convenient.

Punishment was a cool skill, too.

Honestly, having at least one attack skill would be helpful for survival.

As for Blind Faith...

*'Lieutenant Won, I'm sorry.'*

I'd probably have to go out there and bow my head in apology the moment I stepped outside.

Though I'd likely be dragged away in a straitjacket before that could happen.

"To think you chose Resurrection over such trivial abilities."

Dominic seemed very displeased that I had erased my other skills to create Resurrection.

But I had no choice.

At that moment, Resurrection was what I wanted most.

I wasn't entirely sure, but it seemed I had to erase another ability to gain Resurrection. I wasn't yet powerful enough to hold multiple abilities at once.

With nothing to say to Dominic, I remained silent. He rose to his feet.

"This will happen again."

Dominic looked up at the sky.

The world shifted.

"...!"

I was in space.

Countless stars were embedded in a darkness so profound it made the night sky seem pale. A massive band of the Milky Way spanned the void, while the stars shone in a kaleidoscope of colors and sizes.

In the distance, nebulae and star clusters glowed with a mystical light.

As I gazed at their wondrous beauty, I spotted a pale blue star.

It was Earth.

Dominic reached out his hand to me. When I took it, a new sight unfolded.

My eyes widened.

Meteorites appeared around Earth.

As if summoned from somewhere, dozens, then hundreds of meteorites materialized in sequence and began falling toward the planet.

The Meteorites of Trial were descending.

The endless rain of stars left me breathless.

"This is the near future," Dominic said. "I've accelerated time slightly for Roasted Chestnut's benefit... but the flow of events will be similar."

Countless Meteorites of Trial already existed on Earth.

The vast majority of the meteorites had not yet hatched, yet we were struggling to deal with the Trial—a mere fraction of the threat.

And now, so many more are falling?

If they hatch immediately upon impact, like The Cell X...

The words* Earth's Destruction* were the only thing I could picture in my mind.

"The Outer gods will descend upon Roasted Chestnut's planet. Some may be even stronger than the one you've already faced."

Dominic asked me, "Can you protect it? With a mere ability like Resurrection?"

I couldn't answer.

*I have to protect them.*

*My subordinates... and my domain.*

As I stared blankly at the falling meteorites, the scene before me shifted.

Before I knew it, I had returned to the Black Sea.

"Roasted Chestnut."

I stared blankly up at Dominic, the sound of rough waves crashing around us.

"If you wish to protect Chestnut Planet without even facing punishment, the time has come to choose."

He placed the marble in my hand once more.

"Whether you will grow and move forward, or stop and fade away. I leave the choice to you, Roasted Chestnut."

Dominic said no more.

He fell silent, as if vowing not to interfere any further.

I stared at the candy-shaped marble resting in my palm.

"..."

I had a strong premonition that eating this would definitely change something.

Though it was hardly a premonition at all.

*'It would be stranger if nothing happened.'*

What would the System Window have said?

Judging by its silence, it seemed to have run out of power again. Or perhaps it couldn't interfere because this was Dominic's domain. This was a moment when I had to make a choice entirely on my own.

Suddenly, I remembered the System Window that had appeared when I closed the CallOVo and came out.

  <div class="system-content" style="color: var(--texto-secundario) !important;">
    <strong>You are the Young god of the planet 'Earth'.</strong>

Back then, I found the title "Young god" absurd. I dismissed it as some kind of Second Job Advancement and firmly defined myself as a human. Besides, being in charge of Earth? For someone like me, an unemployed person who struggled to manage a single-room apartment, it sounded incredibly vague.

But now, I know.

I know that I have become a being who has transcended humanity.

The memory of speaking and acting as a Young god was so chilling that I couldn't believe it every time I looked back. I could also vaguely sense that Earth was my domain.

Why me, of all people?

I was most curious about the reason the Creator had chosen me. The Epic Quests and the System Window that kept appearing were also a mystery.

The System Window was manipulating me, dangling a perfect body as the final reward for the Epic Quest while threatening me with the loss of my limbs and eyes if I failed.

I couldn't bring myself to believe the System Window was the same entity as the Creator.

Lost in a tangle of thoughts, I finally spoke.

"If I eat this... will I change a lot?"

I wanted to live. I wanted to eat the Orb just to avoid being annihilated, but something still weighed on my mind.

I didn't know what kind of being I would become.

I didn't want to think or act like a young god.

If I ate this, I would surely... become a little more like a young god.

My hand trembled briefly. The thoughts I had suppressed came rushing back in an instant.

I barely managed to push them away again.

Dominic didn't answer right away.

His steady gaze felt as if he were trying to understand me.

It must have been a frustrating situation for Dominic. After all, I was choosing the difficult path when the easy one was so obvious.

But I didn't want to become a god with a caste-based mindset like Dominic. Every time I vividly recalled the moment I had recklessly invaded someone else's mind, the sensation of trampling Number 1 underfoot... it felt so... disgusting.

If I had to become a god, I wanted to be a god for those I cared about.

Dominic finally spoke after a long silence.

"There are things that won't change, no matter how hard you try."

It was an ambiguous answer, but it seemed like the best one he could give right now.

What I had to protect wasn't just my subordinates and my domain.

It included myself.

I looked at the Orb again and let out a short laugh.

*'A choice?'*

Wasn't there only one path from the beginning?

Earth would shatter, and my life would be forfeit.

But since there was a chance I wouldn't be corrupted, the gamble was worth taking.

I could do this.

"Mr. Dominic."

"Yes, Roasted Chestnut."

"I want to ask you one last thing. Will you answer me honestly?"

"Go ahead."

I asked the question I had been hesitating to ask.

"I want to know why you are so good to me."

"You are my contractor, Roasted Chestnut, and you are still young."

"Is that all?"

Dominic smiled without a word.

As if he knew everything I feared and dreaded.

He whispered back, "Is that not enough?"

"..."

It seemed he wasn't ready to tell me yet.

When would I ever be able to understand him?

I got the feeling it wasn't going to get any easier.

I brought the orb to my lips. But I couldn't bring myself to eat it, so I just held it against my closed mouth for a moment.

It looked exactly like a candy, but it felt damp against my skin.

I didn't want to eat it anymore...

I let out a deep sigh inwardly.

"Roasted Chestnut."

His voice was gentle, yet the command was firm.

Under his blue gaze, I parted my lips. Then, I pushed the orb into my mouth.

The round shape touched my tongue, and soon it began to melt, thick and viscous.

Even so, it tasted sweet, and I found myself smiling a little.

It finally sank in—he had really made this just for me to eat.

The candy melted quickly.

As I swallowed the last bit of sweetness, Dominic praised me softly.

"You've worked hard," he said.

"Thank you," I replied, my voice a little awkward.

His blue eyes gleamed as he reached out to me. Sensing his intention, I turned my head slightly, avoiding his touch.

Dominic spoke to me in a gentle, soothing tone. "I promised it wouldn't hurt, didn't I? I'll have it finished before you even wake."

"No, it's fine. Just do it."

Dominic looked at me as if I were a stubborn child.

But I was sick of forgetting things.

He reached for me again. "Then I'll just cover your eyes."

The moment his fingertips touched the skin around my eyes, my vision went black. His low voice warned me, "This will be quite long and painful."

And then, I endured an eternity of torment.

<br>Thunder roared in the black sky.

A blinding flash tore through the night, painting the world in a vivid blue. Azure veins, tangled like a skein of thread, spiderwebbed across the heavens.

When the lightning struck the earth, a brilliant mist erupted, and shards of blue electricity scattered like flames in all directions.

Kwak Hanmuk slumped in the midst of the searing blue heat.

The warm blood streaming from his eyes and ears mixed with the pouring rain, staining him completely red.

In the past, and now, as he relived it in his dreams, he always had the same thought.

*'I want to die.'*

But even death was not granted to him.

Because he knew what would happen after he died.

There was nothing he could do.

He was simply crushed, helpless, by an overwhelming and unknown presence.

Even in his dream, Kwak Hanmuk smiled bitterly.

Though it was the same nightmare he'd endured countless times, the emotions it stirred—a crushing weight he could never grow accustomed to—still bore down on him.

But it would be over soon.

He waited, as he always did, to either wake from the dream or for it to repeat from the beginning.

Yet, strangely, the dream refused to end.

Kwak Hanmuk looked up at the sky.

It was as if someone had called his name.

Though the black sky was repeatedly scarred by nothing but brilliant blue lightning, he couldn't tear his gaze away.

And then, finally.

He saw a brilliant golden light.

A golden meteor shower, trailing long tails of fire, tore powerfully across the black sky and the blue lightning.

The moment he saw the falling meteor shower, he knew.

It was the star that had come for him.

"...Hah!"

Kwak Hanmuk's eyes snapped open as he bolted upright.

His entire body was drenched in a cold sweat. He covered his face with trembling hands.

It was the same dream he always had—the memory of being trapped in that fucking place.

But the dream and the memory had always ended at the same point...

For the first time, the dream had continued. A new scene had begun.

It was a memory that didn't belong to him.

He muttered under his breath, recalling the golden light etched into his mind.

"Fuck."

He wanted a cigarette. Just one.

He rubbed his face, trying to ignore the intense urge.

He looked around.

He was in a hospital room at the Era Agency. It seemed they had successfully Cleared The Cell X.

*'Goyo...'*

The last thing he remembered was being wrapped in the tentacles of that spawn. The moment he saw it, he knew instinctively that the creature was the same being that resided within him.

He had lost consciousness as his brain felt like it was being crushed, yet upon waking, his body felt perfectly fine. In fact, he felt better than he had in days. It didn't even feel like much time had passed since the Clear.

His first priority was to check if Han Goyo was alright.

Just as he threw off the covers and swung his legs off the hospital bed, a screen flickered to life in front of him.

— Captain Kwak Hanmuk.

The holographic man on the screen stared at Kwak Hanmuk with an expressionless face.

"Samra?"

Kwak knew Samra wasn't the type to offer a warm greeting just because he'd regained consciousness, so he found the sudden appearance strange.

— Report to the Research Analysis Headquarters immediately.

Kwak stared at him, wondering what was going on since he'd just woken up, but Samra spoke with a hint of urgency.

— Han Goyo has been pronounced dead.
