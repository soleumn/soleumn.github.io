<br>My mind went completely blank for a moment.

I immediately sprinted toward Zone X.

CEO Cha kept pace with me as I ran frantically, then suddenly grabbed me.

"I'm...!"

"Zone X," he said, tossing the words out before I could even finish my sentence. Then he took off at a blistering speed.

Once again, I was tucked under his arm, carried along as he ran.

As the Warden, CEO Cha had the authority to control every area of the prison. He didn't need to stop and swipe his ID card at every checkpoint. Every door swung open automatically before him, allowing him to move without a single interruption.

The scenery blurred past in the blink of an eye.

Thanks to him, we reached Zone X in an instant.

CEO Cha dropped me off in front of Room 6.

Two prison guards, panting and gripping their batons, turned to look at me. One of them was bleeding neon green liquid from a ragged gash on his arm.

"Fucking... Hey, Medical Officer! You're here. Do something about this bastard."

The guard snarled as he explained the situation. Number s6 had bitten his arm, and they had immediately fired the creature-killing gun. The moment the bullet struck, the creature had gone into Code Blue.

I practically rolled into Room 6. The guards often called a Code Blue without knowing better. I prayed this was one of those times as I checked Orca's condition.

No pulse. No breath.

"Get me the AED!"

Zone X was equipped with an AED because creatures often went into shock.

When I roared my command, the prison guards hesitated for a moment before bringing the red box. They only obeyed me because of my high Affection Level.

I opened the box and switched the device on, then attached the pads to Orca's chest. My hands were shaking violently, but I managed to place the pads without fumbling.

The creature-specific AED was set to a much higher electrical output than a human one. It took several seconds to charge, forcing me to wait as I continued chest compressions. Those few seconds felt like an eternity.

*"Charging complete. Press the button to deliver the shock."*

The moment the mechanical voice spoke, I slammed the button.

The moment the button was pressed, Orca's body jolted upward.

A powerful current surged through his entire body, piercing every nerve and muscle fiber. His broad chest muscles spasmed violently before his body collapsed back onto the floor with a dull thud. A faint, acrid smell of burning flesh wafted from the pads, accompanied by a wisp of steam.

The AED wasted no time, immediately issuing its next command.

*"Begin chest compressions."*

I interlaced my fingers over Orca's chest again. Just as I was about to put all my weight into pressing down on his thick sternum—

"...!"

Orca gasped sharply, his eyes snapping open.

"Orca!" I cried out urgently.

His gaze quickly regained focus.

And in that instant, I saw it.

The flicker of disappointment that crossed his eyes.

Orca couldn't bring himself to be happy about returning from the brink of death.

For him, death had always been a welcome prospect.

Orca called my name, his voice raspy.

"Mr. Chestnut."

He spoke to me with a faint smile.

"It would have been a disaster if you weren't here."

I didn't respond to his insincere words. Instead, I glared at him.

<span style="color: var(--texto-secundario);">"Do you still think this is a game?"</span>

I had never thought of it as a game.

In fact, precisely because it wasn't a game—because this was reality—I wanted to do my absolute best.

Even if it was difficult and painful...

No, I wanted to achieve a happy ending, no matter what it took.

*'Should I just modify him?'*

What if I completely rebuilt his mind and body, so he could never think of dying again?

Even a Creature's mind would be forced to shatter if it were flooded with the psychotropic drugs from the infirmary.

Since he was an orca beastman, there would be few side effects to grafting deep-sea creature organs onto his body.

If I wanted, I could even find something from a similar species to the orca and attach it to him.

*'Crush everything that was sick and broken, and remake it into something strong and new.'*

...The letters seemed to have grown larger. And there was some kind of spacing between them.

The System Window snapped me back to my senses, and I swallowed a sigh.

The brainwashing had made me have these strange thoughts, but a tiny sliver of sincerity had been mixed in.

Not that I absolutely wanted to modify him, but it was all because I wanted to save Orca somehow.

But I still didn't know how.

To create a happy ending for Orca, I had to let him swim freely in the vast ocean.

But his leg was beyond even Neo's healing.

I had tried everything in the game...

*'Wait.'*

There was still one method I hadn't tried.

The Octopus Leg.

He was the god of this place. He must contain energy far more powerful than Neo's.

Even a tiny fragment of it might be enough to restore Orca's leg.

*'After all, they are both sea creatures.'*

I couldn't guarantee the effect, but it seemed worth a try.

"Mr. Chestnut."

Lost in my new idea, I looked at Orca.

He smiled, revealing his sharp, pointed teeth.

"Don't be angry."

I didn't reply, instead scanning my surroundings. The prison guards who had called the Code Blue stood far off, watching as if it were someone else's problem. CEO Cha stood by the iron bars, his gaze fixed on me.

"I'm not angry," I replied quietly, retrieving a first-aid kit from my inventory.

"But your eyes say otherwise."

Donning surgical gloves, I inserted the tweezers—which I had scrubbed vigorously with an alcohol swab—into the wound. Orca groaned in pain, but I ignored him. Creatures were resilient to pain and healed quickly. Once the bullet was removed, the wound would close on its own.

The tip of the tweezers, probing between raw flesh and bone, met a hard, foreign resistance. I clamped down on it and pulled.

With a sickening scrape, the bullet emerged, saturated with blood.

*Clink.*

It fell onto the tray I'd placed on the floor, producing a cold, metallic sound.

I immediately poured hydrogen peroxide over the wound. White foam hissed and bubbled, covering the injury.

True to a Creature's regenerative abilities, the bleeding had already stopped.

I applied a gauze pad heavily coated in antibiotic ointment, then wrapped it with bandages before asking, "Why did you attack the prison guards?"

Orca looked up at me, a smirk pulling at the corners of his mouth.

"Mr. Chestnut, being locked in such a small space for so long... it can make one lose their mind."

"..."

"Before, I was too weak for my madness to show. But today, I suppose I just couldn't help myself."

Orca laughed as if I'd made a joke, but then doubled over with a groan as the pain from his gunshot wound flared.

Pretending to check his bandages, I leaned closer.

"The Creature Execution Ceremony is in two days."

Orca's eyes snapped open.

I kept my voice low and urgent. "The Security Chief is planning a coup that day. I've already lied to him, telling him I can control the creatures. I need you to pretend to attack the Warden."

Meanwhile, Number 1 was gorging itself on Neo in the basement.

The blisters hidden within its mass of vines could store liquid, and its maximum capacity far exceeded the size of a swimming pool.

The goal was simple: when all the prison guards gathered in one place, I would unleash a torrent of Neo and turn them all into Scrap.

However, the Security Chief wouldn't turn into Scrap, so he would have to be dealt with separately.

I promised Orca I would bring him a Scrap's heart so he could recover before the execution ceremony, then asked, "Can you handle the Security Chief? My colleagues will help you."

Orca was the only Creature capable of taking him on.

Upon hearing my question, Orca readily agreed. "Of course, Mr. Chestnut."

Seeing his sparkling eyes, a strange thought crossed my mind again. 'What if I can't fix his leg... what if I created a world filled only with things Orca loves?'

I shook my head inwardly.* 'Maybe it's the brainwashing,'* I thought, *'but I keep thinking things I never would have normally.'*

"And during tomorrow's rounds, I might say something strange. Just ignore me."

"...?"

I didn't go so far as to say I'd been brainwashed. I didn't want to worry him for no reason.

Orca seemed to have more questions, but I stood up anyway.

After I gathered the blood-stained gloves and gauze and threw them away, CEO Cha, who had been watching me all this time, silently walked away first.

I quietly followed behind him.

Only after we left Zone X did CEO Cha turn to look at me.

His posture seemed to be inviting me to explain.

CEO Cha and I were the only two survivors among the players who had played the Creator's Game.

He was the only one who had experienced the trials of reality through the game.

That was probably why he... had said such a thing to me.

I slowly opened my mouth.

"I've never thought of it as a game."

"Then why are you acting like that?"

CEO Cha looked down at me and asked again.

"Why do you act as if you can just restart by turning it off and on again?"

Was I just imagining things?

It felt as if he actually cared about me.

It was hard to believe because he was so vivid, just as he was in the game.

In the game, CEO Cha had killed me five times in a row right after I started playing. His reason? He didn't want to play with an annoying bastard. He had such a terrible personality that he only reluctantly took me along after confirming I was a regressor.

Even though I'd resolved not to judge him solely by the game, I'd spent so much time with him that changing my mind wasn't easy.

I slowly spoke.

"Because I think I'll regret it."

I could feel CEO Cha's gaze on me, even through the mask. I'd expected him to sneer and tell me to stop talking nonsense, but for some reason, he remained silent.

Honestly, if I had a better excuse, I would have said something else. But at this moment, those were the only words I could think of.

I was sick and tired of failure and running away.

If there was anything I could do, I wanted to try it. If I was going to regret it, I'd rather do it and then regret it.

I didn't care if I got hurt a little in the process.

I was already...

A faint pain throbbed in my left wrist.

The tingling sensation spread to my fingertips, so I slowly clenched and unclenched my fist.

A sharp voice cut through the air.

"So you're saying you're doing this because you don't want to regret trembling over the death of a mere NPC?"

I had shown too much intimacy with Orca in front of CEO Cha.

He wouldn't understand my actions.

But that didn't matter. I replied impassively.

"Yes."

CEO Cha wasn't someone I needed to make understand.

I liked him, but separately from that, I had no confidence in making him understand my actions.

It was a contradiction to think that just because I liked him, he had to understand me.

"I'll make sure the Clear and the dispatch contract go smoothly."

CEO Cha was an efficiency-driven person, even if I couldn't figure out why he was acting like this right now. In the end, as long as I fulfilled the contract well, nothing else would matter.

CEO Cha let out a sharp, short laugh.

"Han Goyo."

He leaned his face toward me. The words he spat out seemed to be ground between his teeth.

"I don't need a True Ending."

Then he vanished from my sight. He had raised his Cognitive Decline to 100%.

I let out a soft breath, not knowing if he was still right in front of me or if he had gone far away.

*'Does this mean he won't support me anymore?'*

It was a miracle I had even put up with his temper this much.

Actually, the operation was a coup intended to bring him down, so it was only natural for him to be displeased.

With a heavy heart, I finished my remaining duties as a Medical Officer and trudged back to my quarters.

I sent the message to Je Hyun-oh and opened the door to my quarters.

"..."

I froze, unable to send the next message, the door still wide open.

Je Hyun-oh, leaning against the wall, and Kwak Hanmuk, who had dragged a chair over and was sitting in it askew, both stared at me.

The Medical Officer's Quarters, not very large to begin with, felt cramped with two tall men occupying the space.

Kwak Hanmuk spoke, his expression unreadable.

"You're late, Goyo."

He called me by my name, not the "Night" series.

Won Taebeom's words flashed through my mind.

"I'll also share Trainee Han's current status with Captain Kwak."

I had agreed to it without a second thought, thinking I hadn't been brainwashed. I'd planned to avoid them both and only communicate through messages until tomorrow.

I never expected Kwak Hanmuk to bring Je Hyun-oh along to the Medical Officer's Quarters.

I swallowed hard.

*'I have to act completely normal.'*

I needed to show them that the brainwashing was no big deal.

Steeling my resolve, I approached my superiors.

"Lieutenant Colonel Je, Captain Kwak."

As I hesitated for a moment, unsure whether to salute, Kwak Hanmuk stared at me and asked, "Were you brainwashed?"

I stood straight and replied, "Yes, sir. The effects will wear off in two days. The brainwashing was weak, so there are no major issues."

"Is that so...?"

Kwak Hanmuk turned to Je Hyun-oh.

Je Hyun-oh's metallic hand tapped a steady, rhythmic beat against the table as he fixed his gaze on me.

I quickly added, "It's not spoiled."

But the metallic hand kept tapping. Kwak Hanmuk spoke up, his voice laced with boredom.

"Octopus looks good these days."

"Octopus is great. It's nutritious, too. Is it in season?"

*'Phew...'* I thought I was in the clear.

Just as I was inwardly apologizing to Mr. Octopus Leg, a 'tap' sound made me turn. Je Hyun-oh was poking the statue of Mr. Octopus Leg that I had placed on the table with his metallic hand.

Before leaving today, I had found the statue covered in dust in a corner of the Medical Officer's Quarters. I'd scrubbed off all the filthy grime before bringing it here.

I watched him, my heart pounding with anxiety, terrified he might crush it.

"Goyo."

He called my name, his voice languid. I answered, my heart in my throat.

"Yes...?"

"Do you like this kind of thing?"

I found myself completely speechless.

As the silence stretched on, Kwak Hanmuk rose from his chair.

"Hey, Lieutenant Colonel Je. Why are you intimidating him? He seems fine to me."

He strolled over to the table, looking down at the statue.

"Up close, it's actually quite impressive. Maybe I should get a 'blessing' from Lieutenant Won tomorrow?"

The moment he said that, a strange wave of aversion washed over me.

Without thinking, the words tumbled out of my mouth.

"Um, Captain Kwak! I know he's great and all, but I still think..."

*'Ah.'*

I clamped my mouth shut, realizing my mistake.

The lights on Je Hyun-oh's goggles flickered.
